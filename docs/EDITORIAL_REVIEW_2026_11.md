# Scholar's Edition — Full-Collection Editorial Review
Reviewed 10 November 2026 | 112 public encyclopedia articles

**Scope:** A catalog-wide first-pass audit of every published article's metadata, narrative length, headings, portrait assignment and reader-facing language, followed by close reading of selected illustrative articles (including Stanleyville, River City, East Port, Age of Ascendancy, Aethrion, Primordial Powers, Planes of Existence and Lathander and the Dawn). This is an *editorial review in progress*, not a verification of every sentence against original manuscripts, nor a claim that all 112 articles are finished.

## Overall assessment

The strongest accounts, including Lavitz Shawe, Renada, the Six and the Misfits, offer a coherent voice and genuine narrative depth. The principal weakness is inconsistency across the collection: some entries are essays, others terse registers; occasional paragraphs still discuss what an author must *not* claim instead of simply documenting history. The atlas and gallery navigation have been addressed separately.

### Quantitative scan (approximately, body text only)

- 112 published articles; **37** carry a front-matter image assignment and **75** carry none. The absence of artwork is not a missing historical fact.
- About **16 entries** contain fewer than 300 narrative words. Briefness is often appropriate for settlements with few known details, and should not automatically be cured by fictional filler.
- Representative range: **River City ~163 words**, **Renada ~2,870**, **Lavitz Shawe ~3,060** (the scan excludes front matter and uses a Unicode word-token approximation; earlier word-count tools may differ).
- All reviewed entries have named themes/headings; a short entry need not imitate a full biography's structure.
- A flagged word like `public`, `record`, `not established` or `invented` is a *manual review candidate*, not proof of bad prose. Some uses are ordinary historical scholarship or literally describe fictional events.

## Editorial priorities

### Priority A — Evidence and continuity (source review before publication)

These deserve paragraph-by-paragraph checking against the user's authorized historical materials before being strengthened or expanded. **Do not assume that a compelling custom or anecdote is an independently attested part of the world.**

- **Primordial Powers:** Check whether *the Stone and the Seed* lesson, Heureux's purported joke, and scholars' attributed interpretations come from confirmed source material or are editorial illustrative inventions. Keep the distinction clear if retained.
- **Lathander and the Dawn:** Verify the *First Loaf* observance and the degree to which the practice is established in the setting. Its moral character is plausible, but plausibility is not historical evidence.
- **Planes of Existence:** Verify the *Three Questions* at Shamman’s Tower, the *Atlas of Returns*, and the *Returning Thread* custom as recorded practices rather than unattributed embellishments.
- **Aethrion:** Recheck the scholarly interpretations, *Scribe's Question* and *Blank Folio* for provenance. Preserve the contested voices where documented; do not turn an unattributed moral illustration into a purported rediscovered historical artifact.
- **Chronological continuity:** Check which dates are exact, approximate or reign-based; retain distinction between the founding in 1 AF and Heroes Hill; keep Jaznaar's exact class unresolved unless the author settles it. Avoid giving publication dates the appearance of in-world AF dates.
- **Character images:** A filename is not sufficient evidence of identity; any new image-to-character associations must be drawn from the image field of the published biography or from reviewed gallery entries.

### Priority B — Stronger prose (safe structural editing)

- **Stanleyville:** Repeated the Hjoldan/pony naming story in adjacent paragraphs and retained author-facing "invented" and exhaustive negative claims. These were tightened in this pass while preserving the smithing economy, Jack's distinct cause of death, Stalker and the preceding Lord Marshal.
- **Cosmology:** Planes of Existence and Primordial Powers sometimes hedge in developer terminology. Favor one precise note about the limits of surviving testimony over multiple disclaimers.
- **Black Tower / Denzians Forest:** Replace "the public sources do not establish" with in-world observations.
- **Major character articles:** Audit long biographies (Lavitz, Heureux, Sven, Tel'Lora, the Six, the Misfits) for repeated conclusions and passages that substitute philosophical generalities for actual biography. Preserve established events and meaningful emotional detail.
- **History and legal institutions:** Use the Commission's narrative voice when discussing unrecorded charters, boundary surveys and testimony, without making up missing officials or law.

### Priority C — Differentiate record depth responsibly

**Brief places and settlements:** River City, East Port, Port Orange, Briyan's Autumn, Osmostown, Stannisport, Taveras, Tarnapolus, Jogar Village and Heroes Hill are examples of concise entries. Their short length is a valid editorial choice. Consider a concise “Brief Entry” indication for place entries and cross-link to geography, relevant history or current kingdom administration. Expand only with cited facts; never invent mayors, trade statistics, local festivals or dates to reach a target length.

**Character sketches:** Short biographies such as Jaaris, Noki, Jack and Malikeah can feel incomplete beside Lavitz. Where established source material supports more history, enrich with relationships, a turning point, and consequences. Otherwise preserve a compact record and let the gallery provide visual context.

**Images:** Do not attach arbitrary fantasy art to locations merely to even out page length. Commissioned or reviewed depictions are preferable, with specific alt text and truthful captions. Published character-article portraits are automatically eligible for the Gallery.

## Standards for future copy and new Gazette issues

1. Read the full entry and its named public source before editing substantive claims.
2. Lead with what *happened* or what witnesses *said*. Treat ignorance as an archival finding, not a warning about the author's creative limits.
3. Keep competing interpretations in a properly attributed Contested Account only when the disagreement is documented.
4. Do not equate article length, lack of art, or an imperfect surviving record with poor quality.
5. Check related articles for contradictions before adding chronology, office, identity, ancestry, or geography.
6. Avoid attributing verbatim quotations to characters or archival contributors without the text existing in a confirmed source.
7. Prioritize corrections that affect readers' understanding, then eliminate repetition, and only then consider stylistic polish.
