# The Scholar's Edition: In-World Editorial Voice

The public Archives of Avalor are written as historical scholarship produced by the Royal Commission of Historical Inquiry, not as developer commentary about a fantasy setting.

## Style rules
- Write assertively about evidence that actually survives, while reserving uncertainty for genuine gaps.
- Never show the reader production vocabulary such as `canon`, `author-approved`, `invented lore`, `Keeper material`, or editorial instructions about claims the author may not make.
- Prefer "No surviving charter names its founder," "The date is unrecorded," or simply omit a field, to "Not established in the reference canon."
- Keep uncertainty local to its relevant sentence. Do not repeat several paragraphs explaining what the article is *not* claiming.
- Distinguish testimony, rumor, interpretation, and documented history through the Commission's narrative voice. A royal archive can acknowledge conflicting accounts without inventing an entire dispute.
- Use the existing `contested` callout only when public source material actually provides multiple different views. Identify proponents or sources; never fabricate their statements or quotations.
- Use the optional "Brief Entry" marker for concise location accounts; brevity does not indicate incomplete or inferior writing.
- Leave the process guardrails in repository documentation, build validation, and editorial tooling, not in the player's article text.
- The setting's genuine unknowns remain unknown. Never manufacture dates, routes, relationships, species, images, historical border locations, or future-campaign revelations to remove uncertainty.

## Typical edits
| Avoid | In-world revision |
|---|---|
| "The reference canon does not establish a founding date." | "The Commission has found no surviving founding charter." |
| "Not established" in a facts box | "Unrecorded," or omit the field if it conveys nothing useful |
| "No additional invented crimes are needed." | Describe the specific crimes for which surviving testimony exists |
| "No invented historical borders are shown." | "The boundaries of earlier ages have not survived in the available surveys." |
| "The public record cannot confirm..." repeated throughout | Explain the one concrete evidentiary limit once |

A finished article should read like a coherent piece of historical writing. Missing evidence is material for scholarship, not an apology from the author.
