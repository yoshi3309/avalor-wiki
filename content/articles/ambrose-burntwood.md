## A quiet place in a busy harbor

At **Blackwater Bay**, the **Burntwood Tavern** is a familiar stop for sailors, traders, travelers, and adventurers who need shelter, conversation, or a decent place to sit after a difficult crossing. Its proprietor, **Ambrose Burntwood**, is a mountain dwarf whose friendliness has never prevented him from noticing exactly who comes through his door.

Ambrose remembers names and faces with uncommon precision. He speaks easily with harbor workers, captains, and merchants, and knows when a visitor needs questions answered and when that visitor would rather not be asked anything at all.

He is often seen carrying a **copper mule mug** to which he is sentimentally attached. The tavern's rooms and stores also contain an extensive collection of blankets, a detail that has become one of the more affectionate oddities in stories told about its owner.

## A life built with Aelianos

Ambrose shares his life with **Aelianos Burntwood**. Together their home is bound up with the ordinary work of a harbor tavern: keeping guests comfortable, dealing with changing company, and making sure the establishment remains a place people wish to return to.

His manner is patient and observant. Friends describe a man who prefers to settle trouble by talking, arranging a compromise, or helping someone take a safer road. He does not seek out quarrels for their own sake.

The instinct extends beyond paying customers. Individuals in difficulty have sometimes found help at the Burntwood Tavern without being pressed for immediate repayment. Ambrose seems constitutionally unable to leave a neighbor's hardship entirely alone.

## More capable than he appears

People who judge Ambrose by his comfortable domestic life risk underestimating him. He knows coastal routes, keeps friendships across ports and merchant circles, and has a talent for finding practical exits from complicated situations.

He prefers conversation to violence and planning to needless risks. His calm is not uncertainty, and his friendliness should not be mistaken for an inability to act decisively when people are endangered.

Much of what makes him effective is quiet work: remembering a useful name, arranging a passage, distracting an unfriendly observer, or helping someone reach safety before a confrontation becomes unavoidable.

## The call to service

Ambrose had good reason to be content with the life he had made. The Burntwood Tavern gave him a home, a livelihood, a beloved spouse, and the company of people who came and went with the tides.

Yet he was among the operatives recruited by **[[Lavitz Shawe]]** for the undertaking associated with **Blackwater Bay**. The Lord Marshal's choice suggests that a useful operative need not be the loudest warrior in a room.

Ambrose would rather see someone safely home than win a fight that might have been avoided. That preference does not mean he will stand aside when the people he cares for need him.
