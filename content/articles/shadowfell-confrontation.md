## A rescue delayed but not abandoned

The last confrontation of [[The Misfits]] did not begin at the gates of the Blood King's fortress. It began in [[Renada]], when [[Cid the Blood King]] abducted [[Queen Melmiri]] during the [[The Tournament of Champions|Tournament of Champions]].

The companions pursued the queen, only to have the road interrupted more than once. [[Ros Furband]] seized the company aboard his pirate galleon and delivered them toward Jinlin Vechon, a dragonborn vampire whose hatred of Cid did not make him an ally of the rescuers. The Misfits defeated Jinlin in a real battle. Then [[Zauks and the False Lives|Zauks]] imprisoned the companions in counterfeit adventures and false lives drawn from their own memories.

**Melmiri remained a captive through every interruption.** The trials did not take place before her abduction, nor were they further episodes after her rescue. They were the obstacles that stood between the fellowship and the queen.

When the Misfits escaped Zauks's illusions, they still had to reach the Blood King.

## Into the Shadowfell

Their pursuit finally led them into the **Shadowfell**, where Cid had long survived the ruin of the man once celebrated as [[Aldus Greystone III]]. The truth of his identity had reached the company through [[Jaznaar]], last survivor of the founding Six. By this point the struggle was no longer simply the rescue of a ruler. It was the culmination of a deception that had shaped almost two thousand years of Avalorian history.

Within Cid's fortress stood dangerous defenders, among them the necromancer **[[Jaaris]]**, whose crimes were associated with the earlier loss of [[fo-gurn|Fo'Gurn]] at the ruined temple near Stanleyville. Jaaris was **killed in the final struggle**. He was not the unnamed necromancer of the Misfits' first royal commission.

The testimony is certain of the defenders' defeat but incomplete on every movement through the fortress. The absence of a full route or sequence of blows should not be mistaken for an invitation to fill the gaps with a dramatic itinerary of the historian's invention.

## The friend who appeared again

The brass dragonborn **Fo'Gurn** had **died** defending the company against a hydra at the ruined temple near Stanleyville. Afterward **Jaaris resurrected him through necromancy and compelled him to fight the friends who mourned him**.

Within the Shadowfell fortress, **Lavitz followed Fo'Gurn into a separate chamber**. The paladins fought, but Lavitz understood that killing his friend would not release him from Jaaris's will. He **lowered his defenses** rather than deliver the fatal blow.

Fo'Gurn could have struck. **He chose to protect Lavitz**, breaking the conditioning through an act of his own. This happened **before** Jaaris was killed: the death of the necromancer did not manufacture Fo'Gurn's recovery of free will.

The two returned to the central battle in time to intercept Cid's attack on a wounded Samuel. For a moment the companions fought beside the man whom they had thought lost forever.

## Samuel and the emerald

At the height of Cid's threat, unnatural fire turned into harmless butterflies and the exhausted company found a brief reprieve. **Anna**, the noncombatant friend who had traveled with the Misfits, arrived beside a radiant warrior who appeared to be the ancient paladin **[[Lowkee]]**.

The figure was **[[Samuel the Devoted|Samuel]]**, a Monadic Deva who **genuinely but temporarily channeled Lowkee's spirit through the [[Emerald of Armanti]]**. He addressed Cid as **Aldus** and fought him away from the throne room while the others confronted Jaaris and the defenders. Cid wounded Samuel severely, ending the manifestation. **Lowkee was not permanently raised from the dead.** The precise cause of the transformed flames remains unknown.

The Emerald's intended purpose was **to bar Cid permanently from the Material Plane**. It was carried by Anna, later revealed to be **Erevan Ilesere in disguise**; its transfer to Fo'Gurn took place amid the battle without a recorded handoff.

**Cid's Bane**, the sword Jaznaar had entrusted to Lavitz, was a separate heirloom of Lowkee's. Neither relic should be mistaken for the other.

## Cid's attempted escape

The Misfits confronted Cid after Jaaris had fallen. With Samuel badly injured, Lavitz and Fo'Gurn reunited in the throne room and joined the fight that ended the Blood King's immediate power.

Cid sought escape through a dimensional doorway. **Fo'Gurn pressed the Emerald of Armanti into Cid's chest**, using the gem in accordance with its purpose of preventing the Blood King's return to the Material Plane.

Then immense **black hands** reached from the darkness and took Cid into the **Beyond**. The accounts differ on whether witnesses saw a single vast hand or several; they describe the same event. **No one has established who or what directed those hands**. The Emerald's sealing action and the hands' removal of Cid were distinct. Nothing in the original confrontation establishes who commanded those hands.

The victory left one final pain. Because Jaaris's death had begun the failure of the dark existence sustaining him, **Fo'Gurn could not accompany his friends home**. He remained in the Shadowfell to **rest one last time**, able at least to say goodbye to Lavitz before the survivors escaped. The words of that farewell are not preserved.

## The queen who helped her rescuers

**Melmiri did not merely wait to be carried home.** The queen helped the Misfits escape the failing fortress after the confrontation. Whatever the precise passage out of the Shadowfell, the testimony establishes that she acted in her own survival and that of the people who had come for her.

The returning company brought Melmiri back to Avalor. Its members had endured captivity, deception, separation and the reappearance of a friend long mourned. They had also secured the testimony needed to challenge the kingdom's deepest historical falsehood.

To remember only the defeat of Cid would be to overlook what the rescuers came to accomplish: **bring a living queen home**.

## A victory that changed the kingdom

The consequences unfolded in the Material Plane. [[King Gradvum Frozenroar]] publicly [[The Vindication of Jaznaar|vindicated Jaznaar Mizzrorzza]], rejecting the centuries-old accusation that the drow founder had betrayed the kingdom. The Misfits' victory helped transform the history of Aldus from a reassuring legend into an account of terrible choices and their consequences.

The five adventurers celebrated afterward did not remain a single traveling company forever. Lavitz entered royal service as Lord Marshal; Tel'Lora became High Wizard; Sven, Heureux and Amara followed distinct callings. Anna and Jack belonged to the history of that fellowship even though the songs rarely gave them equal space.

This final confrontation is therefore best read not as an additional chapter of **Cid's biography** alone, but as the end of the Misfits' long campaign—and the beginning of the [[The Age of Ascendancy|age]] that followed it.
