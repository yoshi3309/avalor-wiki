## The rescue interrupted

After the Tournament of Champions, the Misfits had a clear purpose: recover **Queen Melmiri**, whom Cid had taken from Renada. Their pursuit did not proceed directly to the Blood King's fortress.

A pirate captain named **[[Ros Furband]]**, working under a paid arrangement, captured the company. The rescuers became prisoners themselves, held aboard his galleon while the queen remained in Cid's power.

The encounter was a reminder of how many dangers could interrupt even an urgent royal mission. A person could command mighty spells or wield a legendary sword and still find that a hired captain with a ship controlled the next stage of the journey.

## Work aboard the galleon

The surviving accounts recall the practical indignities of captivity. **Lavitz and Heureux labored at the oars**; **Tel'Lora and Anna worked in the galley**; Sven was among those assigned lookout duties.

The admiral **[[Beldir Willowrun]]** was also present. His precise reason for being aboard has not been conclusively established. The Commission will not fill that gap by guessing that he was an accomplice, a willing passenger, or the author of some undisclosed naval mission.

These small details matter because they expose the distance between the image of celebrated heroes and the daily helplessness captivity could impose. The Misfits had promised to rescue a queen and were themselves made to perform labor at a stranger's command.

## The Wrath of Innocent

Ros's arrangement delivered the companions toward **Jinlin Vechon**, a dragonborn vampire and founder of the **[[Independent Factions of the Misfits Era#the-wrath-of-innocent|Wrath of Innocent]]**.

Jinlin hated the Blood King, but common enmity was not the same as common purpose. He had gathered followers around grievances and the pursuit of vengeance, and that cause could turn cruelly upon people who had not committed the crimes he sought to punish.

The circumstances of Jinlin's earliest life and the origins of his condition are not established in the Commission's public account. His hatred does not justify inventing those details. What can be judged is the treatment of others by the Wrath and the actual confrontation in which the Misfits opposed him.

## A real battle before a false ordeal

The companions **defeated Jinlin in the waking world**. This is one of the essential divisions in their campaign's chronology.

Later the mind flayer **Zauks** fashioned imitations of their previous adventures and offered the company false lives. The real defeat of Jinlin preceded that illusion. It was not an invented adventure within Zauks's trap, nor a second identical battle undertaken after the first had somehow been erased.

Melmiri remained a captive throughout both the pirate voyage and the conflict with the Wrath.

## Onward into another trap

When the Misfits came away from Jinlin's defeat, they had not yet recovered the queen. Their next ordeal would make the facts of their own lives uncertain as Zauks drew from memories of old dangers and losses.

The pirate voyage and the Wrath episode belong together because one interrupted pursuit led directly toward the other. They should be read as a single stretch of the failed straight road from Renada to the Shadowfell, rather than as isolated adventures detached from the queen's captivity.
