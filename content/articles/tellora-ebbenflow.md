## The wizard behind the eighth seat

**Tel'Lora Ebbenflow** serves Avalor as **High Wizard**, a position that places him at the heart of royal counsel without confining his life to the court. The half-elven scholar is also one of [[The Misfits]], whose discovery of the truth behind [[Cid the Blood King]] forced the kingdom to reconsider almost two thousand years of accepted history.

For the public, Tel'Lora may appear to belong to two different stories. One is the improbable tale of an adventuring fellowship, its losses, and its victory over a threat from the Shadowfell. The other is the quieter history of a royal adviser and teacher, concerned with the consequences of magic long after the battle has ended.

Those are not separate callings. A man who witnessed the destruction wrought by Aldus Greystone III's research has particular reason to believe that **powerful knowledge must be accompanied by careful judgment**.

## Before the names became famous

The surviving public accounts say little about Tel'Lora's childhood, early teachers, or the precise road that brought him to Avalor's service. They identify him as a **male half-elf wizard**, not as the heir of an established magical dynasty or the product of a particular school.

What the record establishes is that Tel'Lora joined the adventurers summoned by the preceding Lord Marshal to investigate an **unnamed necromancer**. The early company also included [[Lavitz Shawe]], [[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]], [[Amara]], and [[fo-gurn|Fo'Gurn]].

The investigation ended with the necromancer's defeat. Among the recovered materials were fragments of research later linked to the experiments of Aldus Greystone III. Neither the fragments nor their discovery proved that the necromancer had personally served Cid. They did, however, become part of the evidence the companions would eventually learn to understand.

A second commission concerning [[The Cult of Gol'Goroth|the cult of Gol'Goroth]] brought [[Heureux]] into their lives. In the company that followed, Tel'Lora's knowledge was not an academic curiosity: it was something his friends depended upon when strange evidence, enchantments, and unfamiliar dangers resisted simpler explanations.

## A scholar among companions

Tel'Lora's reputation is that of a man who values exact language, careful distinctions, and the difference between a plausible explanation and a demonstrated fact. The voice preserved in his scholarly comments on the Living Chronicle is frequently precise, sometimes dry, and particularly impatient with stories that sacrifice accuracy for an elegant conclusion.

There is affection behind that precision. Heureux delighted in teasing Tel'Lora about his attachment to scholarship, while the wizard's replies often returned the conversation to what could actually be proved. Their exchanges give the reader a sense of two companions who remembered the same dangers but approached the telling of them differently.

Tel'Lora was not, however, a detached observer traveling in the company of people who did the dangerous work. He faced the ordeals of [[Moonville Manor]], the extraordinary affairs of [[Noki]], and the dangers surrounding [[The Stalker of Stanleyville|Stanleyville's Stalker]] as one of the fellowship. Whatever he later wrote or taught was informed by experience alongside friends whose lives depended upon the outcome.

## Stanleyville and the limits of certainty

The deaths at [[Stanleyville]] demonstrated what can happen when magic is deliberately concealed from those expected to encounter it. A child named [[Jack of Stanleyville|Jack]] died after reading a spell hidden in manuscripts purchased by his family. A later inquiry identified the seller as Cid in disguise.

The companions befriended Jack's conscious ghost, who traveled with them rather than remaining in the town. The Stalker also killed the very Lord Marshal who had commissioned their early adventures.

These events left the company with grief and increasingly troubling evidence, but not yet the complete explanation of what they were facing. A careful account of Tel'Lora's work must preserve that distinction: the companions did not suddenly know the whole history of Cid because they had encountered one of his servants.

For a future High Wizard, Stanleyville was an especially stark illustration of why the handling of magical knowledge could never be separated from questions of responsibility.

## The last survivor of the Six

Among Tel'Lora's most consequential encounters was the meeting with [[Jaznaar]] at [[Jaznaar's Hidden Refuge|his hidden refuge]].

The Misfits had followed clues toward a passage associated with the tomb of [[Bradgem Glav]] at [[Heroes Hill]]. Heureux's account also mentions an object associated with Tel'Lora's family. The precise means of reaching the refuge have not been fully made public, and the Archives do not reconstruct them.

Jaznaar was the last survivor of [[The Six]], remembered for centuries as the Betrayer. He revealed that the healer Aldus Greystone III and the Blood King were the same being, and that he had spent generations resisting the danger his old companion left behind.

**Jaznaar instructed Tel'Lora** and entrusted knowledge to the company. The precise content of those lessons remains outside the public record. Tel'Lora's surviving reflections suggest both gratitude and the painful realization that there were questions he could no longer put to the man who had lived through the founding age.

Jaznaar died after the companions departed. The truth he had offered survived him.

## Memory, deception, and the Shadowfell

The Misfits' road continued through encounters in which appearances could not be taken at face value. The vampire [[Jinlin Vechon and the Wrath of Innocent|Jinlin Vechon]] hated Cid, but that shared hatred did not make him trustworthy. Later, the mind flayer [[Zauks and the False Lives|Zauks]] used the companions' own memories to fashion convincing imitations of past dangers and desired lives.

The precise hopes each companion saw in Zauks's illusions have not been publicly disclosed. It is enough to know that the fellowship rejected a comforting deception and returned to the real responsibilities of their lives.

At the [[The Tournament of Champions|Tournament of Champions]], the companions tried to protect the royal couple from Cid. The Blood King abducted [[Queen Melmiri]], and the Misfits followed him into the Shadowfell to recover her.

They succeeded, defeating Cid and helping reveal the foundational lie that had condemned Jaznaar. Tel'Lora's part in the victory did not make him an eyewitness to every event of the founding era. It made him one of the people who helped bring surviving testimony into the light.

## The eighth seat of the Aegis

Following the victory, King Gradvum appointed Tel'Lora **High Wizard**. The office itself had predecessors, but Tel'Lora's appointment changed the form of government: magical affairs received an **independent eighth seat on [[The Aegis]]**, whose earlier council had consisted of seven.

The change acknowledged that magic had become too important to be treated merely as an additional matter for some other minister. Questions of defense, public safety, education, extraordinary artifacts, and travel between planes could all require informed arcane counsel.

Expertise did not make Tel'Lora sovereign over every magic-user in Avalor. He became an adviser serving within a government of distinct offices, required to make his judgments understandable to those responsible for other concerns.

His closest working partner was [[Lavitz Shawe]], who became Lord Marshal within weeks of the Blood King's defeat. Their friendship had begun in danger, but their responsibilities now demanded decisions on behalf of people who would never know either man personally.

## Shamman’s Tower and the responsibility to teach

Tel'Lora also revived [[Shamman's Tower|Shamman’s Tower]], situated off Avalor's eastern coast, as a center of arcane study and instruction. The institution was not intended simply to assemble powerful practitioners under one roof.

The Tower's public scholarship stresses the testing of assumptions and the scrutiny of dangerous conclusions. Among its recorded practices is **Second Witness**, which calls for another qualified observer during hazardous investigations. The **Atlas of Returns** gathers accounts of planar journeys for comparison rather than treating each traveler's recollection as infallible.

These disciplines have special meaning in a kingdom whose most celebrated founding healer was eventually revealed to have committed terrible crimes in pursuit of immortality. The lesson is not that magical inquiry itself is suspect. It is that exceptional talent does not excuse secrecy, cruelty, or refusal to hear evidence of harm.

The [[The Great Library of Aldus Greystone|Great Library]] at Heaven's Heart preserves records and historical evidence. Shamman’s Tower trains scholars and practitioners to examine and test what such records describe. Their functions are complementary, not interchangeable.

## Lavitz, Sven, and an enduring fellowship

Across the following twenty years, Tel'Lora remained especially close to Lavitz and [[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]]. Lavitz's role became one of the kingdom's most visible offices. Sven's later activities were harder for public historians to trace and gave rise to the epithet **Iron Shadow**.

Popular storytellers sometimes portrayed the three as two royal officials and a third companion moving unseen. The Royal Archives do not certify the existence of any particular secret organization from rumor alone. Their continued friendship, however, is not in doubt.

[[Heureux]] continued to travel and teach, and [[Amara]] followed her own service to the natural world. The five did not reunite as a complete company during the succeeding twenty-year interval, but their differences of duty did not end their loyalty.

For Tel'Lora, the academy and council made it possible to serve those friends in a new way: to help build a kingdom where knowledge could outlast its holders and where a mistake of history need not be repeated simply because it had once been taught as truth.

## What the High Wizard preserves

The public record does not identify every teacher Tel'Lora studied under, each spell he mastered, or every private decision made during his travels. Such gaps should remain gaps unless better evidence or approved testimony emerges.

What can be known is substantial enough. He helped a fellowship survive deception and danger, learned from the last surviving member of the founding Six, took an unprecedented place in the Aegis, and gave new life to an academy.

It is fitting that the wizard who helped overturn Avalor's oldest false certainty should now teach others to ask what they know, how they know it, and what may happen if they are wrong.
