## An age named after the victory

The **Age of Ascendancy** is the name later historians gave the period following [[The Misfits]]' defeat of [[Cid the Blood King]] in **2000 AF**. The present royal chronology places Avalor approximately twenty years later, in **2020 AF**. The kingdom recovered [[Queen Melmiri]], learned that its celebrated founder Aldus Greystone III had been the Blood King, and began correcting the centuries-old accusation against [[Jaznaar]]. Avalor's growing reputation abroad belonged to that same period, but it should not be confused with one unbroken triumph.

No independent record establishes the age's formal creation by royal decree. Its name is a historical description of change across roughly twenty years, **not a claim that every year is fully documented**.

## The first obligations of peace

The crown faced responsibilities that battle alone could not settle. [[King Gradvum Frozenroar]] restored Jaznaar's name to the royal record and acknowledged that the public legend of Aldus had protected a terrible deception. The correction reached memorials, historical instruction, and the collections of institutions that had long repeated the older story.

The [[Royal Commission of Historical Inquiry]] later assembled testimony and competing sources to understand the founding age and the Misfits' campaign. Its work includes preserving evidence of the false account rather than pretending the falsehood had never been taught. In Renada, the statue of Aldus at Rynhold Park remained, but the inscription was corrected and its earlier plaque preserved at the Great Library.

The kingdom's dead could not share these honors. [[kel-maris|Kel'Maris]], the Lion's Shadow who first commissioned the companions, had been killed at Stanleyville. Captain Funkin brought her body home before dying himself in the later Hydra disaster. Inspector Sihnion and other guards were also lost there. [[fo-gurn|Fo'Gurn]] sacrificed his life to save the fellowship, and [[jaznaar|Jaznaar]] died before hearing his vindication. Their absences are part of the history of victory.

## A kingdom's widening horizons

Gradvum's court extended Avalor's diplomatic connections, while ports and established trading routes benefited from renewed commerce. That growth did not reach every community equally or resolve the older disputes among regions and settlements. Prosperity brought fresh questions about who benefited from the kingdom's standing and whose needs remained distant from the capital.

The age also saw institutions adapting to knowledge that earlier generations had mishandled. **[[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]] became High Wizard** and received a new, independent eighth seat on [[The Aegis]]. He revived [[Shamman’s Tower]] as a place of arcane learning and inquiry, while the Great Library of Aldus Greystone confronted the reputation of its namesake. Their tasks were not identical: one taught and investigated magic, while the other preserved and re-examined historical evidence.

**[[Heureux]]** served at court for a time and later founded [[Heureux’s Bard Academy]] in [[Taveras]]. His work as a bard and witness connected the arts to the question of how histories should be told. The academy was a place for learning performance and storytelling, not a second government archive.

## The fellowship beyond the road

The surviving companions did not spend the following twenty years as an unbroken adventuring party. **[[Lavitz Shawe]]** accepted the office of Lord Marshal, vacant since Kel'Maris's death. Tel'Lora took up his work as High Wizard. Heureux taught and continued to travel. [[Amara]] served as steward of the royal library for a time before following her calling beyond the capital. [[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]] received a promise of royal assistance rather than a permanent court appointment and continued work beyond ordinary public view.

They remained bound by friendship even as their duties diverged. Their remembered fellowship also included Anna, the apparent noncombatant later revealed as Erevan Ilesere; Jack, the ghost boy who traveled with them; and Fo'Gurn, who belonged to the original company. Historical fame did not make them interchangeable, nor did it erase those who did not return.

## The years not yet written

The name **Age of Ascendancy** embraces twenty years of change without claiming that a single chronicle can recover every life within them. Trade, diplomacy, scholarship and royal service are remembered in public accounts. In other parts of the kingdom, households and communities lived through years for which the Commission possesses few firsthand records. Their histories may yet enlarge this account without altering the events already known.

The enduring question of the age is whether a kingdom capable of correcting its old history can also govern more wisely because of what it learned.
