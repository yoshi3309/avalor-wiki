## A village among uncertain woods

**Tumbledown** is a small gnome village tucked among woods described in its people's accounts as lying at the edge of the **[[The Planes of Existence#the-feywild-and-the-forest-at-home|Feywild]]**. The surrounding forest holds strange creatures and tracks not easily understood by those who encounter them. Its mysteries have long demanded both curiosity and caution from those who live there.

Tumbledown's exact position relative to Avalor's surveyed lands is not recorded. The village's association with the Feywild should not be mistaken for proof that it lies within Avalor's separate Forest of the Fey, nor does it establish a particular planar crossing.

The community's history is preserved most clearly in the life of **[[ferris-rumbuckle|Ferris Rumbuckle]]**, a gnome whose childhood was shaped by people who worked with their hands and others who traveled the dangerous woodland paths beyond the village.

## Leatherworkers and rangers

One of Tumbledown's established households was that of **Thaddius Thimblewood**, a leatherworker whose family had practiced the trade for generations. His daughter **Rowena** learned at his bench but chose to serve among the village's rangers. There she worked alongside **Graham Rumbuckle**, who had come to Tumbledown from elsewhere and eventually became her husband.

Their son Ferris grew up learning both traditions. Rowena taught him to read tracks and move through the woods; Graham taught him where to stand, when to retreat, and how to defend himself. His grandfather taught him to cut, stitch, and mend leather, while his grandmother, known as **Grams**, taught him how to find food, shelter, and comfort in difficult country.

Such skills served different needs, but all helped sustain a village that depended upon people willing to make things useful and others willing to guard the paths leading home.

## Those who went into the forest

When Ferris was **eleven**, a dangerous presence was reported in the woods near Tumbledown. Rangers went to confront it before it could reach the settlement. Rowena and Graham were among them, and neither returned alive.

**Sylvie**, the village's lead ranger, survived the encounter and returned badly injured with the leaders of the scouts and archers. Rowena's longbow came back broken; Graham's daggers were recovered. The people of Tumbledown remembered the dead as defenders whose stand had spared the village, even as their son struggled to understand the loss.

The nature of the threat that killed the rangers has not been identified in the surviving account.

## The headless rider

Six years after the deaths of Ferris's parents, Tumbledown faced another attack. A **headless rider** arrived upon a nightmarish horse, wielding an ebony blade wreathed in unnatural green flame. The violence reached the village itself, injuring Ferris's grandparents and taking the lives of people he had known since childhood.

Ferris was **seventeen** by then. He fought despite his fear, standing between the attacker and the family still alive behind him. Tumbledown survived, as did Thaddius and Grams, though both suffered serious wounds.

The rider's identity and fate remain unresolved. Nothing in the account establishes that it was the same danger the rangers had confronted six years earlier.

## A home worth returning to

After the attack, Ferris trained and resolved to pursue the rider. His grandfather opposed the departure, having already lost a daughter who chose the woods over the leatherworker's bench. He could not persuade his grandson to stay.

Instead, Thaddius made the leather Ferris would wear on the road. Ferris carried his father's daggers and the longbow he had repaired from his mother's broken weapon.

Tumbledown remains central to the young hunter's story not because it was a famous royal settlement, but because it was a home whose people had reason to fear the woods and reason to defend one another. The full account of the Rumbuckle family, their losses, and Ferris's departure is preserved in **[[Ferris Rumbuckle]]**.
