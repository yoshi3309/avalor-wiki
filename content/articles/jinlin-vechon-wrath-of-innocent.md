## A hatred the companions understood

After their discovery of the truth at Jaznaar's refuge, [[The Misfits]] encountered **Jinlin Vechon**, a **dragonborn vampire** associated with the **Wrath of Innocent**.

The established historical record identifies Jinlin as the **founder** of the Wrath of Innocent. Its full membership, internal arrangements, and the original circumstances of its foundation are not authenticated in the public account.

## An enemy's enemy

Jinlin's **hatred of [[Cid the Blood King]]** was genuine. The Misfits had every reason to oppose Cid as well. Yet the surviving account is explicit that shared hatred did not make the vampire trustworthy.

The affair became a reminder of the danger of confusing vengeance with justice. One may wish to punish a genuine wrongdoer without necessarily acting to protect the innocent. Common opposition to a feared enemy is not a substitute for judging another person's character or actions.

The exact events within **Jinlin's lair** are not reconstructed by the public source. The Royal Archives do not supply a fabricated battle, the names of lieutenants, or an invented fate for the organization.

## A second lair made of memory

The mind flayer Zauks later turned the Misfits' remembered experiences into magical ordeals. **Jinlin's lair** was among the remembered places imitated in those trials.

That later experience should not be mistaken for the original encounter or evidence that Jinlin directed the illusions.

The Wrath of Innocent remains part of the age's history through its founder. Until stronger records emerge, its grand-sounding name cannot serve as proof of any specific creed. What survives most clearly is the Misfits' lesson: the sincerity of revenge does not prove the righteousness of its cause.
