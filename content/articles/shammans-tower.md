## The tower across the water

**Shamman’s Tower** stands off Avalor's eastern coast. Its isolation has long contributed to the reputation of its arcane studies, yet it is neither a forgotten ruin nor simply an outpost of the royal court.

As a young wizard from [[Cove City]], [[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]] studied here, earning a reputation as a curious, somewhat unconventional student accompanied by an octopus in a makeshift traveling tank. The tower later fell into decline.

After the Misfits defeated Cid and Tel'Lora became High Wizard, he returned to the institution of his youth and **revived it as a center of instruction and inquiry**. His restoration built upon an older tradition, while giving its future students new responsibilities.

## Knowledge with accountability

Shamman’s Tower is often contrasted with the [[Black Tower]], where Aldus Greystone III's forbidden experiments brought disaster. The distinction does not absolve the newer academy of caution. Powerful learning requires evidence, good judgment, and the willingness of teachers to recognize their own mistakes.

The tower's work must therefore be judged by more than the discoveries made within it. An institution that encourages students to question a dangerous conclusion may preserve more lives than one that simply rewards dazzling talent.

## The Atlas of Returns

One of the academy's recorded projects is the **Atlas of Returns**, collecting accounts of travel between planes. Such reports invite comparison rather than uncritical repetition. Even an accomplished traveler can misunderstand what was witnessed, and later scholars must be willing to examine competing testimony.

The academy also employs **Second Witness**, a practice providing an additional observer during hazardous investigations. The custom is no guarantee of safety, but recognizes that one investigator's certainty may be insufficient protection against error.

## Two ways to preserve knowledge

[[The Great Library of Aldus Greystone]] at [[Heaven's Heart]] preserves testimony, manuscripts, and the difficult history of magical inquiry. Shamman’s Tower teaches practitioners to test and refine what those records describe. Their duties complement rather than replace one another.

Tel'Lora's role on [[The Aegis]] joins this work of scholarship to the responsibility of public office. The institution's future reputation remains unfinished, and its students will share in the making of it.
