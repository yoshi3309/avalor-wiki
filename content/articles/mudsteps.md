## The streets above the working bank

The **Mudsteps** are a neighborhood within the [[Rhys Quays]] of [[Renada]]. Their dwellings and passages rise unevenly above the working bank of the River Rhys. A family's income here may depend upon a vessel docking on time, the availability of cargo work, or the health of someone whose wages cannot easily be replaced.

The name belongs to the everyday capital rather than the ceremonial one. Its households inhabit a world that court chroniclers have too often described only when a celebrated person emerged from it.

## Lavitz's beginnings

[[Lavitz Shawe]], the drow paladin who became Lord Marshal, grew up in the Mudsteps. His family's circumstances were modest, and his father's livelihood was connected to the riverfront's labor.

His rise brought unusual public attention to a neighborhood whose inhabitants seldom appear in royal registers. The story has been told as evidence that Avalor now judges service and courage more fairly than it once did.

That interpretation contains truth. It must not become an excuse to ignore those who have not received the opportunities that Lavitz found.

## The distance to Crownrise

A climb from the Mudsteps toward [[Crownrise]] crosses more than a difference of elevation. Housing, access to patrons, education, employment, and the ability to withstand misfortune vary greatly between the capital's districts.

The kingdom's historic discrimination against drow residents made such inequalities more severe for some families. The vindication of [[Jaznaar]] and the appointment of a drow Lord Marshal have altered public expectations, but they have not instantly erased every prejudice or hardship.

## More than a childhood story

The Mudsteps do not exist solely to explain Lavitz's character. Their people sustain the capital through work that continues regardless of which hero occupies the seat of Lord Marshal.

The measure of Avalor's present age will lie partly in whether children growing up in these streets are offered a more generous future, even when their names never enter a song.
