## Two names, one man

**Cid the Blood King** is remembered as an enemy whose influence stretched across nearly two thousand years of Avalorian history. For most of that time, his identity was concealed behind one of the kingdom's most cherished legends.

He had once been **[[Aldus Greystone III]]**, the gifted healer and weather-worker counted among [[The Six]]. The names *Aldus* and *Cid* do not describe two different people or a master and his servant. They are the names attached to one man before and after his transformation.

The distinction between the entries in the Royal Archives is historical rather than personal. Aldus's biography follows the rise and ruin of a founding hero; this account traces the danger that survived the destruction of his mortal body and the consequences of a deception that outlived all but one of his companions.

## The face of the Blood King

The being who emerged as **Cid** still carried traces of the healer he had been. He stood roughly **six feet and three inches tall**, gaunt and unnaturally still, with short dark-brown hair and a closely trimmed beard. His eyes shone orange like embers beneath ash. A smile revealed teeth sharpened beyond those of an ordinary man.

Cid wore dark robes beneath ancient black armor marked with crimson runes. A ragged cloak shifted behind him as though the fabric were moving through deep water, and shadows seemed to stretch toward his feet even when the light did not change. Those who faced him sometimes saw the unsettling suggestion of other shapes following his motions a heartbeat behind.

He carried an enormous **enchanted scythe**, its edge steeped in blood sorcery and the corruption of the Shadowfell. It threatened more than flesh; those who survived its passage spoke of the harm it could do to the spirit itself.

Cid rarely needed to shout. He spoke with the composure of someone utterly convinced that resistance would end in his favor. His most frightening weapon was not always magic. He understood despair, and he knew how to offer a suffering person exactly the hope that might persuade them to surrender their judgment.

## The healer before the Blood King

Aldus's earliest work was admired. His talent for healing promised relief to people wounded by war, illness, and hardship, and his companions knew of his first inquiries into what healing might accomplish.

The change was not merely that he attempted an unusually difficult spell. He began to pursue **the defeat of mortality itself**, justifying suffering in the name of a future without death. His companions eventually recognized that the people harmed by his experiments were not unfortunate details to be omitted from the account of his ambition.

[[Lowkee]] and [[Faelynn]] increasingly believed that he must be stopped, even if stopping him required his death. [[Petris]] and [[Jaznaar]] still hoped for his redemption. [[Kelsalor]] struggled to hold the others in conversation. All five opposed the cruelty. The disagreement was over whether their old friend could still be saved.

The division brought the fellowship to the [[Black Tower]] and became known as [[The Sundering of the Six]].

## The catastrophe at the Black Tower

Approximately a decade after Avalor's founding, Aldus attempted a ritual involving the boundary between the Material Plane and the **Shadowfell**. His purpose reached beyond prolonging life: he sought to alter the conditions under which mortals could die.

The remaining five companions intervened. The ritual was interrupted, and Aldus's **mortal body was slain**. The survivors believed the danger ended. No surviving public evidence identifies one certain final blow or authenticates every word spoken inside the tower.

The experiment left widespread harm. The magical corruption remembered as [[The Blood Sickness]] arose from Aldus's research. The region now called the [[Great Desert]] underwent a gradual unnatural change, while already troubled lands such as the [[Dead Marshes]] suffered further injury.

The catastrophe was real. What the kingdom believed about its author would become almost completely wrong.

## A life endured in shadow

Aldus had not simply ceased to exist when his mortal body fell. He **endured in the Shadowfell**, transformed into the entity later called Cid the Blood King.

Jaznaar eventually discovered his former companion's survival. Rather than allowing the threat to return unchecked, he undertook a long guardianship: he sought to **bind Cid** within the Shadowfell and restrain much of the Blood Sickness left by the failed work.

For almost two thousand years the kingdom went about its business without understanding the danger that still endured in the Shadowfell. Cid's efforts to escape the bonds placed upon him remained largely hidden.

The most lasting evidence of his influence is found not in an authenticated catalogue of battles, but in the false account that came to dominate Avalor's public memory.

## The lie that condemned a friend

Cid's agents deliberately encouraged two complementary legends: **Aldus the tragic savior** and **Jaznaar the treacherous saboteur**. The story reversed the meaning of the Black Tower catastrophe, placing blame upon the drow spellcaster who had worked to contain it.

The founding witnesses, including [[Rynhold Frozenroar]], **had not believed Jaznaar guilty**. The false accusation took hold in later generations as testimony passed out of living memory and institutions repeated claims they could no longer independently verify.

The tale benefited Cid by concealing his own responsibility. Its consequences spread far beyond the founding heroes: prejudice against drow communities deepened, and later rulers drew upon the distorted history to justify discriminatory policy. Under [[Arnaud's Edicts]], suspicion was expressed in royal restrictions and displacement.

The deception is documented in [[The Betrayer's Legend]]. Repeated agreement among chronicles, songs, and sermons was not independent proof when their accounts rested on the same corrupted tradition.

## The philosophy that survived Aldus

Long before his mortal death, Aldus had believed that **mortality was a flaw in the world's design**. As Cid, that conviction remained, stripped of the restraint that had once made his healing a gift.

He no longer asked how to ease one person's suffering. He demanded that the world submit to his design, no matter how many people were sacrificed to achieve it. Compassion became, in his eyes, a weakness that delayed an inevitable transformation.

Even after centuries of corruption, the calm and persuasive habits of the old healer remained. Cid did not simply seek to destroy an opponent. He took particular pleasure in breaking the convictions that opponent had once cherished, treating another person's despair as proof that goodness would always fail.

This was the cruelest continuity between Aldus and Cid: the man who once offered relief from pain had learned to make pain the argument for his own rule.

## Prisoners at the ruined temple

**After the Stalker killed Kel'Maris at Stanleyville**, the ruined temple near the town made the Blood King's cruelty visible in another way. Cid and his servants confined the adventurers with others caught in his scheme, restraining their ability to use magic while preparing a rite involving the bodies and power of his captives. **[[Anna, Lowkee's Keeper|Anna]]**, a noncombatant friend of the Misfits, was endangered there upon an altar.

Cid was attended by servants including [[Jaaris]], **[[karina|Karina]]**, **[[black-agnes-mudwallow|Black Agnes Mudwallow]]**, and the mind flayer **[[zauks|Zauks]]**. Jaaris had joined him **after independently leading the hostile fey who killed Tristan and Kristofer**; that earlier crime did not originate with Cid. The venture ended in catastrophe: Captain Funkin, Inspector Sihnion, and guards died. **Anna survived**, but **Fo'Gurn remained to face a hydra** so that his friends could escape. **Kel'Maris was already dead**; Captain Funkin had returned her body to Renada before he died in the temple.

Kel'Maris's death came **earlier**, in the Stalker affair. Cid's legacy cannot be understood by collapsing the Stalker and Hydra losses into one event, or by treating the companions' courage as evidence that those whom they could not rescue mattered less.

## The suffering at Stanleyville

**Before the Hydra temple disaster**, during the travels of [[The Misfits]], the Blood King's actions reached a town whose people did not yet understand the founding-era struggle.

At [[Stanleyville]], an ordinary-looking merchant sold manuscripts to a local family. Later testimony identified the merchant as **Cid in disguise**. One manuscript concealed a **Fireball** spell; when a boy named [[Jack of Stanleyville|Jack]] innocently read the text, the magic killed him.

Jack was not an apprentice seeking forbidden knowledge. His death was the result of a lethal trap placed in something a child could read. His conscious spirit later befriended the adventurers and traveled onward with them.

The creature known as [[The Stalker of Stanleyville|the Stalker]] also hunted Stanleyville under Cid's direction. It killed **[[kel-maris|Kel'Maris]]**, the kingdom's Lord Marshal, who had commissioned the Misfits' early investigations. Captain Funkin returned her body to Renada before his own later death at the Hydra temple. Accounts additionally describe an attempt to burn the town during a brief manifestation of the Blood King.

The Stalker did **not** kill Jack; these were different deaths within the same broad affair. The companions left Stanleyville without yet knowing that Cid and the celebrated founding healer were one being.

## A pattern, not a single hidden hand

The Misfits faced many enemies during their travels, but the presence of a strange creature or dark spell does not prove direct service to Cid.

The unnamed necromancer of their **first commission** was distinct from [[Jaaris]]. The cult of [[The Cult of Gol’Goroth|Gol'Goroth]], the living Moonville Manor, and other early dangers cannot all be assigned to the Blood King merely because the companions later confronted him.

Cid manipulated people and used servants, but the Misfits' every enemy was not his agent. Their travels were larger and stranger than the designs of any one villain.

The crimes traced to Cid were grave enough to darken the history of the kingdom. His deceptions ruined lives, turned allies against one another, and endured long after the original fellowship fell apart.

## The revelation from Jaznaar

The Misfits eventually reached [[Jaznaar#the-refuge-beyond-ordinary-knowledge|Jaznaar's hidden refuge]], where they met the last surviving member of the founding fellowship.

Jaznaar told them that **Aldus and Cid were the same being** and described his long effort to contain the danger. He also instructed [[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]], giving the party knowledge important to their understanding.

The meeting was decisive but did not make every private lesson or the route into the refuge public information. The Royal Commission accepts Jaznaar's central testimony in conjunction with later inquiry and independently examined evidence.

Jaznaar died after the companions departed. The truth he carried survived him, though he did not live to witness the kingdom's acknowledgment of his innocence.

## The Tournament of Champions

The Blood King ultimately brought the danger into the royal capital at [[The Tournament of Champions]]. [[King Gradvum Frozenroar]] and [[Queen Melmiri]] were expected to attend a public celebration in [[Renada]]. The Misfits entered under assumed identities, hoping to protect the royal couple should Cid appear.

They had **not arranged for the monarchs to attend as bait**. Despite their precaution, Cid seized Melmiri and escaped with her, forcing the companions to pursue him toward the Shadowfell.

The surviving evidence does not support a claim that the queen possessed unique blood or ancestry needed for a ritual. She was important as a royal captive and as a person the Misfits would not abandon.

## The final confrontation

The completed account is preserved in **[[The Shadowfell Confrontation]]**. Jaaris had raised the dead Fo'Gurn through necromancy, forcing the paladin against his friends. Fo'Gurn resisted this control by protecting Lavitz before Jaaris died.

The warrior beside Anna was **Samuel temporarily channeling Lowkee through the Emerald of Armanti**. Anna was later revealed to be **Erevan Ilesere in disguise**. Fo'Gurn pressed the Emerald into Cid's chest to bar his return to the Material Plane. The black hands that dragged Cid into the Beyond had no established controller, and their action was distinct from the Emerald's seal.


The companions rescued Melmiri in the Shadowfell and confronted the Blood King. The event is now recorded separately in **[[The Shadowfell Confrontation]]**, where the rescue and the choices of the Misfits receive their own historical treatment. In accounts of the final struggle, **[[fo-gurn|Fo'Gurn]]**, the dragonborn paladin long believed dead, appeared with the **Emerald of Armanti** and drove it against Cid.

Then an immense **black hand** emerged from an unidentified darkness and seized the Blood King, drawing him away. The companions witnessed his removal, but did **not** establish who or what had claimed him, its origin, or the place to which Cid was taken.

The immediate danger was defeated. The Misfits survived in part through Melmiri's intervention, and the queen returned home.

Cid was taken from the battle, not confirmed annihilated. The immense hand and the darkness from which it emerged remained mysteries to the people who witnessed his defeat.

## The kingdom after the lie

With Cid's defeat, the recovered evidence overturned Avalor's oldest false account. [[King Gradvum Frozenroar]] formally [[The Vindication of Jaznaar|vindicated Jaznaar]], while institutions began correcting records and monuments that had honored Aldus as an innocent savior.

At [[Renada#rynhold-park-and-public-memory|Rynhold Park]], the statue of Aldus remained standing, but the former inscription was replaced with a corrected account and preserved at [[The Great Library of Aldus Greystone]].

The episode did not erase the healer Aldus had once been, or the trust that his companions had given him. It established the terrible consequences of what he chose to become.

**Cid's legacy is a warning not only about forbidden magic, but about the ease with which power can turn a convenient historical lie into an inheritance passed down by honest people.**
