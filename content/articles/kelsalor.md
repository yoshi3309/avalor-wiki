## The world beyond a boundary stone

**Kelsalor** was a firbolg druid among [[The Six]], remembered for a philosophy of the natural world that did not begin or end at the borders of Avalor.

Forests grow and die. Rivers change their courses. Creatures hunt, flourish, and perish. Kelsalor understood these processes as elements of a living balance, not as events that could always be judged by whether they helped or hindered a single kingdom.

His work was not to hold nature still. It was to protect the conditions upon which life depended.

## A different kind of stewardship

The founding kingdom required roads, timber, cultivation, and settlements. Such needs sometimes brought rulers and merchants into conflict with ancient woodlands and communities whose ways of life could not be reduced to the interests of the crown.

Kelsalor's knowledge was valuable precisely because he did not regard every forest as material awaiting use or every creature as an obstacle. His regard for the living world might complicate the ambitions of administrators; it could also spare them errors whose consequences would endure for generations.

This principle is useful in considering the [[Forest of the Fey]] and the [[Dead Marshes]], though the public record does not assign Kelsalor a formal office governing either region.

## Between friends at the Black Tower

The division of the Six over the experiments of Aldus Greystone III demanded more than a judgment about magic. It threatened a fellowship whose members had shared danger, labor, and affection.

[[Lowkee]] came to believe Aldus had to be stopped. [[Faelynn]] shared that conclusion increasingly, while [[Petris]] and [[Jaznaar]] wished to preserve whatever hope remained of saving him. Kelsalor tried to **mediate** between them.

His belief in balance did not make him indifferent to cruelty. It made him reluctant to declare a painful choice simple merely because urgency demanded that some choice be made.

## A legacy carried by travelers

Kelsalor traveled widely. Unlike Lowkee, whose teaching became associated with a lasting order at [[Heaven's Heart]], he left much of his inheritance in the places he protected and the traditions he passed to others.

His descendants carried that inheritance forward. Among them was [[Heureux]], the firbolg bard who became a member of [[The Misfits]] in the Age of Ascendancy.

The passage of time altered the forms of their service. It did not erase the possibility that attention to the lives of others might survive across centuries.
