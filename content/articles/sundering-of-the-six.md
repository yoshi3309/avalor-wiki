## When a companion became the danger

The Six—Aldus Greystone III, [[Lowkee]], [[Faelynn]], [[Petris]], [[Kelsalor]], and [[Jaznaar]]—had helped give the young kingdom an image of cooperation across different peoples and beliefs.

The discovery of Aldus’s experimentation shattered their unity. The five companions agreed that his work had caused suffering. They disagreed about whether he could still be saved.

Lowkee concluded that Aldus must die if necessary to end the danger; Faelynn increasingly agreed. Petris and Jaznaar hoped the man they had loved could be reached. Kelsalor sought to mediate. None of these positions meant that the crimes of Aldus were acceptable.

## At the Black Tower

Approximately a decade after Avalor’s founding, Aldus attempted a ritual at the [[Black Tower]] in the [[Spine of the World]]. His ambition was not merely to extend life, but to change the conditions of mortality itself. The ritual involved the Material Plane and the Shadowfell.

The remaining five traveled to stop him. Later ballads preserve persuasive farewell speeches and exact words no contemporary record authenticates.

The accounts of the confrontation itself vary. Cathedral traditions emphasize an assault, while arcane historians describe a disastrous magical backlash.

## A mortal defeat

The facts upon which the principal traditions agree are grave: **the ritual was interrupted, Aldus’s mortal body was slain, and the worst of his intended catastrophe was prevented**.

There is no securely established single killing blow. The companions believed their friend dead and left the tower mourning what had become of the fellowship.

But Aldus survived in another form. He endured in the Shadowfell as the being later called [[Cid the Blood King]].

## The wound in memory

Only later did Jaznaar discover what had survived. His efforts to protect Avalor were eventually hidden beneath a false history, which named him betrayer and recast Aldus as savior.

The original eyewitness companions **did not believe Jaznaar guilty**. This was a lie that gained its strength afterward, as living testimony faded and repeated stories took its place.
