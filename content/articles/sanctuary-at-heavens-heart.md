## The families behind the gates

When **Arnaud's discriminatory decrees** threatened drow households, some families sought sanctuary at [[Heaven's Heart]], the settlement associated with [[The Cathedral Paladins]].

The paladin order had been founded by [[Lowkee]], whose First Teaching insisted that an oath was owed above all to those who could not stand without protection. During Arnaud’s reign that teaching became a direct challenge to an unjust royal demand.

## Jeyse and Cal’el

The order's leader was **Jeyse**, a **deva** responsible for the sanctuary's discipline and the people seeking shelter. Alongside her stood **Cal’el**, an **aasimar** recognized as an earthly representative of Tyr, Helm, and Torm.

Royal officers demanded that those sheltering under the order's protection be surrendered for inspection. Jeyse refused to permit their seizure **on the basis of ancestry alone**. Cal’el argued publicly that a law could not make an innocent person guilty simply by naming them suspect.

Their roles were different but complementary. Jeyse bore immediate responsibility for the sanctuary and the paladins; Cal’el challenged the religious and moral legitimacy of the order.

## An order that did not agree unanimously

Some paladins feared that defiance would invite violence and leave the sheltered families in greater danger. Others believed surrender would betray the very teaching they had sworn to honor.

Cathedral histories describe armed officers halted before the sanctuary. Royal records describe obstruction of a lawful inquiry. Their perspectives differ, but **neither supplies evidence for a general battle between the crown and the paladins**.

Later versions attribute stirring words to the participants. They are not authenticated direct quotations of what was said at the gates.

## A negotiated end

Opposition to the harshest restrictions increased. The confrontation ended through negotiation, and the families were **not surrendered merely for being drow**.

The settlement curtailed the immediate threat without restoring every seized home, trade, or feeling of safety. **No general war followed.**

The event remains a living illustration of Lowkee's teaching: duty measured not by obedience to the powerful, but by the people a sworn protector refuses to abandon.
