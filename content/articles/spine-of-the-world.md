## The mountains of northern Avalor

The **Spine of the World** rises across the northern kingdom, shaping the journeys of those who live near it and the routes by which travelers move between communities. Its heights and passes offer protection against some dangers while imposing others of their own: difficult approaches, isolation, and weather that does not readily submit to the plans of merchants or armies.

Although great buildings have made the mountains famous, the region is not simply a backdrop to the lives of celebrated figures. Communities near and among its slopes have kept routes open, housed travelers, and sustained traditions seldom preserved in royal histories.

## Heaven's Heart beneath the heights

Near the mountains' foot stands [[Heaven's Heart]], a settlement and sanctuary associated with [[Lowkee]] and the traditions of [[The Cathedral Paladins]]. Its students are taught that strength is a duty owed to those who need protection, rather than a privilege granted for the benefit of the powerful.

The [[The Black Stair]] is remembered among the approaches to the mountains and the sanctuary. Its demanding ascent belongs to the ordinary experience of travelers, scholars, and pilgrims who have had reason to make the journey.

The nearby [[The Great Library of Aldus Greystone]] preserves manuscripts and contested histories, including evidence through which Avalor has reconsidered its account of the founding heroes.

## The Black Tower

Within the same mountain region stands the [[The Black Tower]], the site where **Aldus Greystone III** attempted his catastrophic work upon life and death. The effort was interrupted by his former companions, [[The Six]], but its consequences endured beyond the mountain walls.

For generations, royal restrictions limited access to the tower. Reports of dangerous terrain and disappearances reinforced its forbidding reputation. The later defeat of Cid the Blood King and the vindication of Jaznaar compelled historians to reconsider the story once attached to the place.

The tower's location has not changed. The understanding of its history has.

## Consequences beyond the mountains

The catastrophe altered lands well beyond the Spine. Country in the north gradually became the [[Great Desert]], while the already wounded [[Dead Marshes]] suffered further magical disturbance. Accounts also describe storms troubling the mountain region, though scholars cannot assign every instance of violent weather to one ancient ritual.

The distinction between established effects and conjecture remains important. The mountain range contains the place from which a disaster spread; it does not follow that every danger in its passes shares that cause.

## A landscape of contrary inheritances

The Spine of the World contains two striking legacies within one geographical region. At Heaven's Heart, generations have learned to defend others and question unjust commands. At the Black Tower, a celebrated healer's ambition brought ruin to lives and landscapes alike.

To regard the mountains only as a warning is to forget the service of those who dwell beneath them. To regard them only as a place of sanctuary is to ignore the catastrophe whose effects remain visible across Avalor. Their history requires room for both.
