## The sixth voice at the campfire

**Heureux** was the firbolg bard who joined the company that became [[The Misfits]] during its investigation of a cult devoted to Gol’Goroth. He came to a fellowship whose members already had their own habits and loyalties, and helped give it the character by which it would later be remembered.

His ancestor was [[Kelsalor]], the firbolg druid of [[The Six]]. Where Kelsalor devoted himself to the living balance of nature, Heureux preserved the lives and experiences of others through song and story.

## A historian who was there

The public account of the Misfits rests in part upon **Heureux’s published history** and testimony authorized by the surviving companions. His writing preserved the unheroic moments alongside the celebrated ones: quarrels, strange transformations, fear, humor, and the memory of friends who had been lost.

His account must still be read critically. Friendship gives a witness knowledge outsiders lack, but it also shapes what is remembered and how events are understood. The Royal Commission treats Heureux as an important participant, not an infallible record.

His wit became one of the fellowship’s signatures. Its value lay partly in reminding readers that their heroes had once been ordinary companions facing extraordinary events.

## The academy and the road

After the defeat of Cid, Heureux founded [[heureuxs-bard-academy|a bardic academy]] in **Taveras**. He continued to travel, perform, and gather stories rather than retiring permanently from the wider world. The institution became part of the cultural life of the years following the victory.

[[The Bard Guild]] is another established institution, but the public record does not establish a formal administrative relationship between it and the academy. Neither its hierarchy nor the academy’s precise charter should be invented.

## The fellowship at a distance

The five publicly celebrated Misfits did not gather again as a complete company during the subsequent twenty years. Their loyalty endured. Heureux’s own journeys, and his commitment to recording the lives of his friends, helped keep a history shared by people whose duties now led along different roads.

For the bard, remembrance was neither the end of adventure nor an attempt to make every hardship beautiful. It was a way of refusing to let the lives behind the legends disappear.
