## A forest older than the kingdom

Long before the lion banner of Avalor was raised, elven communities lived within the **Forest of the Fey**. Its paths and settlements belong to histories that do not begin with Rynhold Frozenroar. The forest is neither empty wilderness awaiting settlement nor a single realm whose inhabitants all speak with one political voice.

For those who live there, ancient woodland may also be ancestral territory. Rights over travel, habitation, and the use of the land have consequently carried a significance that royal cartographers and visiting merchants have not always understood.

## The Elven Concord

During the reign of **Dietmar Frozenroar**, disputes over roads, settlement, taxation, and ancestral authority grew into open conflict between the crown and elven communities. The settlement remembered as the **Elven Concord** recognized the Frozenroar monarchy while preserving important traditional rights and elements of clan government.

The subsequent marriage of Dietmar's **son and heir, Halton Frozenroar**, to **Dathyra Blueshadow** strengthened the peace. Their son **Sylavor** inherited the crown after Halton, bringing Dathyra's elven ancestry into the royal bloodline.

Royal accounts have sometimes described the Concord as a set of concessions granted by the crown. Woodland traditions offer another reading: that the kingdom acknowledged rights already held before Avalor existed. These perspectives need not be made identical in order to understand why the agreement endured.

## The Concord Walk

An agreement can outlast the rulers who signed it and still require attention from the people who inherit its obligations.

Some woodland communities observe a practice called the **Concord Walk**. At locally chosen intervals, elven elders and crown representatives walk portions of agreed boundaries together. They may discuss new roads, altered use of woodland, encroachment, or obligations that require renewal.

The custom is neither practiced in every settlement nor a substitute for the laws that protect ancestral rights. Its significance lies in making the political settlement an occasion for continuing dialogue, rather than a document remembered only when conflict returns.

## Many communities, not one allegiance

The forest's inhabitants maintain different relationships with the wider kingdom. Some communities trade peacefully with royal towns and welcome travelers; others protect their autonomy more closely. Reports of a genuinely hostile wild-elven faction deserve attention, yet its actions must not be mistaken for the character of every clan or settlement.

Such differences are not evidence of one people incapable of agreement. They are the ordinary variety of a long-inhabited region whose members have different memories, obligations, and interests.

## The Ringtree beneath the canopy

The **[[The Ringtree Clan|Ringtree Clan]]** preserves a different example of the lives hidden among these woods. Its firbolg communities maintained **semi-nomadic canopy settlements**, protected by illusion and invisibility, while recording generations of stories in written and spoken form.

Their reverence for **[[The Primordial Powers#her-and-the-work-of-becoming|Her]]** connected the rhythms of growth and decay with music, invention, and the survival of memory. The bard **[[Heureux]]** grew up among them before the destruction of their library forced him into exile.

The Ringtree do not represent every forest community. Nor did their history begin with the crown's treaty. Their presence underscores why the forest has never been merely a border on a royal map.

## Land rights and an inhabited forest

The woodland communities' claims concern more than trees. Routes, settlements, traditional authority, and the use of particular places can be inseparable from the stories by which those places are remembered.

The Elven Concord provided a way to respect such histories within a united kingdom, but did not end every disagreement. Some communities remain close to royal institutions; others guard a greater distance.

Neither an unfamiliar way of governing nor a hidden home makes a community less real. The Forest of the Fey is inhabited country whose people have repeatedly had to insist upon that fact.

## The name and the plane

Despite its name, the **Forest of the Fey** is a place in the mortal world. It should not be confused with the **Feywild**, a separate [[The Planes of Existence|plane of existence]]. The resemblance of their names has invited comparisons, legends, and speculation, but does not make the two places geographically identical.

The nearby [[Dead Marshes]] likewise have their own troubled history. Farther east, [[Denzians Forest]] is a separate woodland with a different name and body of traditions. A visitor studying the lands of Avalor should not mistake all forests—or all their inhabitants—for one another.
