## A forest older than the kingdom

Long before the lion banner of Avalor was raised, elven communities lived within the **Forest of the Fey**. Its paths and settlements belong to histories that do not begin with Rynhold Frozenroar. The forest is neither empty wilderness awaiting settlement nor a single realm whose inhabitants all speak with one political voice.

For those who live there, ancient woodland may also be ancestral territory. Rights over travel, habitation, and the use of the land have consequently carried a significance that royal cartographers and visiting merchants have not always understood.

## The Elven Concord

During the reign of **Dietmar Frozenroar**, disputes over roads, settlement, taxation, and ancestral authority grew into open conflict between the crown and elven communities. The settlement remembered as the **Elven Concord** recognized the Frozenroar monarchy while preserving important traditional rights and elements of clan government.

The subsequent marriage of Dietmar's **acknowledged heir**, **Halton Frozenroar**, to **Dathyra Blueshadow** strengthened the peace. The Royal Register does not specify Dietmar and Halton's kinship. Halton and Dathyra were the parents of Sylavor. Their union also established an elven ancestry within the royal house that later generations would inherit.

Royal accounts have sometimes described the Concord as a set of concessions granted by the crown. Woodland traditions offer another reading: that the kingdom acknowledged rights already held before Avalor existed. These perspectives need not be made identical in order to understand why the agreement endured.

## Many communities, not one allegiance

The forest's inhabitants maintain different relationships with the wider kingdom. Some communities trade peacefully with royal towns and welcome travelers; others protect their autonomy more closely. Reports of a genuinely hostile wild-elven faction deserve attention, yet its actions must not be mistaken for the character of every clan or settlement.

Such differences are not evidence of one people incapable of agreement. They are the ordinary variety of a long-inhabited region whose members have different memories, obligations, and interests.

## The name and the plane

Despite its name, the **Forest of the Fey** is a place in the mortal world. It should not be confused with the **Feywild**, a separate plane of existence. The resemblance of their names has invited comparisons, legends, and speculation, but does not make the two places geographically identical.

The nearby [[Dead Marshes]] likewise have their own troubled history. Farther east, [[Denzians Forest]] is a separate woodland with a different name and body of traditions. A visitor studying the lands of Avalor should not mistake all forests—or all their inhabitants—for one another.
