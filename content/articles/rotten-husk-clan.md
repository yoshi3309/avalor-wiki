## A people, not merely an encounter

**The Rotten Husk**, an established **orc tribe** also described in some accounts as a clan, has a history of its own. Its name appears in the travels of [[The Misfits]], but the people themselves should not be reduced to an opponent in a famous adventuring story.

The surviving record does not identify the clan's origin, the location of its homeland, or the internal customs by which its members understood their community. The name and the fact of their existence are established. Claims about ritual, rank, or geography would be later invention.

## Under a dangerous chieftain

In the age of the Misfits, the tribe's **chieftain became a danger to his own people**. That fact is central to what the adventurers found, but it is not proof that the tribe as a whole supported his actions.

The chieftain's personal name, the circumstances through which he acquired authority, and the particulars of his abuses have not been securely preserved in the public testimony. The Royal Archives therefore do not attempt a reconstruction of the clan's political order.

## Liberation and a friendship earned

The Misfits helped free the Rotten Husk from their chieftain. The accounts disagree on the precise form of the confrontation, yet agree upon its essential outcome: **the people were liberated, and the companions departed with the tribe's goodwill**.

The episode is told in [[The Misfits#the-liberation-of-the-rotten-husk|the fellowship's history]], where its place among their other adventures can be understood without making the clan's identity depend entirely upon that day.

## The history that remains theirs

The clan's later leadership, territory, and alliances are absent from the Commission's surviving accounts. The Misfits' intervention ended the immediate danger, while the community's subsequent history remains largely unwritten.

The significance of the Rotten Husk is precisely that they were **people worth defending on their own terms**. Avalor's history has too often treated entire communities as though the deeds of one leader defined them all. The surviving account of this clan remembers a better distinction.
