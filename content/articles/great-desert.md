## The country before the sand

To travelers of the present age, the **Great Desert** can appear as old and unchanging as the mountains beside it. Historical records preserve a more troubling account. The lands now known for heat, sand, and difficult crossings did **not** possess their modern form during the founding of Avalor.

The transformation followed the catastrophe at the **Black Tower** in the [[Spine of the World]]. Aldus Greystone III's forbidden experiments brought ruin far beyond the tower itself. In the north, country previously accustomed to different seasons began an unnatural change into the Great Desert.

The change unfolded over time, rather than in a single instant. Its consequences altered roads, patterns of settlement, and the needs of those who defended and supplied the frontier. The precise course of that transformation, including the full extent of displacement and the magical workings involved, remains imperfectly documented.

## The shadow of the Black Tower

The founding generation knew Aldus as a gifted healer. His attempt to overcome mortality led instead to a catastrophe whose effects can still be read upon the landscape. The Great Desert is among the clearest reminders that a choice made in one age can alter the lives of generations never consulted in its making.

It would nevertheless be an error to assign every feature of the present desert to a single spell or to pretend that historians can reconstruct each stage of the disaster. The Royal Commission distinguishes the established connection to Aldus's experiments from details the surviving records cannot establish.

## The peoples of the desert

The desert is no empty memorial to an ancient crime. Its inhabitants have developed their own knowledge of movement, survival, commerce, and community in difficult country. These histories belong to them, not merely to the man whose actions helped change their homeland.

**Queen Melmiri's** ancestry among the **Northern Desert Tribes** has brought part of this heritage into the Frozenroar royal household. Yet no royal marriage or single family can stand for the varied lives and traditions of all desert communities.

## A frontier that still changes

The Great Desert continues to influence travel, political interests, and the practical responsibilities of the crown. Those crossing it must reckon with its present conditions, while those studying it must remember that it was not always as it is.

The [[Dead Marshes]] also bear injuries associated with the Black Tower, but their history began earlier and followed a different course. The two landscapes should not be treated as products of an identical magical event simply because both preserve the consequences of Aldus's work.


## The place where the change began

The [[Black Tower]] in the Spine of the World remains central to the historical explanation of the desert's transformation. Its new public record distinguishes the consequences of Aldus's work from legends surrounding the mountain site.
