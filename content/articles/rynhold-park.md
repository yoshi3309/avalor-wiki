## A public place among the monuments

**Rynhold Park** is one of the public spaces of [[Renada]], where inhabitants of different stations may walk without entering a royal residence or a private household. Its paths and monuments commemorate the founding king and those associated with the union of Avalor.

The park therefore belongs to two histories at once: the story the kingdom has chosen to tell about its origins, and the changing judgments of the people who pass through it.

## The monument to Aldus Greystone

Among its monuments stands a statue of **Aldus Greystone III**. For centuries, the figure was honored as a founding hero, while [[Jaznaar]] was falsely condemned as the Betrayer.

After [[The Misfits]] defeated [[Cid the Blood King]], evidence established that Aldus himself had become Cid and was responsible for the crimes long laid upon his former companion. The monument's continued presence became a matter of public debate.

The city did not pull down the statue. Instead, its old inscription was removed and transferred to the historical collections of [[The Great Library of Aldus Greystone]] at [[Heaven's Heart]]. A new plaque identifies Aldus with Cid and records the correction of Jaznaar's history.

## What should remain in stone?

The decision has not satisfied every citizen. Some regard the correction as overdue honesty. Others believe the statue should no longer stand at all. A smaller number cling to the account once presented as truth.

Historians have also disagreed about how public memory ought to work. Lady Miren Dast has argued for preserving evidence of what earlier generations believed; Professor Xiomara Thal has insisted that changing an inscription cannot undo the lives damaged by the original lie.

Both concerns belong to the study of the park.

## A place for the living

Rynhold Park is not merely a lesson carved in stone. It is also a place where citizens meet, rest, and share the space of a city whose inequalities are visible beyond its paths.

The monument now demands more of its viewers than admiration. It invites them to ask what the kingdom honored, what it overlooked, and what it has at last chosen to remember.
