## The man behind the title

A traveler arriving in [[Renada]] may hear the name **Lavitz Shawe** before ever seeing the man. Some know him as the paladin who helped end the reign of [[Cid the Blood King]]. Others know him as **Lord Marshal**, the officer entrusted with advising the crown on the kingdom's defense. For children in the poorer quarters of the capital, his name means something less distant: one of their own grew up to stand among the realm's highest counselors.

Lavitz did not begin his life among generals or courtiers. He grew up in the [[Renada#the-mudsteps|Mudsteps]], the working neighborhood within the [[Rhys Quays]] district of Renada. The distance between those streets and the [[Great Citadel]] is not merely a matter of steep roads. It is a distance in wealth, influence, and the assumptions a kingdom makes about who is permitted to wield authority.

That Lavitz crossed it does not make the hardships of the Mudsteps disappear. It makes his appointment unusually meaningful.

## Appearance and bearing

Lavitz is a tall, powerfully built **drow**, often recognized by his white hair, bright blue eyes, and a visible facial scar. The bearing of a trained warrior is difficult for him to conceal, even when the occasion calls for a ceremonial coat rather than plate armor.

His official insignia identifies the Lord Marshal, but his reputation was made long before he wore it. Court dress and military ceremony have not turned him into a man inclined to mistake the appearance of authority for the practice of it.

To many Avalorians, the figure is immediately recognizable: a paladin who could have remained in the songs of an adventuring age but instead chose the daily burdens of protecting a realm.

## Varis and Thia

Lavitz was born to **Varis and Thia Shawe**, two drow who had escaped slavery and the reach of Lolth's teachings in search of a different life. They came to [[Renada]] hoping that Avalor's reputation for greater tolerance would give them a chance to live in peace.

They found freedom, but not equality.

Varis worked as a ship hand along the [[Renada#rhys-quays|Rhys Quays]], often concealing his face to avoid the hostility directed toward drow. Thia found work behind the scenes in a seamstress's shop run by a kindly halfling. Together they built a modest household in [[Renada#the-mudsteps|the Mudsteps]], proud of the freedom their new life represented even when others saw only their poverty.

They raised their son away from the worship of the Spider-Queen. The gods **Tyr, Helm, and Torm** were part of Lavitz's childhood, along with the belief that a person's worth should be measured by what they chose to do.

When Lavitz was **five**, Varis broke up a dispute between a human and a dwarf on the docks. In the struggle, his covering fell away and the workers saw that he was drow. The owner dismissed him rather than face their anger. On the way home, Varis was attacked and stabbed by unknown assailants. He made it back to the family door, where Thia and their son found him dead.

The boy grew up knowing that his father had been punished for trying to stop a fight.

## A home called Vixen's Heart

Without Varis's wages, Thia could no longer keep the family in its little house. She found work at **Vixen's Heart**, a brothel in Renada, and brought Lavitz with her.

To an outsider, the place might have seemed an unlikely home for a child. To Lavitz, it was full of women who cared for him, taught him what they could, and made room for him beside the communal fire. Thia worked to give him a life, and the women of Vixen's Heart became an extended family who treated him as one of their own.

That warmth made the next loss all the more terrible. When Lavitz was **fourteen**, Thia was murdered after refusing the demands of a nobleman. Her death went unpunished.

Lavitz knew what it meant to be surrounded by love. Now, for the first time, he began to nurture a hatred so fierce that he imagined killing the man responsible.

**Mother Kathra**, the dwarf who ran Vixen's Heart, let him stay. He helped in the kitchens, learned to fight from the bouncers and to handle knives from the head cook, and grew into a formidable young man. Eventually he took charge of the establishment's security. Protecting the women who had raised him gave his growing strength a purpose, even as the memory of his mother's murderer remained close.

## The night he set the knife down

Lavitz found the nobleman who had killed Thia. One night, after praying to Tyr and Helm, he crossed into the wealthier part of Renada and entered the man's home with a dagger.

The nobleman slept unaware while Lavitz stood over him. He had wanted this moment for years, imagining it would bring justice at last.

But with the blade raised, he understood that murder would neither restore his mother nor cure the anger he carried. He could not bring himself to make the killing blow.

Lavitz left a warning instead. He wrote that he knew what the nobleman had done, invoked the fear of the Spider-Queen as a threat, and demanded that money be given to improve the lives of the women of Vixen's Heart. He drove his dagger into the note above the sleeping man's bed and disappeared into the night.

It was not the justice Thia deserved. Nor was it a perfect act of mercy: Lavitz had used fear to force a response. Yet the choice **not to kill** marked the first time he refused to let his grief decide what kind of man he would become.

When he finally left Vixen's Heart, the family he had found there gave him a farewell worthy of a son. His first longsword he named **Thia's Lament**, and his dagger **Twig**, remembering the nickname his father had given him. Mother Kathra offered an unexpected gift of her own: a young **velociraptor named Rodrigo**.

Rodrigo became Lavitz's faithful companion, affectionate with those he considered family and fiercely protective when they were threatened.

## Astronoph and Heaven's Heart

Lavitz traveled north to **[[Heaven's Heart]]** in search of a calling that might put his strength to better use. He arrived cautiously, knowing how much suspicion a drow could still provoke.

During a visit to the sanctuary, **[[Astronoph]]**, the female deva who served as High Priest of [[The Great Cathedral]] in Renada, met him and recognized his potential. She encouraged him to join **[[The Cathedral Paladins]]**. Lavitz might become a defender of those treated as outcasts, and a living reminder that no drow was bound to the Spider-Queen's path.

He took the opportunity seriously. Training in heavy armor and with a wide range of weapons became part of his daily life. He worked hard to build the endurance his calling demanded, drawing on lessons of justice, vigilance, and duty rather than the anger that had almost consumed him.

At Heaven's Heart he met **[[fo-gurn|Fo'Gurn]]**, a **brass dragonborn** training in the same tradition. Fo'Gurn treated him with respect rather than suspicion. They became close friends, challenging and encouraging each other as they learned the responsibilities of a paladin.

## Ninety days at the Black Stair

One trial of Lavitz's training took him to a **tomb near the foot of the [[The Black Tower#the-black-stair|Black Stair]]**, where he kept a **ninety-day vigil**. The long days demanded a different kind of strength from the physical discipline of the training grounds: the patience to remain at his post and the resolve to keep the commitment he had made.

Beyond him, the Black Stair climbed toward **the Black Tower**, a place burdened with the memory of the founding catastrophe. **Heaven's Heart lay nearby, but the stair was not a route to the sanctuary.** Lavitz's vigil belonged to his own paladin journey, not to an ascent of Aldus's tower.

## The curse beneath a full moon

Years into his service, Lavitz was sent toward the [[Forest of the Fey]] after reports of trouble between a village and a hostile group of wild elves.

The elves ambushed him and subjected him to a cruel blood ritual. They sought to humiliate him for being drow, using the night itself as the instrument of their contempt. Under a **full moon**, their magic transformed him not into a fearsome beast but into a **small, harmless-looking dog**.

The paladins at Heaven's Heart could not immediately undo the curse. Lavitz had every reason to hate those responsible. Instead, remembering the hatred that had once brought him to a sleeping nobleman's bedside, he chose to **forgive them**. He would rather try to guide people away from cruelty than allow their cruelty to determine his own heart.

It was an early expression of the devotion he would embrace more fully after the defeat of Cid: justice without the surrender of mercy, and protection without the need for hatred.

## The companion before the legend

The group later known as [[The Misfits]] did not gather to defeat an ancient evil or rewrite the kingdom's history. Its members were brought together by a commission from the **preceding Lord Marshal**, who sought help investigating a necromancer.

Alongside Lavitz stood [[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]], the half-elven wizard; [[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]], the eladrin; [[Amara]], a druid whose family line reached back to the founding age; and Fo’Gurn. They overcame their first enemy and recovered fragments of magical research whose significance became clear only much later. The affair is preserved as [[The Misfits’ First Commission]].

A second commission, concerning [[Independent Factions of the Misfits Era#the-cult-of-gol-goroth|the Cult of Gol’Goroth]], brought [[Heureux]] into their company. The firbolg bard became both a companion and, in time, one of the most important tellers of their shared history.

The fellowship endured because its members repeatedly chose to remain together. Their temperaments differed, and their missions did not always belong to one great conspiracy. They became friends through the less ceremonial work of surviving the road.

## Adventures on roads without a single destiny

The histories of the Misfits begin with the living halls of [[The Misfits#moonville-manor|Moonville Manor]], **before their first royal commission**. Later came [[Noki]], a wizard transformed into a sheep, and the liberation of the [[The Rotten Husk Clan|Rotten Husk]] orc tribe from a dangerous chieftain.

These encounters are easily overshadowed by the later defeat of Cid. They should not be. People who received help during those journeys did not need an explanation of the Black Tower to know whether the adventurers had made a difference.

Later came the crisis at [[The Tournament of Champions]], where Cid took Queen Melmiri. The pursuit was interrupted by Ros Furband's pirate ship, a real confrontation with Jinlin Vechon and the Wrath of Innocent, and finally [[Zauks and the False Lives|Zauks's illusions]]. These dangers tested the companions' trust as much as their strength. The public accounts preserve separate histories of these events precisely because not every enemy was secretly an instrument of the Blood King.

For Lavitz, the road offered something the office of Lord Marshal would eventually take from him: the freedom to respond immediately to a person in danger, to choose a course with his companions, and to shoulder the risk himself.

## Fo’Gurn and the cost of survival

Among the losses that shaped Lavitz, few were more personal than the stand remembered in [[Fo’Gurn and the Hydra]].

When the company faced a hydra, Fo’Gurn remained behind to buy his friends enough time to escape. They survived, believing the dragonborn had died. For Lavitz, this was not simply the loss of another adventurer. He had known Fo’Gurn before there were Misfits to speak of, and the memory of his friend's choice followed him long after the encounter itself.

Later discoveries involving the necromancer [[Jaaris]] complicated the company's understanding of Fo’Gurn's fate. They did not undo the years in which the surviving companions mourned him. Testimony of the final Shadowfell struggle also remembers Lavitz refusing to abandon his oldest friend when they met again, and a choice by Fo’Gurn to stand beside him. The reasons for his reappearance remain beyond the public account.

Lavitz's willingness to place himself between danger and others is among his defining virtues. It can also be a difficult habit for a leader: he would rather bear the risk himself than ask another to bear it. A Lord Marshal must learn that protecting a kingdom cannot depend upon a single person standing in every breach.

## Stanleyville, Jack, and the vacant office

The Misfits' investigation of mysterious deaths at [[Stanleyville]] brought them face to face with another kind of cruelty. They encountered [[Jack of Stanleyville]], a murdered child whose conscious spirit sought the truth of his own death. Rather than remain in the town, Jack traveled onward with the companions.

The affair also claimed the life of the Lord Marshal who had first summoned the adventurers. He was killed during the attack of [[The Stalker of Stanleyville|the Stalker]], a creature later connected to Cid.

The office was left vacant. At the time, Lavitz had no reason to know that he would one day be asked to fill it.

The tragedy belonged not only to the kingdom's military history, but to the lives of those who survived the town's ordeal. Jack was a traveling companion, not merely a clue; the preceding marshal had been a man who placed trust in people not yet recognized as heroes.

## Against the Blood King

The Misfits' journeys eventually led to the discovery of **[[jaznaar|Jaznaar Mizzrorzza]]**, the founding hero long falsely condemned as the Betrayer. They learned that the celebrated healer **Aldus Greystone III** had endured after the [[The Sundering of the Six|Black Tower catastrophe]] as **Cid the Blood King**.

Before they faced Cid, Jaznaar entrusted Lavitz with **Cid's Bane**, a sword that had once belonged to **[[Lowkee]]** himself. It was a gift heavy with history: a founding paladin's weapon placed in the hands of a drow paladin who had grown up in the shadow of the kingdom's prejudice.

In the Shadowfell, the companions confronted and defeated Cid. The victory brought more than the end of a formidable enemy. It exposed the old lie that had stained Avalor's history for nearly two thousand years and made possible [[The Vindication of Jaznaar|Jaznaar's public vindication]].

Lavitz's part in that victory carried a particular weight. He was a drow helping restore the honor of a drow founding hero while serving a kingdom whose history included grievous injustice toward drow communities. The connection did not erase those injustices, but it made their continued defense increasingly difficult.

## From vengeance to devotion

Lavitz once followed the **Oath of Vengeance**. In the years after the defeat of Cid, his calling matured into the **Oath of Devotion**. The change is not best understood as the rejection of everything that once sustained him. It marks an expansion in whom he believes himself responsible for protecting.

Vengeance may demand that a particular wrong be answered. Devotion asks what must be preserved when the wrong has been answered and those who survive must still live together. To fight one enemy is a terrible burden; to remain steadfast for thousands of people, most of whom one will never meet, demands a different constancy.

The tradition at [[Heaven's Heart]] had always placed the vulnerable at the center of sacred obligation. Lavitz's later oath made that principle the enduring measure of his public life.

## Lord Marshal of Avalor

Within weeks of Cid's defeat, [[King Gradvum Frozenroar]] appointed Lavitz **Lord Marshal**. The office had stood vacant since its previous holder—the man who had first put his trust in the Misfits—was killed at Stanleyville. Accepting the appointment meant carrying forward a duty that had already cost someone Lavitz respected his life.

As commander of [[The Avalorian Forces#lion-s-bane|Lion's Bane]] and a member of [[The Aegis]], Lavitz could no longer answer every danger by drawing his sword. He now had to make decisions that would send other people into harm's way, weigh the king's needs against the lives of those who served, and trust his officers to act when he could not be beside them.

That last lesson was perhaps the hardest. Lavitz had survived because Fo'Gurn once stayed behind. His instinct was still to be the last man standing between danger and his friends. As Lord Marshal, he had to learn that protecting a kingdom also meant allowing others to shoulder duties he could not carry alone.

The title took something from him. Where an adventurer could follow the next road with his companions, the Lord Marshal was expected to remain, prepare, and answer for what happened even when he was far from the fighting.

## The companions who remained

The Misfits did not spend the next twenty years assembled around the same campfire. [[Heureux]] continued his travels and teaching, and [[Amara]] followed paths that carried her far from the royal court. Their absence did not mean the fellowship had ended.

Lavitz remained particularly close to **Tel'Lora** and **Sven**. Tel'Lora's appointment as High Wizard gave Avalor an eighth seat upon the Aegis and a learned counselor whose judgment Lavitz relied upon. Tel'Lora also revived [[Shamman’s Tower]], extending his work beyond the capital.

Sven's later work as the **Iron Shadow** drew him into a separate intelligence network, away from the public offices held by Lavitz and Tel'Lora. Its quiet operations were seldom discussed, but the friendship between the three men endured.

Their shared history matters more than the differences in their later offices. They had learned how to disagree, how to trust one another, and how to carry a friendship beyond the adventure that first forged it.

## A drow beneath the lion banner

Lavitz's elevation did not repeal centuries of prejudice. Under [[Arnaud's Edicts]], drow families had endured discriminatory laws, and although [[The Reforms of Queen Reina|Reina's reforms]] began reversing that injustice, the memory and consequences persisted.

Gradvum's decision to entrust the defense of Avalor to a drow was therefore public as well as personal. Children in Renada's poorer streets could now see a drow officer holding one of the kingdom's highest posts, where earlier generations had seen suspicion or exclusion.

That example matters. But Lavitz's worth is not a special exception that makes other drow deserving of respect only if they become heroes. The ordinary citizen deserves justice no less than the commander whose name appears in the chronicles.

For Lavitz, duty remains less about standing as a symbol than about ensuring the kingdom is worth protecting. The most meaningful measure of his service may be found not in the height of the citadel, but in whether the streets from which he came become safer for the children still living there.
