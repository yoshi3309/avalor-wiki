## The man behind the title

A traveler arriving in [[Renada]] may hear the name **Lavitz Shawe** before ever seeing the man. Some know him as the paladin who helped end the reign of [[Cid the Blood King]]. Others know him as **Lord Marshal**, the officer entrusted with advising the crown on the kingdom's defense. For children in the poorer quarters of the capital, his name means something less distant: one of their own grew up to stand among the realm's highest counselors.

Lavitz did not begin his life among generals or courtiers. He grew up in the [[Mudsteps]], the working neighborhood within the [[Rhys Quays]] district of Renada. The distance between those streets and the [[Great Citadel]] is not merely a matter of steep roads. It is a distance in wealth, influence, and the assumptions a kingdom makes about who is permitted to wield authority.

That Lavitz crossed it does not make the hardships of the Mudsteps disappear. It makes his appointment unusually meaningful.

## Appearance and bearing

Lavitz is a tall, powerfully built **drow**, often recognized by his white hair, bright blue eyes, and a visible facial scar. The bearing of a trained warrior is difficult for him to conceal, even when the occasion calls for a ceremonial coat rather than plate armor.

His official insignia identifies the Lord Marshal, but his reputation was made long before he wore it. Court dress and military ceremony have not turned him into a man inclined to mistake the appearance of authority for the practice of it.

To many Avalorians, the figure is immediately recognizable: a paladin who could have remained in the songs of an adventuring age but instead chose the daily burdens of protecting a realm.

## A childhood beside the river

The [[Rhys Quays]] has always depended upon hands that rarely appear in royal chronicles: those who unload vessels, maintain river trade, mend what breaks, and make a household's provisions last when work is scarce. Lavitz's childhood belonged to that world.

His family lived modestly, and his father's livelihood was connected to the docks. As drow residents of a kingdom still marked by centuries of inherited prejudice, they experienced the difference between what law promised and what neighbors or employers were willing to grant.

Later admirers have sometimes treated his rise as proof that any determined child could find the same path. Such a conclusion asks too little of the kingdom. Lavitz's achievement is extraordinary; it does not excuse the unequal conditions from which he emerged.

The intimate losses of his family belong to a more personal history than this public account can responsibly reconstruct. What the surviving civic record establishes is his origin in Renada's working quarter, not a tale in which every private grief must become public entertainment.

## The discipline of Heaven's Heart

Lavitz's road eventually brought him to [[Heaven's Heart]], beneath the [[Spine of the World]]. There he trained within the paladin tradition inherited from [[Lowkee]] and the [[The Cathedral Paladins|Cathedral Paladins]].

The order's teaching is closely connected to [[The Threefold Devotion]], honoring Tyr, Helm, and Torm. Its central obligation is not obedience for its own sake, but the defense of those who cannot stand safely without protection. The history of Heaven's Heart offers examples of that duty carried out even when the crown itself issued unjust commands.

For a young drow raised among people accustomed to the shortcomings of official justice, such a distinction was not an abstract exercise. A righteous law and a righteous act are not always identical. Power deserves respect when it serves those in its care, not simply because it demands to be obeyed.

At Heaven's Heart, Lavitz also formed an enduring friendship with a dragonborn paladin named [[fo-gurn|Fo’Gurn]]. The bond began before either man became part of the fellowship that would change Avalorian history.

## The companion before the legend

The group later known as [[The Misfits]] did not gather to defeat an ancient evil or rewrite the kingdom's history. Its members were brought together by a commission from the **preceding Lord Marshal**, who sought help investigating a necromancer.

Alongside Lavitz stood [[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]], the half-elven wizard; [[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]], the eladrin; [[Amara]], a druid whose family line reached back to the founding age; and Fo’Gurn. They overcame their first enemy and recovered fragments of magical research whose significance became clear only much later. The affair is preserved as [[The Misfits’ First Commission]].

A second commission, concerning [[The Cult of Gol’Goroth]], brought [[Heureux]] into their company. The firbolg bard became both a companion and, in time, one of the most important tellers of their shared history.

The fellowship endured because its members repeatedly chose to remain together. Their temperaments differed, and their missions did not always belong to one great conspiracy. They became friends through the less ceremonial work of surviving the road.

## Adventures on roads without a single destiny

The histories of the Misfits include the living halls of [[Moonville Manor]], the peculiar affair of [[The Ripped Beard]], and the transformation of a wizard named [[Noki]] into a sheep. The company also helped free the [[The Liberation of the Rotten Husk|Rotten Husk]] orc tribe from a dangerous chieftain.

These encounters are easily overshadowed by the later defeat of Cid. They should not be. People who received help during those journeys did not need an explanation of the Black Tower to know whether the adventurers had made a difference.

Other dangers followed, including [[Zauks and the False Lives|the illusions of Zauks]], the crisis at [[The Tournament of Champions]], and confrontations that tested the companions' trust as much as their strength. The public accounts preserve separate histories of these events precisely because not every enemy was secretly an instrument of the Blood King.

For Lavitz, the road offered something the office of Lord Marshal would eventually take from him: the freedom to respond immediately to a person in danger, to choose a course with his companions, and to shoulder the risk himself.

## Fo’Gurn and the cost of survival

Among the losses that shaped Lavitz, few were more personal than the stand remembered in [[Fo’Gurn and the Hydra]].

When the company faced a hydra, Fo’Gurn remained behind to buy his friends enough time to escape. They survived, believing the dragonborn had died. For Lavitz, this was not simply the loss of another adventurer. He had known Fo’Gurn before there were Misfits to speak of, and the memory of his friend's choice followed him long after the encounter itself.

Later discoveries involving the necromancer [[Jaaris]] complicated the company's understanding of Fo’Gurn's fate. They did not undo the years in which the surviving companions mourned him.

Lavitz's willingness to place himself between danger and others is among his defining virtues. It can also be a difficult habit for a leader: he would rather bear the risk himself than ask another to bear it. A Lord Marshal must learn that protecting a kingdom cannot depend upon a single person standing in every breach.

## Stanleyville, Jack, and the vacant office

The Misfits' investigation of mysterious deaths at [[Stanleyville]] brought them face to face with another kind of cruelty. They encountered [[Jack of Stanleyville]], a murdered child whose conscious spirit sought the truth of his own death. Rather than remain in the town, Jack traveled onward with the companions.

The affair also claimed the life of the Lord Marshal who had first summoned the adventurers. He was killed during the attack of [[The Stalker of Stanleyville|the Stalker]], a creature later connected to Cid.

The office was left vacant. At the time, Lavitz had no reason to know that he would one day be asked to fill it.

The tragedy belonged not only to the kingdom's military history, but to the lives of those who survived the town's ordeal. Jack was a traveling companion, not merely a clue; the preceding marshal had been a man who placed trust in people not yet recognized as heroes.

## Against the Blood King

The Misfits' journeys eventually led to the discovery of [[Jaznaar]], the founding hero long falsely condemned as the Betrayer. They learned that the celebrated healer **Aldus Greystone III** had endured after the [[The Sundering of the Six|Black Tower catastrophe]] as **Cid the Blood King**.

In the Shadowfell, the companions confronted and defeated Cid. The victory brought more than the end of a formidable enemy. It exposed the old lie that had stained Avalor's history for nearly two thousand years and made possible [[The Vindication of Jaznaar|Jaznaar's public vindication]].

Lavitz's part in that victory carried a particular weight. He was a drow helping restore the honor of a drow founding hero while serving a kingdom whose history included grievous injustice toward drow communities. The connection did not erase those injustices, but it made their continued defense increasingly difficult.

## From vengeance to devotion

Lavitz once followed the **Oath of Vengeance**. In the years after the defeat of Cid, his calling matured into the **Oath of Devotion**. The change is not best understood as the rejection of everything that once sustained him. It marks an expansion in whom he believes himself responsible for protecting.

Vengeance may demand that a particular wrong be answered. Devotion asks what must be preserved when the wrong has been answered and those who survive must still live together. To fight one enemy is a terrible burden; to remain steadfast for thousands of people, most of whom one will never meet, demands a different constancy.

The tradition at [[Heaven's Heart]] had always placed the vulnerable at the center of sacred obligation. Lavitz's later oath made that principle the enduring measure of his public life.

## Lord Marshal of Avalor

Within weeks of Cid's defeat, [[King Gradvum Frozenroar]] appointed Lavitz **Lord Marshal**. The office had stood empty since the death of its former holder at Stanleyville.

Lavitz accepted responsibility for the kingdom's defenses, senior military counsel, and command of the royal guard known as [[Lion's Bane]]. He also took his place on [[The Aegis]], the crown's eight-seat council, where military concerns must be weighed beside finance, law, faith, intelligence, and the other obligations of government.

The work differs sharply from adventuring. A paladin may meet a threat personally; a commander must plan for danger that may arrive months later, in places he has never visited. He must decide how soldiers are trained, whether defenses are ready, and how to advise a king when the easiest answer is not the wisest one.

The broader [[Avalorian Standing Army]] trains at sites including [[The Great Fork Tower]]. It is not identical to Lion's Bane, nor to [[Dina Hyanda]], the separate protective order established by Queen Melmiri. The distinction matters in understanding the offices Lavitz must coordinate without confusing their duties.

If appointment brought honor, it also demanded sacrifice. The man who had chosen his next road now held responsibilities that could not be set aside whenever distant companions called.

## The companions who remained

The Misfits did not spend the next twenty years assembled around the same campfire. [[Heureux]] continued his travels and teaching, and [[Amara]] followed paths that carried her far from the royal court. Their absence did not mean the fellowship had ended.

Lavitz remained particularly close to **Tel'Lora** and **Sven**. Tel'Lora's appointment as High Wizard gave Avalor an eighth seat upon the Aegis and a learned counselor whose judgment Lavitz relied upon. Tel'Lora also revived [[Shamman’s Tower]], extending his work beyond the capital.

Sven's later activities were less visible. Stories about the figure called the **Iron Shadow** belong partly to rumor; the public record does not certify the methods or membership of any supposed covert network. What it does preserve is his continuing friendship with Lavitz and Tel'Lora.

Their shared history matters more than the differences in their later offices. They had learned how to disagree, how to trust one another, and how to carry a friendship beyond the adventure that first forged it.

## A drow beneath the lion banner

Lavitz's elevation did not repeal centuries of prejudice. Under [[Arnaud's Edicts]], drow families had endured discriminatory laws, and although [[Reina's Reforms]] began reversing that injustice, the memory and consequences persisted.

Gradvum's decision to entrust the defense of Avalor to a drow was therefore public as well as personal. Children in Renada's poorer streets could now see a drow officer holding one of the kingdom's highest posts, where earlier generations had seen suspicion or exclusion.

That example matters. But Lavitz's worth is not a special exception that makes other drow deserving of respect only if they become heroes. The ordinary citizen deserves justice no less than the commander whose name appears in the chronicles.

For Lavitz, duty remains less about standing as a symbol than about ensuring the kingdom is worth protecting. The most meaningful measure of his service may be found not in the height of the citadel, but in whether the streets from which he came become safer for the children still living there.
