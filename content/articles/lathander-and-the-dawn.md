## A beginning worth honoring

Among Avalor's many religious traditions, the worship of **Lathander**, the Morninglord, places particular importance upon the promise of a new beginning. Those who honor him speak of renewal, restoration after suffering, and the courage required to begin again when an earlier hope has been lost.

Devotion need not be confined to a temple. The blessing of a new endeavor, a prayer before setting out upon a journey, or the care of a community recovering from disaster may all express these convictions. Some families observe such customs openly; others follow quieter practices inherited from those who came before them.

The Morninglord's worship takes many forms across Avalor. Temples, charitable communities, small congregations, and individual households observe the faith according to their own traditions.

## The First Loaf

A familiar custom in *some* communities devoted to the Morninglord is **the First Loaf**. At dawn, families or congregations able to spare food set aside bread or another morning provision for those who might otherwise begin the day hungry. The offering may pass through a temple, a neighbor, or a local work of charity.

The observance is deliberately practical. Its meaning lies not in the size of the gift but in the decision that the promise of a new day should extend to a person in need.

Scholars do not claim that all devotees practice the First Loaf, nor that its form is identical in every settlement. Neither is its origin securely attributed to any one historical figure.

## Petris and the work of compassion

[[Petris]], the elven cleric counted among [[The Six]], remains one of the founding age's best-known servants of Lathander. She tended the wounded and displaced during the struggles from which Avalor emerged, understanding that a victory announced by a king could not, by itself, heal the suffering left in its wake.

Petris's example has often been contrasted with that of Aldus Greystone III. Both possessed extraordinary healing gifts. Yet Petris's traditions emphasize caring for a life within its mortal limits, while Aldus came to believe those limits themselves must be overcome at terrible cost.

The contrast belongs to Avalor's history, not to a claim that every follower of Lathander agrees upon every question of healing or mortality.

## A faith without a single face

The kingdom's religious life is varied, and the Morninglord's worship is no exception. His followers may share communities with those devoted to the [[The Threefold Devotion|Threefold gods]], honor a family practice without belonging to an organized congregation, or carry acts of service into places where no formal shrine stands.

For a traveler, the most memorable sign of this faith may therefore be neither a grand building nor a procession. It may be the simple knowledge that someone has saved a place at the morning table.
