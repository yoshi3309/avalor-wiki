## The strength to stand for others

**Lowkee Nicklebane**, usually remembered simply as **Lowkee**, was a human paladin among [[The Six]], the companions whose friendship and service helped shape Avalor's founding age. Closely associated with **Rynhold Frozenroar**, he took part in a generation that had seen divided kingdoms threatened by conquest and had learned that victory alone could not guarantee a just peace.

Lowkee believed that strength carried obligations. A sword could enforce a decree, but the possession of a sword did not make the decree righteous. That distinction became central to his legacy.

## The first teaching

Lowkee established [[The Cathedral Paladins]] at [[Heaven's Heart]], near the [[Spine of the World]], in a tradition devoted to **Tyr, Helm, and Torm**. His most influential instruction was remembered as the **First Teaching**.

It called upon paladins to be guided by justice, guarded by vigilance, and steadied by duty—while placing their deepest oath with **those unable to stand without protection** rather than simply with kings or other powerful patrons.

The teaching did not make difficult decisions disappear. It made the moral burden of those decisions impossible to ignore. Generations of initiates learned that lawful commands could be unjust and that the people who most needed a defender were often those without the influence to obtain one.

## The sundering of the fellowship

The work of **Aldus Greystone III** eventually divided the Six. Aldus had begun as a gifted healer, and his earliest investigations into mortality were not universally condemned. When his experiments passed into cruelty, Lowkee came to believe that his former companion had become a danger that must be stopped.

[[Faelynn]] increasingly shared this judgment. [[Petris]] and [[Jaznaar]] still hoped that Aldus might be saved, while [[Kelsalor]] struggled to hold the companions together. Their confrontation at the [[Black Tower]] ended the experiments but did not prevent the catastrophe that followed.

To present the Six as a fellowship united in every judgment would erase the difficulty of their final choice. Lowkee acted from conviction, not from the certainty that every friend agreed with him.

## The sword that outlived him

Lowkee once carried the sword later remembered as **Cid's Bane**. Long after the founding age, his fellow hero [[jaznaar|Jaznaar Mizzrorzza]] entrusted the blade to **[[Lavitz Shawe]]** as the Misfits prepared to confront Cid in the Shadowfell.

The sword's passage from one paladin to another carried a meaning greater than its steel. Lowkee had taught that strength was owed first to those who needed protection. Nearly two thousand years later, the man chosen to wield his sword was a drow whose childhood had been shaped by the kingdom's failure to live up to that teaching.

## The Keeper who followed his memory

The Misfits' search for knowledge concerning **Lowkee's Keeper** brought them to **[[Anna, Lowkee's Keeper|Anna]]**, a tavern waitress who became their noncombatant traveling companion. She was associated with the **[[Emerald of Armanti]]**, a jewel remembered in the history of the final struggle with the Blood King.

The title *Keeper* has invited speculation about an office, a bloodline, or a formal guardianship stretching back through the centuries. The reliable public accounts do not define such an institution. They record the search, the woman who accompanied the Misfits, and a jewel whose name was attached to the hope of drawing upon the old paladin's aid.

Lowkee's legacy therefore reached the company in more than one form. His sword **Cid's Bane** came to Lavitz through Jaznaar. The emerald belonged to another history: it was borne by Anna and appeared during the last struggle. Neither relic proves that Lowkee returned permanently to mortal life.

## A promise tested after his lifetime

During the later persecution of drow families under Arnaud Frozenroar, people sought sanctuary at Heaven's Heart. The order's leader **Jeyse**, a deva, refused to surrender innocents merely because the crown had declared their ancestry suspect. **Cal'el**, an aasimar associated with the gods of the Threefold Devotion, publicly defended that refusal.

The confrontation between sanctuary and royal authority made Lowkee's instruction a living question rather than a revered phrase. The Cathedral Paladins did not all agree about the risks, but the families remained under their protection.

Lowkee's enduring contribution was therefore more than the founding of an order. He left Avalor a measure by which the use of power itself could be judged.
