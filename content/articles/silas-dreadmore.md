## A man who could sell a story

**Silas Dreadmore** was clever long before he was anything approaching formidable. He learned early that a convincing lie might earn him a better bed, a smaller punishment, or a heavier purse. Honesty rarely seemed to bring such immediate rewards. As a young man he became a wanderer and swindler, passing between settlements on charm, invention, and the willingness of strangers to trust a face they had only just met.

Profit was not always the point. Silas sometimes lied simply to learn whether another person would believe him. It was a dangerous pastime for someone who had started to mistake escaping consequences for being beyond their reach.

He brought that confidence into the **[[Dead Marshes]]**, a place whose inhabitants knew better than to bargain lightly with the lights moving through the fog. They warned travelers against wandering lanterns, witches wearing borrowed faces, and cottages that never seemed to stand where they had the night before.

Silas listened long enough to hear of a hut supposedly filled with enchanted valuables. He ignored the rest.

## The widow in the walking cottage

Deeper in the marsh, Silas found a cottage raised upon twisted roots. Its occupant appeared to be a beautiful, silver-haired widow with patient eyes. She offered him food and wine, allowed him to sit beside her fire, and listened while he invented the name and purpose of the traveler he pretended to be.

The woman was **Mother Vey**, a swamp hag of the **Sisters of the Night Garden**, who served a power known as the **Den Mother** of the marshes. She understood the man before her better than he understood his host.

That night Silas tried to rob her. While searching through the cottage he laid his hand upon an old broom made from **black thornwood**, hung with charms of animal bone. The cottage fell silent.

Mother Vey cast aside the widow's appearance. Rather than kill the thief, she offered him his life, magical power, and knowledge of paths hidden among the Fey. The price was not coin. **Silas would belong to her**, and when she called for him he would have to answer.

She snapped the handle from her broom and threw the piece before him.

*“Every liar deserves a proper tongue.”*

Silas took the wood and accepted. He had bargained for survival, but he had placed his own future in the hag's hands.

## A bargain that changed the body

The pact did more than change what Silas could do. Beneath the moon his skin paled to blue, his ears split into crooked points, and thornlike growths rose from his head like tangled briars. He was becoming a **Hexblood**, carrying the visible signs of the magic that had spared his life.

There was another cost. The change had not finished. If the curse fully matured, Silas faced the prospect of becoming a hag himself.

His cleverness had always helped him escape obligations. This time he had accepted one he could not readily talk his way out of. Mother Vey did not have to accompany him for her claim to travel with him.

## Charm Twister

Silas fashioned the broken broom handle into a bow and named it **Charm Twister**. Something of Mother Vey's magic remained in the black wood. When its arrows struck, they hissed cruel insults and whispered truths made savage by supernatural malice.

It was a fitting weapon for a man who had spent so much of his life turning words into tools. Silas now carried a weapon that could make language as merciless as a sharpened point.

The bow was not the only reminder. Sleep brought dreams of a cottage striding through the marsh upon enormous roots. In those dreams Silas heard branches snapping, wet earth dragging, and laughter somewhere beyond the fog. At times he woke certain that Mother Vey's home was still looking for him.

Whether every dream foretold a real pursuit or reflected the terror of his bargain, it kept the hag close to the life he tried to build away from her.

## Work among the cursed and forgotten

In the years that followed, Silas became known in the less reputable corners of central Avalor. He sought out cursed relics others feared to handle, dealt openly with Fey beings, and accepted dangerous hunts when the reward justified the risk.

Stories about his deeds were seldom consistent. One village told of children he rescued after they disappeared in the woods. Another account claimed he had bargained away years of a dying man's life to learn where they had gone. Both stories followed him, and neither supplied a simple answer to what kind of person Silas had become.

People learned to be careful around him. Yet when a curse surfaced or a creature came out of the dark, there were those who quietly looked for Silas Dreadmore. A man who knew the ways of dangerous magic could be useful even to people who knew better than to trust him.

## The marsh and the marshal's letter

Word of that reputation eventually reached **[[Lavitz Shawe]]**, the Lord Marshal of Avalor. The reports spoke of a man who understood curses, survived improbable encounters, and could pass through dangerous country without leaving much sign of his passage.

One rainy evening Silas returned to his campsite to find a sealed letter beside the fire. There were no tracks to show who had delivered it. The wax bore the crown of Renada.

Its author offered gold, pardons, and opportunities in exchange for service requiring uncommon discretion. Silas read it three times, searching for what the message might conceal. Then he laughed.

He had once bargained with a hag to escape death. Now the kingdom itself had need of the talents that bargain had given him. He knew better than most that a promise of freedom could conceal an obligation.

Nevertheless, Silas accepted a place among the operatives called upon by the Lord Marshal for the undertaking associated with **Blackwater Bay**.

Whether he regarded the summons as an opportunity, an escape, or another gamble was less important than the fact that he answered it. A man who had made a living negotiating the terms of every encounter was once again preparing to make a bargain.
