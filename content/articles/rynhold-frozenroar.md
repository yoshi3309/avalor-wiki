## Before the first lion banner

**Rynhold Frozenroar** is remembered as the first king of Avalor, but he did not begin his reign as the sovereign of a united realm. Before the founding, he wore the crown of **Kroubia**, one of several independent kingdoms whose rival rulers guarded their borders, traditions, and interests.

Later generations liked to imagine Rynhold already dreaming of a united north. In Kroubia, however, his first responsibilities were those of a king with a smaller realm to govern: roads, trade, defense, and the quarrels of neighboring rulers.

Kroubia was not simply an earlier name for Avalor. Its former borders remain disputed, and later cartographers sometimes mistakenly projected the boundaries of the united kingdom backward into Rynhold's earlier reign.

The distinction matters. Rynhold inherited one kingdom; he helped create another.

## The conqueror who made division dangerous

The mortal commander **Lord Osmo** pursued dominion over neighboring realms through military force and political calculation. He was neither a supernatural tyrant nor the instrument of an established hidden power. His threat was the threat of a determined conqueror exploiting the rivalries of smaller kingdoms.

One ruler might refuse to aid a neighboring army for fear of empowering an old enemy. Another might bargain separately with Osmo in the hope of preserving some portion of independence. Such decisions could make sense to the individual courts that took them even as they weakened the region as a whole.

Rynhold could not simply command these rulers to join him. They were sovereigns in their own right. To assemble resistance, he had to persuade them that cooperation offered a future that none could secure alone.

No single speech brought the crowns together. It took repeated persuasion and a growing fear of what Osmo might achieve while the kingdoms remained divided.

## The gathering of crowns

The coalition against Osmo emerged through agreements whose precise sequence is no longer known. They may have included passage for allied troops, shared scouts, supply arrangements, and promises to defend threatened settlements. Those examples are historical reconstructions of the period's practical needs, not the terms of a surviving treaty.

What distinguished Rynhold's leadership was an understanding that resistance would fail if every ruler measured success solely by the security of his own borders.

Later traditions portray him as inclined toward negotiation where it could accomplish what military force might win only at grave cost. That preference should not be mistaken for an unwillingness to fight. He worked to unite armies against a conqueror who had made peaceful coexistence increasingly difficult.

His achievement was political before it was royal: he brought people accustomed to rivalry toward a common purpose without first possessing the right to command them.

## The price of Heroes Hill

The campaign against Osmo included marches, skirmishes, threatened crossings, displaced families, and years of uncertainty that later songs rarely record in full. Its decisive moment came at [[Heroes Hill]].

There **[[Bradgem Glav]]** reached Osmo and killed him, suffering mortal wounds in the act. The oldest accounts agree upon that sacrifice, though they differ concerning the maneuvers and magic that made it possible. Bradgem's memorial, marked by a rearing horse, remains one of the founding age's most enduring monuments.

The victory belonged to a coalition, not to Rynhold alone. Nor did the death of the conqueror return every displaced family to a home or erase the injuries war left across the land. The [[Dead Marshes]] already held dangers before Osmo's defeat; the founding wars contributed to a history of magical injury whose exact causes remain contested.

Avalor's founding traditions remember the battle as an ending. For those tasked with caring for the wounded and rebuilding settlements, it was also the beginning of more difficult work.

## From an alliance to a kingdom

After Osmo's defeat, the allied crowns had no automatic obligation to recognize Rynhold as their sovereign. They remained rulers with laws, territories, ambitions, and subjects of their own.

The founding required a **second achievement**: negotiation of a lasting political union. Rynhold worked to turn temporary military cooperation into an arrangement through which formerly independent realms could share defenses and resolve obligations without surrendering every local tradition.

The complete provisions of the founding agreements have not survived. Historians nevertheless distinguish the process from the forcible annexation of all neighboring realms.

In **1 After Founding (AF)**, the [[The Kingdom of Avalor|Kingdom of Avalor]] was established with Rynhold as its first sovereign. The Avalorian calendar takes its beginning from this settlement. The first year must not automatically be treated as the independently verified date of Osmo's death at Heroes Hill.

The founding is described in greater detail in [[The Unification of Avalor]].

## A capital that already existed

Rynhold selected **[[Renada]]** as the capital of the new kingdom. The city was already an inhabited settlement with its own earlier history; its selection did not create its people, markets, or river trade from nothing.

The choice carried political significance. A capital at Renada represented something more than the enlargement of Kroubia's former court. It became the administrative center of a realm with multiple former crowns and distinct communities.

The [[Great Citadel]] was first constructed during Rynhold's founding reign. Later kings expanded and altered it, but its earliest royal purpose dates to the period when Avalor's governing institutions were taking shape.

Over time, Renada grew into a city of courtly ceremony and working districts. The history of its people cannot be reduced to the king who designated it a capital, even as that designation transformed its future.

## The Aegis and the problem of permanence

A kingdom held together only by admiration for one ruler is a fragile kingdom. In the years after unification, laws, defenses, trade obligations, and competing local rights required attention that no individual could provide alone.

Rynhold founded the council known as **[[The Aegis]]**. Its original seven offices addressed responsibilities such as military defense, intelligence, public order, maritime affairs, finance, and sacred duty. The exact procedures of the earliest council are not fully reconstructed in surviving sources.

The institution's importance was that it **could outlast its founder**. Rynhold's reign ended, but the council remained part of the political structure through which later sovereigns governed.

In the Age of Ascendancy, the appointment of [[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]] as High Wizard created an independent eighth seat. This later development should not be projected backward into Rynhold's original seven-member arrangement.

The endurance of the Aegis expresses one of the founding reign's central achievements: unity needed institutions, not merely victories.

## The founding heroes and a king's friendships

Rynhold's age is inseparable from the people whose actions made the kingdom possible. Bradgem Glav gave his life ending the threat of Osmo. [[Lowkee]], a human paladin among [[The Six]], was particularly close to Rynhold and later founded the Cathedral Paladins at [[Heaven's Heart]].

[[Jaznaar]], the drow spellcaster of the Six, was also close to the founding king. That friendship would acquire tragic significance in the generations when Jaznaar was falsely accused of betraying his companions.

The circumstances in which the Six first gathered remain debated. Some traditions credit Rynhold with recognizing the value of champions able to act beyond normal military structures. Others describe an independent fellowship developing from smaller groups whose paths crossed during the founding wars. Neither view is established as the sole authenticated account.

It would be misleading to portray every member of the Six as a royal servant simply because their deeds helped secure Rynhold's kingdom. Their friendships, loyalties, and disagreements belonged to them as well as to Avalor's history.

## What he knew, and what later generations forgot

The Black Tower catastrophe occurred approximately a decade after the founding. Aldus Greystone III's experiments had become destructive, and the remaining members of the Six confronted him.

Rynhold **knew Jaznaar had not betrayed the fellowship**, although he did not know that Aldus would endure in the Shadowfell as Cid the Blood King. The later accusation against Jaznaar was not the genuine judgment of the founding king and surviving eyewitness companions.

The false legend took hold in later generations, as direct testimony receded and Cid's agents encouraged a story in which Aldus was the innocent savior and Jaznaar the saboteur.

That distinction matters to Rynhold's biography. It would be inaccurate to make him the author of a lie that later harmed drow communities. It would be equally inaccurate to suggest that one king's knowledge automatically protected the kingdom from the manipulation of history after his lifetime.

The correction of Jaznaar's reputation would come nearly two thousand years later, under [[King Gradvum Frozenroar]].

## The first reign and the royal succession

Rynhold ruled Avalor from **1 to 37 AF**, according to the [[House Frozenroar#the-royal-register|Royal Register]]. His name begins the sequence of sovereigns that extends through thirty-four reigns to the present king.

**Benedikt Frozenroar** succeeded him, beginning his reign in 38 AF. The surviving authenticated pedigree does not establish that Benedikt was Rynhold's son. A succession in the register does not, by itself, prove a parent-child relationship.

Rynhold's personal family story has faded behind the kingdom he founded. His reign, his friendships, and the institutions he built have proved more enduring than any surviving account of his household.

The chronology of his reign is clear; the private family story behind it is not.

## The father of Avalor—and its limits

Rynhold is often called **the father of Avalor**. The title honors the achievement of bringing competing crowns into a durable union and founding institutions capable of preserving it.

It does not mean that Avalor's people began their histories with him. Renada had existed before it became a capital. Communities, ancestral territories, and regional customs preceded the new kingdom and continued within it.

Nor does the success of the union mean that all later sovereigns fulfilled its promise. House Frozenroar would govern through years of achievement, conflict, injustice, reform, and renewed historical reckoning.

Rynhold's lasting accomplishment was not that he eliminated disagreement. It was that he gave former rivals a political structure in which a shared future became possible. Two thousand years of changing history have tested that foundation, but have not erased the first king's place within it.
