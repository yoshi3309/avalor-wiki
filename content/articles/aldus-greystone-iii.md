## The healer before the Blood King

For much of Avalor's history, **Aldus Greystone III** was celebrated as one of the kingdom's greatest founding heroes. His name was associated with healing, scholarship, and the hope that suffering might one day be overcome. The discoveries made after the defeat of [[Cid the Blood King]] transformed that reputation, but they did not erase the man who existed before the catastrophe.

Aldus possessed remarkable gifts in restorative magic and an unusual command of the forces of weather. Accounts of his early work describe a healer sought by the sick and injured, one whose abilities gave hope to people beyond the reach of ordinary remedies. His reputation was earned long before the crimes for which he would later be remembered.

## A student of the Silver Forge

Aldus spent time at **[[The Guilds of Avalor#the-clerics-guild|the Silver Forge]]**, the fellowship of clerics founded alongside the united kingdom of Avalor. There he learned more of the healing arts that had already made him admired by the sick and the injured.

He **returned to the guild when his desire to prevent death had become a quest to remove mortality itself**, seeking more knowledge from the institution where many faiths and traditions of healing met.

That return would acquire a darker meaning in hindsight. The Silver Forge taught and gathered healers; it did not share responsibility for the cruelty Aldus later justified in the name of an impossible perfect world.

## A companion of the Six

Aldus belonged to [[The Six]], the fellowship whose service became closely associated with Avalor's founding. Alongside [[Lowkee]], [[Faelynn]], [[Petris]], [[Kelsalor]], and [[Jaznaar]], he traveled, faced danger, and helped shape the young kingdom's conception of heroism.

Their friendship was not an incidental detail of his life. Aldus's companions knew him as a man who wanted to preserve life; his earliest investigations into the limits of healing were neither secret nor initially understood as a threat. In those years, they had reason to believe his learning might benefit the realm.

The later tragedy must not be used to make his friends accomplices in experiments whose true character they had not yet discovered.

## When preservation became defiance

Aldus came to regard death not simply as a sorrow to be resisted when possible, but as an injustice that ought to be abolished. His studies moved from improving the healing arts toward the much greater ambition of ending mortality itself.

No surviving account identifies the moment when that ambition became cruelty. The change emerged gradually. Aldus's later work passed beyond accepted healing and involved the suffering of others, giving rise to the arcane corruption known as [[The Blood Sickness]]. Though often described in popular retellings as a plague, its nature was magical; its precise symptoms and workings remain incompletely understood.

What once appeared to be compassion became an obsession powerful enough to excuse terrible means. The man who sought to spare others from death began inflicting harm in the belief that his intended future justified it.

## The fracture of a fellowship

As evidence of Aldus's experiments emerged, his five companions confronted a question that their earlier adventures had never demanded of them: could their friend still be saved, or had he become a danger that must be destroyed?

Lowkee concluded that Aldus had to be stopped even if that meant killing him. Faelynn increasingly shared that judgment. Petris and Jaznaar hoped to reach the man they had known, while Kelsalor sought to prevent the fellowship from breaking altogether.

They disagreed about the possibility of redemption, not about whether the suffering was real. The division culminated in [[The Sundering of the Six]].

## The catastrophe at the Black Tower

Approximately a decade after the founding, Aldus attempted a ritual at the [[The Black Tower|Black Tower]], within the [[Spine of the World]]. It concerned the boundary between the mortal world and the Shadowfell and sought to alter the conditions of life and death on a scale far beyond the fate of one person.

His five former companions intervened. The ritual was interrupted and Aldus's mortal body was slain. Historical accounts disagree about the precise sequence of the confrontation and do not reliably identify a single final blow. The survivors departed believing that their friend's death had ended the threat.

The catastrophe nevertheless left wounds that endured. Aldus's work was bound to the gradual transformation of northern lands into the [[Great Desert]] and to dangerous magical disturbances elsewhere, including the [[Dead Marshes]]. The full relationship among these consequences remains a subject of historical inquiry.

## The man who endured as Cid

Aldus's mortal death was not the end of his existence. He endured in the Shadowfell as **Cid the Blood King**. These were not two unrelated enemies: the celebrated founding healer and the being feared by later generations were the same individual.

Jaznaar eventually discovered the truth and undertook the extraordinary burden of restraining Cid and containing much of the Blood Sickness. For nearly two thousand years that protection remained largely unknown to the people who benefited from it.

Meanwhile, agents of Cid helped construct and perpetuate a false history. In the story that came to dominate Avalor's records, Aldus was a tragic hero whose effort to save the kingdom had been sabotaged by Jaznaar. The innocent guardian was condemned as the **Betrayer**, while the author of the catastrophe retained his place of honor.

That falsehood became more than a dispute among historians. It influenced how generations understood their founders and helped sustain suspicion and injustice toward drow communities.

## Defeat and historical reckoning

The [[The Misfits|Misfits]] ultimately uncovered the connection between Aldus and Cid through their encounters and the testimony of Jaznaar. Their final confrontation with the Blood King took place in the Shadowfell, where they defeated him. An immense black hand drew Cid into an unknown darkness. His enemies had been saved from him, but none of those present could say what awaited him beyond it.

In the aftermath, King Gradvum Frozenroar formally vindicated Jaznaar. The correction also required Avalor to reconsider Aldus: neither the kindness of his earliest deeds nor the fame of his name could absolve the harm of his later choices.

## A name that remains

Aldus's memory has not simply been erased from the kingdom. The [[The Great Library of Aldus Greystone]] at [[Heaven's Heart]] retains his name, even as its scholars preserve the evidence that overturned his reputation. In [[Rynhold Park]], his statue still stands, but its former celebratory inscription was removed and preserved in the Library. A new plaque identifies him with Cid and acknowledges the wrong done to Jaznaar.

The decision remains contested. Some would remove his likeness; others believe a corrected monument is a stronger warning than an empty pedestal.

Aldus Greystone III is therefore remembered neither as a hero whose later crimes can be overlooked nor as a monster who was never human. His life poses a more difficult question: how far can the desire to save others be corrupted when its bearer refuses to accept any limit upon his power?
