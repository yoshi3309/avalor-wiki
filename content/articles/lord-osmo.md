## Before there was Avalor

The northern kingdoms did not unite because their rulers suddenly discovered that old quarrels were foolish. Their agreement grew from a danger they could no longer meet separately.

That danger had a name: **Lord Osmo**.

For generations, accounts of Avalor's founding have naturally celebrated [[Rynhold Frozenroar]] and the allied rulers who resisted him. Osmo appears more often as their defeated enemy than as a man whose actions shaped the choices before them. Yet without his campaigns, the circumstances that produced the kingdom might have been very different.

Osmo was **mortal**. No established public history makes him a wizard, fiend, avatar, or servant of a concealed supernatural power. His ambition was sufficiently dangerous without such an explanation.

## An empire made of victories

Before [[The Unification of Avalor|Avalor's unification]], the north was divided among independent kingdoms. Their boundaries shifted, their courts disputed old claims, and their rulers defended obligations to their own people. **Kroubia**, under Rynhold, was one of these kingdoms; it was not yet the kingdom that would later bear the lion banner.

Osmo sought dominion over neighboring realms. He advanced through **military force and political calculation**, using the divisions among his opponents to make each victory easier than it might otherwise have been.

A ruler might withhold soldiers rather than rescue an old enemy. Another might bargain separately with Osmo in the hope of preserving land or a degree of independence. These decisions did not require cowardice; they reflected the distrust that comes naturally to courts accustomed to rivalry.

The result was dangerous for everyone. Each kingdom could defend a border. Fewer could withstand a conqueror whose reach grew with every rival brought under his control.

## Roads, garrisons, and the weight of occupation

The conquest of a territory is only the beginning of holding it. Garrisons must be supplied, officers must communicate, routes must be kept open, and resistance must be watched for.

The surviving **[[Lord Osmo’s Tower]]** is associated with that martial system. Its presence reminds travelers that Osmo's dominion depended not upon one legendary battle but upon the steady machinery of command.

His campaigns altered ordinary life. Trade routes became military concerns; border fortifications grew in importance; settlements feared passing armies and the loss of provisions upon which survival depended.

Later songs often ask who struck the decisive blow. For the people living between the armies, the more urgent question was how long they could remain in their homes.

## The king who would not submit

Osmo's growth eventually forced **Rynhold Frozenroar of Kroubia** to seek common cause with the other northern sovereigns. Rynhold did not possess the authority to command them; he needed to persuade them that their shared survival mattered more than their old disputes.

The resistance grew through arrangements about defense, movement of forces, scouts, supplies, and political trust. Their exact terms have not survived. Nor can one famous speech or meeting be identified as the moment in which all the crowns suddenly agreed.

Rynhold's challenge was not only to defeat Osmo, but to demonstrate that cooperation against the conqueror need not become submission to another king.

## The wars that the songs shortened

The **[[The Founding Wars|Founding Wars]]** were more than a confrontation between two commanding figures. They involved marches, smaller battles, threatened crossings, displaced people, and communities that supplied armies at considerable cost.

Accounts speak of families carrying provisions on dangerous roads and villages sheltering refugees. Some traditions tell of crops harvested after dark while defenders kept watch. Such stories cannot always be attached to a particular place or date, but they preserve the experience of people absent from the grander military histories.

Osmo's campaigns helped teach the independent realms what their separation could cost. They also left wounds that would not heal simply because an army stopped marching.

## The death at Heroes Hill

The struggle reached its decisive battle at **[[Heroes Hill]]**.

There **[[Bradgem Glav]]** reached Osmo and killed him. Bradgem suffered mortal wounds in the act. The earliest surviving accounts agree on those essential facts, although later traditions disagree about the maneuvers, counterattacks, and possible magical interventions that enabled the final encounter.

Osmo's death ended the immediate expansion of his dominion. It was also the costly victory from which Avalor's founding story came to be told.

Bradgem's memorial bears a rearing horse. No equivalent certainty surrounds Osmo's final words, private reflections, or the disposition of every force that had served him. Their absence need not be repaired through invention.

## The kingdom he never ruled

Osmo's defeat did not make Rynhold the ruler of all the north overnight. The victorious kings still possessed independent realms, subjects, and interests. Having united against a common enemy, they had to decide whether the alliance could survive the return of peace.

Rynhold's negotiations eventually produced **[[The Unification of Avalor|the settlement of 1 AF]]**, the year by which the Avalorian calendar measures the kingdom's founding. The precise year of Osmo's death is not independently fixed by that date.

The difference is fundamental. **Osmo was defeated by an alliance. Avalor was created through a political union.** One achievement made the other possible, but neither was inevitable.

The conqueror who sought to bend many crowns to his own will became, in defeat, the danger those crowns learned to answer together.

## The weight of a mortal enemy

Later legends attach strange powers to old towers and old enemies. Such stories may continue around Osmo's surviving stronghold, but they should not overwhelm what his life actually demonstrates.

A man with armies, the skill to exploit division, and the determination to force obedience can change a continent's history. Osmo's threat was not less serious because it was human.

His death at Heroes Hill made another future possible. Rynhold and the allied kingdoms chose how to use it. That choice—not Osmo's will—became Avalor.
