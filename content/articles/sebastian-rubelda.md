## The queen's brother

**Sebastian Rubelda** is a **human** and the brother of [[Queen Melmiri]]. He holds the crown-appointed office of **sheriff of the Northern March**, governing from [[Land's End]].

His place in the royal family's story is not merely ceremonial. He shares a family connection with the Northern Desert Tribes and has his own ideas about the relationship between those communities and the kingdom his sister helps govern.

Sebastian is plainspoken and favors practical experience over elegant promises. He has a particular appreciation for **Avalorian customs**: the habits of public service, shared obligations, and cooperation that can give people from different communities a reason to stand together.

His respect for Avalor does not mean he rejects the traditions of the desert tribes. He hopes those traditions can endure without being trapped in perpetual rivalries.

## Tired of old quarrels

Sebastian has grown weary of **infighting among the Northern Desert Tribes**. He wants to help their communities find a greater measure of unity rather than continuing to spend strength upon divisions among themselves.

He does not regard that goal as permission to make himself ruler of the tribes. Nor does his sister's position mean that he speaks for every community with which their family has ties. The Northern Desert Tribes are not one household or one political body, and cooperation cannot be imposed by treating them as though they are.

For Sebastian, Avalorian customs offer a model of how different peoples might agree upon shared responsibilities without ceasing to be distinct. Whether the tribes would embrace that vision is a question for the communities themselves.

## The frontier is not the court

At Land's End, Sebastian's daily work is not accomplished through inspiring speeches. Garrisons need supplies, distant settlements need passable routes, and warnings must reach those who can act upon them.

He values preparedness and tends to trust what has been done over what has been promised. That steadiness makes him useful in dangerous country, but it can also become a weakness: he sometimes expects others to endure hardship as readily as he does.

His family connection does not make the Northern March his hereditary possession. Sebastian holds his office through the crown, subject to the same political limits as the other appointed sheriffs.

## A hope larger than his office

The responsibilities of the Northern March are described in [[The Four Sheriffdoms#northern-march-sebastian-rubelda|the regional account]]. Sebastian's biography turns on something more personal: a man close to the royal household who admires the idea of a united kingdom and wants the people of his family's desert heritage to find a way beyond old divisions.

He is a steadfast guardian, but his ambition is not simply to keep the frontier secure. It is to see more of the people beyond it choose cooperation while they still have the freedom to do so.
