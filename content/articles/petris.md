## The hero who stayed

**Petris** was an elven cleric devoted to **Lathander**, the Morninglord, and a member of [[The Six]]. In a history often told through battles, towers, and royal deeds, she is remembered especially for the people who survived because someone chose to care for them.

The struggle against Lord Osmo and the establishment of Avalor left wounded soldiers, displaced families, grieving households, and settlements in need of rebuilding. The kingdom's founding could not become a lasting peace through military victory alone.

Petris's service addressed the suffering that remained.

## The promise of another morning

The worship of Lathander honors renewal, beginnings, hope, and the possibility of restoration after hardship. Petris brought those convictions into the practical work of feeding, healing, sheltering, and consoling people who had little to offer in return.

Some Avalorian communities maintain a dawn custom called the **First Loaf**, setting aside food for a neighbor who might otherwise go hungry. The First Loaf was a custom shared by communities devoted to Lathander, a practice that echoed Petris's work even though its origins were older or more widely shared than her own story.

Her legacy does, however, accord with the duty the custom expresses.

## The disagreement with Aldus

Petris and **Aldus Greystone III** shared gifts and interests associated with healing. Both understood what the loss of life meant to those left behind. Their responses to mortality eventually diverged.

As Aldus's experiments brought suffering in the name of overcoming death, Petris still hoped that something of the friend they had known could be reached. [[Jaznaar]] shared that hope more strongly than [[Lowkee]] and [[Faelynn]], who came to believe the danger could no longer be permitted to continue. [[Kelsalor]] tried to mediate.

Her hope for Aldus did not excuse his cruelty. She was struggling to find a way to save the man she had known without allowing him to hurt anyone else. The division of the Six lay partly in their different answers to a terrible question: whether they could stop the harm without destroying the man who had caused it.

## A peaceful ending and a distant descendant

Petris is recorded as having **died peacefully**, leaving a legacy of service carried through her descendants. Nearly two thousand years later, [[Amara]], one of [[The Misfits]], would belong to that family line.

The two women lived in different ages and faced different dangers. Their connection nonetheless reminds the reader that the founding generation left inheritances beyond royal decrees and monuments.

Petris's importance is measured not by how loudly her name was spoken in halls of power, but by the belief that lives made vulnerable by war were still worthy of care.
