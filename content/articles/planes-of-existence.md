## The world is not alone

The land of [[The Kingdom of Avalor|Avalor]] is one part of the **Material Plane**, but the Material Plane is not the whole of existence. Other realities hold their own landscapes, inhabitants, and histories. Some can be described using the words for mortal forests, seas, and settlements. Others resist such comparisons.

The word **plane** does not mean a distant continent. A traveler may cross a thousand leagues without moving closer to the Feywild and then encounter a passage that reaches it in a single step.

The Material Plane appears at the center of many Avalorian diagrams because Avalorians draw them. Scholars have no reason to assume that every other world would arrange creation around Avalor.

## Roads that are not roads

Known passages arise through spells, rituals, enchanted objects, unusual circumstances, and gateways that do not appear to depend on deliberate spellcasting.

Some remain accessible across generations; others vanish after one use. A passage may carry a traveler away without offering an equivalent route home. Crossing into a realm also offers no guarantee that its conditions, inhabitants, or passage of time will resemble those of the Material Plane.

These uncertainties are not arguments that travel is impossible. They are reasons that responsible travelers plan for more than arrival.

## The Three Questions before a crossing

At **[[Shamman’s Tower]]**, students are taught **the Three Questions**: *How do I enter? What conditions govern my stay? How do I return?*

The exercise makes a crucial distinction between gaining access to another world and knowing how to survive there and come back.

Some crossings require invitations or rituals; others depend on circumstances that may change without warning. The traveler who relies upon a route must distinguish what has been tested from what is merely hoped.

## The Feywild and the forest at home

The **Feywild** appears throughout Avalorian storytelling, sometimes described through heightened landscapes, elusive distances, and extraordinary natural features. But it is a world in its own right, not simply a decorative reflection of the Material Plane.

The eladrin **[[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]]** came from the Feywild before he wandered the **[[Forest of the Fey]]**. His unusual history illustrates the difference between a people’s ancestral relationships with another realm and any assumption that all such people share the same experience.

The Forest of the Fey is an **Avalorian forest on the Material Plane**. Its settlements, claims, and agreements with the crown have their own history. It is not interchangeable with the Feywild merely because travelers sometimes tell stories of strange crossings in its depths.

## The Shadowfell and the Blood King

The **Shadowfell** is often depicted as bleak and unfamiliar, yet it too possesses its own inhabitants and history. No account of a single evil resident can describe an entire realm.

That resident nevertheless changed Avalor. **[[Aldus Greystone III]]** attempted an extraordinary working at the **[[The Black Tower|Black Tower]]** involving the relationship between the Material Plane and the Shadowfell. His former companions opposed him. The catastrophe left magical wounds whose consequences included the transformation of northern lands.

Aldus's mortal defeat did not end his existence. He endured in the Shadowfell as **[[Cid the Blood King]]** until the **[[The Misfits|Misfits]]** followed him into that realm to rescue Queen Melmiri and bring an end to his immediate threat.

Cid's crimes belonged to Cid. They do not make every being of the Shadowfell his accomplice.

## Astral, Ethereal, and the elemental realms

The **Astral Plane** occupies an important place in accounts of journeys and relationships that cannot be explained solely through distance. The **Ethereal Plane** is studied in connection with proximity, perception, and conditions of existence that may not obey ordinary spatial separation.

**Elemental realms** appear in traditions concerned with fundamental forces and substances: fire, air, earth, water, and the altered conditions under which they may behave.

**Celestial realms** are associated with divine beings and their servants. **Infernal and abyssal realms**, including accounts of the Nine Hells and the Abyss, appear in records of bargains, conflicts, and dangerous powers.

These names organize meaningful testimony without proving that every realm follows one shared geography or divine hierarchy.

## Diagrams that are not the world

Scholars use several competing ways to represent the planes. **Concentric diagrams** compare realities by perceived similarity; **networks** show recorded passages; **threshold models** explain conditions required to cross.

Each answers a different question. None has been established as the complete architecture of existence.

A diagram that omits a realm is not necessarily dishonest. It becomes misleading when readers forget that the model is incomplete.

## Journeys with a way home

At Shamman’s Tower, the **Atlas of Returns** distinguishes passages confirmed by travel in both directions from single journeys, incomplete accounts, and testimony whose reliability is still being studied.

Outside the Tower, some families observe **the Returning Thread**: tying a thread beside the household entrance when a traveler departs, then untying it when the traveler returns. The custom makes no claim to magical power. It gives a household a small act of hope while waiting for someone beyond familiar roads.

Those who never return may be dead, trapped, or simply unwilling or unable to find the crossing again. Their absence alone cannot decide which.

## The difference between a door and a disaster

A portal is not necessarily a wound in the world. It can permit movement while preserving the distinction between separate realities.

Aldus's experiment threatened something else: the conditions that kept different worlds from being forced into a destructive relationship. The harm lay not in traveling to another plane but in attempting to transform the boundaries themselves.

Fragments concerning **Aethrion** have sometimes been compared to the Black Tower catastrophe. The resemblance is a question of dangerous ambition; it does not establish a single confirmed history of both events.

## The Beyond and other unanswered questions

The name **the Beyond** appears in scattered cosmological references, but scholars disagree about what it designates. Some use the term for the unknown; others suggest something unlike the realms ordinarily described as planes.

No reliable public account establishes that it is a plane, the darkness between them, or a destination past the last mapped reality.

The gods likewise have diverse associations with their own realms. No single heavenly residence or universal final destination for souls has been demonstrated.

Other worlds are not blank possessions awaiting Avalorian discovery. Their inhabitants have claims, customs, and histories of their own. To cross into a realm is to enter another world—not to acquire it.
