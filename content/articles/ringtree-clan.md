## An unseen people in an inhabited forest

Among the communities of the **[[Forest of the Fey]]** is the **Ringtree Clan**, a firbolg people whose history cannot be reconstructed simply by following the roads of kings.

The Ringtree lived among concealed settlements high within the forest canopy. Their communities were **semi-nomadic**, changing location when their circumstances demanded, and sheltered themselves through **illusion magic and invisibility**. This secrecy protected their families and the traditions they held precious.

To travelers unfamiliar with the forest, a stretch of apparently empty branches might appear uninhabited. The people who knew it could instead remember generations of stories, arguments, celebrations, and obligations tied to that place.

The Ringtree were not all the firbolgs of Avalor. Their way of life was a distinctive cultural inheritance, not an obligation imposed by ancestry.

## Her in the living world

The Ringtree understood life through a spiritual current they called **[[The Primordial Powers#her-and-the-work-of-becoming|Her]]**.

To them, Her touched more than living plants or creatures. **Animals, music, invention, memory, growth, and decay** all participated in the work of change. A tree growing from the soil and a song changing as it passed between generations were not unrelated wonders.

This outlook made preservation an active duty rather than an attempt to stop time. The past survives through the efforts of living people, who must decide what to remember, how to teach it, and what to do when inherited knowledge proves incomplete.

The Ringtree's devotion belongs within Avalor's wider and varied religious life. No source establishes that every firbolg honors Her or practices the clan's customs.

## A library without walls

History was sacred to the Ringtree. Their scholars preserved **written records and oral accounts** across generations, safeguarding knowledge of the clan's origins and practices.

The most respected historian in the community was the father of **[[Heureux]]**, known to his later companions simply as **H**. His work was not the private hobby of a learned man: the ability to recall a community's past was part of the knowledge by which the community understood itself.

When Heureux came of age, he began studying under his father with the expectation that he would learn those responsibilities. He developed a gift for retaining stories through **song and rhythm**, making the work of a historian accessible to listeners who might never consult a manuscript.

The Ringtree therefore held two kinds of repository. Some accounts rested in guarded books. Others traveled in the memory of the people who had learned them.

## The Yesteryear Tome

Heureux's father eventually revealed to him the **Yesteryear Tome**, a hidden archive holding truths concerning the clan's origins and customs that had long been suppressed.

The discovery unsettled what H believed he knew about his people. The histories he had been trained to preserve remained valuable, but they were not the entirety of what his clan had once known. Someone had made choices about which facts were allowed to survive in ordinary memory.

H began setting the discoveries into **lyrics** before dawn. He committed them to memory in a form that could travel beyond the guarded shelves.

This was not a claim that a song and a written record are interchangeable. It was a recognition that if one form were destroyed, another might still preserve what mattered.

## The burning of the library

That same night, the **Ringtree library burned**. H's father disappeared within the flames, and the young historian was pursued through the forest by agents loyal to the clan chief.

The chief, according to H's later account, had been manipulated by forces connected to **[[Cid the Blood King]]** and **mind flayers**. That connection is established by the creator's account. The identities of particular agents, the exact means of manipulation, and the ultimate fate of H's father have not been specified.

H escaped. He believed he had failed to save his father and the knowledge entrusted to their family. That guilt would continue to shape him long after he became a widely loved performer.

The burning was a catastrophe for the clan's historical memory. Yet the destruction of one library did not mean that every Ringtree story, book, or witness had vanished. The future of its surviving communities is not fully established by H's exile.

## The memory that survived

H's verses survived with him. In the years that followed he wandered the roads and taverns of Avalor as a **folklorist and bard**, using songs, blues-inspired ballads, satire, and communal storytelling to carry historical knowledge into ordinary life.

He played dulcimer, drums, and shawm. His performances were warm and often funny, but behind their humor lay a determination that the destruction of one institution must never again be enough to erase a people.

Heureux later joined **[[The Misfits]]**, participated in the discovery that Avalor's official history had been manipulated, and founded **[[Heureux’s Bard Academy]]** in **Taveras** after the defeat of Cid.

His school is not described as a formal branch of the Ringtree Clan. Its founding nonetheless reflects an ideal he first learned there: **history must be taught in ways that allow it to survive the person who teaches it**.

## What remains of the clan

The Ringtree should not be reduced to the destruction of its library, nor should H's life be mistaken for a complete account of every member's experience.

Their exact present population, leadership after the fire, wider family relationships, and custody of surviving texts have not been established. A careful history preserves those uncertainties rather than inventing a restored chief or a new ceremonial council.

What is known is enough to distinguish them: a firbolg people who lived hidden beneath the forest canopy, revered Her through the changes of the world, and guarded generations of written and spoken memory.

From that tradition came a bard who refused to let an important truth remain trapped inside a book.

**The Ringtree story did not end when its library burned.**
