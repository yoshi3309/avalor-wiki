## The crown changes course

**Reina Frozenroar** succeeded Arnaud and reigned from **1316 to 1402 AF**. The kingdom she inherited still carried the damage caused by royal decrees that had singled out drow families for suspicion, searches, and displacement.

Reina **curtailed further restrictions and sought to restore equal treatment**. Her actions were not contingent upon the later discovery that the ancient charge against Jaznaar was false. The monarchy could recognize the injustice of ancestry-based laws without yet knowing the full history of the Black Tower.

## What repeal could not restore

A royal decree can be withdrawn more quickly than its consequences. The earlier measures had affected trade, apprenticeships, households, and the trust between neighbors.

The surviving sources do not establish a precise repeal date for each restriction or verify that every family recovered lost property. To describe Reina's reign as the moment all discrimination ended would give legal reform a completeness it did not achieve.

Some officials had resisted Arnaud's decrees even while they remained law; others had profited by enforcing them. Their different actions and the records they left shaped the difficulties facing those who sought redress.

## Centuries before vindication

The distinction in the chronology is essential. Reina reigned in the **fourteenth century AF**. [[jaznaars-vindication|Jaznaar’s public vindication]] followed the Misfits' defeat of Cid much later, under King Gradvum.

Reina's reforms therefore **preceded** the restoration of Jaznaar's historical reputation by centuries.

The false legend continued circulating after restrictions were curtailed, even as the law changed. Later generations would have to confront not only the old policies but the story used to legitimize them.

## A measure of service

Reina's legacy demonstrates that reform can begin before every historical mystery has been solved. The government need not possess perfect knowledge of the past to stop treating innocent people unjustly in the present.

The changes mattered. They were also incomplete, leaving later sovereigns and communities a responsibility that was not discharged simply by repealing harmful rules.
