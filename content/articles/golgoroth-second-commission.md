## A second summons

After the defeat of the unnamed necromancer, **[[kel-maris|Kel'Maris]]**, the Lion's Shadow, again called upon the companions. The second formal commission concerned a cult devoted to **Gol'Goroth**, a threat whose nature was dangerous enough to draw the crown's attention but whose precise rites have not been preserved in a complete public register.

The company entered this inquiry with a little more shared experience than it had carried into the first commission. It was still far from the royal legend that later historians would make of it. To those endangered by the cult, this mission was not a prelude to the Blood King's story; it was the crisis immediately before them.

## The bard among the adventurers

The lasting change to the fellowship during this investigation was the arrival of **[[Heureux]]**, a blue-furred firbolg bard of the [[Ringtree Clan]]. He came with songs, curiosity, convictions, and injuries of his own. His connection to the ancient founder Kelsalor would eventually be recognized, but he did not join as the appointed representative of an ancestral line.

He joined as a person whose presence altered the company. His songs and later testimony would become important to how the kingdom remembered all of them. He was not a member of the original five who entered the earliest known adventures, yet he became one of the five most publicly celebrated survivors after Cid's defeat.

Heureux's story began well beyond the Lord Marshal's service. The burning of the Ringtree library and the disappearance of his father formed part of the grief he carried. A history that treats him only as the party's bard misses the weight of what he chose to bring to others.

## Danger without a hidden master

The surviving record does not establish that Cid designed the Gol'Goroth commission, nor that the cult existed solely to direct the companions toward him. Later histories have sometimes sought a single hand behind every separate danger the Misfits encountered.

For those who suffered under the cult, such a theory is not necessary to explain why the adventurers' intervention mattered. The company faced a threat and continued together. It was a fuller fellowship when the inquiry ended.

## The next unexpected encounter

A wizard named Noki would soon provide a stranger test of the group's willingness to take an improbable account seriously. The sheep incident became a subject of laughter and, in later years, of more disturbing recognition.

The second commission is remembered principally for its mission against the cult and for the companion it added to the road. It marks the point at which the company most familiar to later Avalor began to take shape.
