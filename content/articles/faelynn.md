## Beneath the east-facing roofs

Long before the kingdom knew her as **the Night Mistress**, **Faelynn Naïlo** was a free-spirited wood elf roaming the high branches of the [[Forest of the Fey]]. She was a gifted archer and tracker, more at home among leaves and birdsong than in any crowded hall. From the canopy she hunted game, explored the forest's secrets, and reported what she discovered to the chieftess of her village.

The village itself was built among the tallest trees, its homes turned toward the east to catch the warmth of the rising sun. Faelynn was not simply an archer hired to guard its outskirts. She was the chieftess's trusted scout, a friend whose judgment had helped preserve the village through many ordinary dangers. That friendship would make the night's betrayal nearly unbearable. Below lay a forest so thick that daylight sometimes struggled to reach its floor. For the villagers, the morning light offered more than beauty; it meant another day beneath the protections that kept the darker wilderness at bay.

Faelynn enjoyed the chieftess's complete confidence. She was a trusted scout, an honest friend, and one of the first people the village would turn to when trouble stirred beyond its borders. That trust was the very thing an enemy would use to destroy them.

## The boar and the gate

One evening Faelynn followed an enormous boar through miles of forest. She tracked it for hours, waiting until it approached a cave she took for its den. Within, the temperature climbed, the air became oppressive, and an urge she did not understand drew her deeper. She expected to corner it in a cave. Instead she found the air turning oppressively hot and, deeper inside, a swirling portal. Beyond it stood the crooked wrought-iron gate of a city whose twisted shapes seemed wrong to the eye. She had never seen a passage between worlds, and she was so transfixed that the boar nearly vanished from her thoughts.

The boar passed toward the gate and changed. Its body twisted into something monstrous. When the thing turned back toward Faelynn, she fled, running through the night until she reached the familiar treetops of home.

Terrified and exhausted, she went straight to the chieftess. She needed to know whether the village's wards would hold. Her friend patiently explained the protective ritual she performed before sunrise each day. She even invited Faelynn to join her in the morning if it would help calm her fears.

Then the fiend's magic took hold.

Faelynn's eyes became black voids. Tears ran down her face as, against her will, she drove an arrow into the chieftess's throat. Under the same control she traced infernal signs in blood and spoke the words that broke the village's wards. Faelynn could watch herself acting, could weep and remember, but could not stop her hands. The creature had not persuaded her to betray the village: it had made her a prisoner of her own body.

Fiends and beasts poured through the defenseless settlement. Huts crashed from the branches, and the screams carried through the forest. The creature that had worn the boar's shape returned to thank Faelynn for opening the way, calmly telling her that its spell would release her when the sun rose. Its courtesy was another cruelty. It spoke to her like a useful servant while the people who had trusted her died outside. It left the door open so she could watch.

Faelynn could see the destruction. She could grieve. She could not stop it.

## The first sunrise

At dawn the spell finally broke. Faelynn collapsed, then crawled outside and called for anyone who might have survived. No voice answered.

When she rose again, grief gave way to a vow: she would find the creature responsible and make it answer for what it had done. She completed the funeral pyres and rites herself, because the clerics who might have guided her were among the dead. Only after the last villager had been laid to rest could she leave. She buried and burned what remained of her people, performing the rites as best she could without the village's slain clerics.

Then she left the home she had once defended.

For years Faelynn lived by her bow, taking monster-hunting work in small settlements while following whatever trace might lead to the fiend. She avoided the great cities and learned how dangerous it was to trust an easy explanation. Her pursuit eventually taught her enough about portals to cross the boundary she had once fled. During those years she kept away from large cities, took monster-hunting work in small settlements, and discovered people whose losses were no less real because kings and soldiers had never heard their names. Her hunt became both an education in the ways of monsters and an encounter with the survivors they left behind.

She returned to her village's ruins for one last sunrise and opened a passage to the place beyond the crooked iron gate. There she hunted the creatures that had taken her people. At last she found the boar-fiend itself.

This time she recognized its attempt to command her mind. She allowed the creature to believe its old trick had worked, waiting until it approached close enough to speak. The hunter who had once fled this being knew precisely when to strike. She drove an arrow into its throat and held its jaws shut as it fell. The monster that had used her hands to murder her friend died by those same hands, now governed by her own will.

**Faelynn's hunt ended before she joined the fellowship later known as [[The Six]].** The victory fulfilled her vow, but it could not bring back the village, the chieftess, or the mornings of her youth.

## A friend among the Six

Faelynn's long hunt was behind her when she became one of Avalor's founding fellowship. She joined [[Aldus Greystone III]], [[Lowkee|Lowkee]], [[Petris]], [[Kelsalor]], and [[Jaznaar]] in the company remembered as the Six.

In later years, when Aldus's experiments at the [[Black Tower]] were revealed to be causing suffering, Faelynn leaned toward Lowkee's judgment that their friend must be stopped, even if stopping him meant his death. Petris and Jaznaar still hoped to save him, while Kelsalor tried to hold the fellowship together.

Faelynn knew what it was to love someone who had become the instrument of another's cruelty. She also knew that waiting too long to stop a danger could destroy everyone placed in its path. Her own past did not make the choice easier. It made its cost impossible to ignore.

The Night Mistress is remembered for her skill as a ranger and for the order she would found. Yet her story began with a young woman watching for sunrise above a forest village, and it remained a story about what she would do so that others might see another dawn.

## The Wayward Sun

After her years among the founding heroes, Faelynn continued wandering through Avalor. In the swamp country of the [[Dead Marshes]], she came upon an abandoned shelter and made it a base for helping travelers and communities threatened by dangerous creatures.

She called the place **[[Wayward Sun]]**, remembering the east-facing homes of her childhood and the sunrise that had finally freed her from the fiend's control. The name carried grief, but also the possibility that morning might come again.

Others eventually joined her cause, many bearing grief like the grief that had first driven her from home. They were not all united by blood or temperament. Some had lost family; some were seeking justice; others had discovered that the desire for vengeance could swallow everything left of a life. Faelynn offered them a home, skills, and a purpose that could outlast any single enemy. They called the work **the family business**, knowing that its members would argue and disagree as families do, but would stand beside one another when darkness came.

Together they established **[[The Hunters Guild]]**. As their methods grew to include hidden signs and the study of uncanny threats, Faelynn adopted the name **Night Mistress**. The guild's later customs—silver coins, guarded recognition phrases, and the answer 'East. Toward the sunrise'—grew from the same memory of the east-facing treetops and the night she could not save them.

The guild endured long after the founding years. Its hunters still serve under [[Malikeah]], though the generations they protect know more of the dangers before them than of the terrible night that first shaped Faelynn's purpose.

## The legacy of the eastward road

The hunters who inherited Faelynn's purpose sometimes traveled openly as trappers and sometimes worked behind an assumed trade. They learned to recognize one another by the silver they accepted, by signs left discreetly along a route, and by a question that remembered their founder's flight toward the dawn.

Their crest came to bear a **pentagram enclosed by an Ouroboros**: a reminder of the fiends they opposed and the recurring passage of life into death. Not every hunter would know Faelynn's exact words, but they knew where the family's oldest refuge stood. Even in the present age, hunters enter the Dead Marshes to honor the Night Mistress at the [[Wayward Sun]] and remember that their work began not with glory, but with grief and the decision to protect others.
