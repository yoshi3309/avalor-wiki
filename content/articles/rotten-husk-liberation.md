## A people behind an adventurer's tale

The Misfits' journey after Stanleyville brought them to the **[[The Rotten Husk Clan|Rotten Husk]]**, an orc community whose chieftain had become a danger to those he claimed to lead.

In later adventure stories, the affair has sometimes been told as a quick confrontation with a brutal opponent. Such accounts may provide the outline of a fight, but they tell little about the people who had lived under the chief's rule. The clan was not itself the villain of the story.

The surviving record identifies the community and the danger posed by its leader. It does not provide a full map of the clan's territory or a complete account of its political customs. Those absences should not be filled with imagined law, ceremonies, or grievances.

## The choice to intervene

The Misfits had already encountered threats of a very different scale. They had mourned Kel'Maris, the Lord Marshal killed in Stanleyville, **before** facing the ruined temple used in Cid's designs and losing Fo'Gurn to a hydra.

Yet the Rotten Husk's suffering was not a distraction that only became meaningful when tied to the Blood King. The people needed help. The companions chose to give it.

They **helped remove the chieftain's oppressive rule**, acting against the person whose authority had become destructive rather than treating his entire community as an enemy. The clan gained freedom and retained its own identity.

The records agree upon that central outcome even where the precise manner of the confrontation is incompletely preserved.

## Friendship without ownership

The community's goodwill toward the adventurers was earned through protection, not the claim that the Misfits had become the tribe's rulers. The company did not annex the clan on behalf of the crown or turn it into a convenient army for a later war.

Histories of famous warriors often make the people they assist into faceless witnesses to heroism. The Rotten Husk deserve more. They were the people for whom the danger mattered every day; they were the people who had to decide how to live afterward.

Their subsequent leadership and internal history have not been fully preserved. **The fact of liberation is not a charter transferring ownership of their future to the adventurers.**

## What the road taught

The affair belongs in the fellowship's historical sequence because it illustrates the kind of courage that has little use for royal spectacle. Faced with an oppressed community, the Misfits helped the people who needed their strength.

Their later work against Cid would change Avalor's understanding of its founders. This earlier act changed the immediate possibilities for one clan. Both are part of the kingdom's history.

In a long campaign of ancient relics, royal captivity and terrible magic, the Rotten Husk episode remains a reminder that protecting people need not be justified by any grander prophecy.
