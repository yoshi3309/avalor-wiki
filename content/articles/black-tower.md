## The mountain that bears a warning

The **Black Tower** stands in the [[Spine of the World]], where its name became a warning to travelers and students of magic. For generations, the crown restricted entry. Dangerous approaches and accounts of disappearances helped reinforce the prohibition without requiring a permanent army at its gates.

Its reputation is not merely architectural. The tower is remembered as the place where one of Avalor's most admired healers crossed boundaries his companions could no longer accept.

## The Black Stair

**The Black Stair** is the steep mountain ascent leading **to the Black Tower** through the [[Spine of the World]]. Its difficult climb, severe stone approaches, and reputation among travelers have made it part of the tower's warning long before one reaches the site itself.

The sanctuary of [[Heaven's Heart]] lies nearby in the same mountainous region, but **the Black Stair is not the road to that sanctuary**. It leads toward Aldus's tower. Confusing the two places misrepresents their distinct histories: one is known for paladin training and scholarship; the other for forbidden experiments, long-standing restrictions, and the catastrophe that divided the Six.

The royal prohibition upon access to the tower made the stair's destination particularly ominous. No surviving inscription names the stair's ancient builder. Its place in history comes from leading travelers toward the Black Tower itself.

Centuries later, **[[Lavitz Shawe]]** kept a **ninety-day vigil at a tomb near the stair's foot** during his paladin training. His vigil did not change where the stairs led, and the sanctuary of Heaven's Heart remained a separate place nearby.

## The work of Aldus Greystone III

**Aldus Greystone III**, one of [[The Six]], was once celebrated for his healing gifts. His earliest investigations into the preservation of life were known to his friends. Later, his determination to overcome mortality led to forbidden experimentation and the suffering of others.

At the Black Tower, Aldus attempted a ritual touching the boundary between the Material Plane and the Shadowfell. His former companions traveled to stop it, though they disagreed about whether he could still be saved. The ritual was interrupted and Aldus's mortal body slain. The consequences did not end with that victory.

The **Blood Sickness** arose from Aldus's work. Lands later called the [[Great Desert]] changed over time, while the already wounded [[Dead Marshes]] suffered further magical injury. The tower's history therefore belongs to the geography of Avalor as well as to the tragedy of its founding heroes.

## The history that was overturned

For centuries, a false account portrayed [[Jaznaar]] as the betrayer responsible for the catastrophe. The discoveries made possible by the defeat of [[Cid the Blood King]] established that Aldus himself had caused it and that Jaznaar had been unjustly condemned.

This correction altered the meaning of nearly every popular story attached to the tower. Ancient books and monuments were no longer beyond question merely because generations had trusted them.

## A closed place and an open inquiry

Scholars now distinguish **restricting access** to an unsafe place from **restricting the examination** of its history. The danger that justified caution need not justify the preservation of falsehood.

The Black Tower remains a place of warning, but its truest lesson may be that even the most revered accounts require evidence.
