## The beacons of the estuary

**Naeris Thorne** was born among low, tidal reaches where rivers emptied into the sea. His people lived beside drowned reeds, blackwater estuaries, mangrove roots, and the lonely lantern towers that watched the shoals. They were custodians rather than conquerors of the water: guardians of channels that shifted with the moon and storms that could rise with little warning.

The **Thorne enclave** maintained old beacons along that coast. Their squat stone foundations were so ancient that no living sea elf claimed kinship with their builders. The lanterns burned **blue-white through rain and gale**, warning ships away from dangerous approaches. Vessels that ignored their lights sometimes vanished into the shallows without returning so much as wreckage.

Children of the enclave learned the movements of tides and currents early. They were taught that the sea was neither cruel nor merciful; it was ancient, powerful, and indifferent to the hopes of those who crossed it. The river mouths, where fresh and salt waters met, were held to possess a memory of their own.

Naeris was not unusually rebellious or ambitious. What set him apart was a curiosity that carried him past the routes and weather his elders expected him to study.

## The things the sea remembered

While other young people learned the safest channels, Naeris wandered among tidal ruins. He followed half-collapsed stairways beneath the surface, cleared barnacles from old statues to read their inscriptions, and brought fragments of ruined instruments, amphorae, and worn devotional coins into the light.

His interest lay less in death than in the people who had lived before him. He wanted to understand what they made, what they worshipped, and what they feared so deeply that they hid it beneath stone and water.

That curiosity was both his calling and a danger. The sea did not preserve old places for the convenience of explorers. A ruin could give way beneath a diver as readily as it surrendered a forgotten inscription.

## The collapse beneath Vey'thal

During a solitary descent near the **delta caverns of Vey'thal**, Naeris entered a submerged ruin. A stone arch collapsed and trapped him beneath the surface.

In his later recollection, the moment came in fragments: stone groaning under pressure, black silt blooming through the water, and the sudden loss of light. He was pinned beneath the fallen stone, struggling for breath as the sea pressed around him.

Then he saw another figure beside him.

**It was himself.**

The second Naeris was no reflection. It moved calmly through the dark and laid a hand upon the shattered stone as if the weight were nothing. Water and pale vapor turned around it in strange ribbons. Naeris reached toward the figure.

He remembered nothing more until he awoke upon the shore hours later.

No witness could explain how he survived. Some elders dismissed his account as a vision caused by suffocation or terror. Others suggested that forgotten ruins might shelter spirits of their own. Naeris himself did not claim to know.

## The other self

After the collapse, the figure returned.

At first Naeris glimpsed it only in reflected water and storm mist. Later it appeared near torchlight, then at last stepped fully into the world. The manifestation was a second image of Naeris shaped from pressure, condensed vapor, and sea spray, though its nature could not be reduced to water alone.

It did not answer to ordinary spellcraft or a familiar ritual. It appeared through movement, memory, and the force of Naeris's will: an echo of the man, yet also something like a possibility standing one breath beside reality.

Others called Naeris an **Echo Knight**. He had little interest in the title, or in claiming that it explained the phenomenon.

The figure had saved him—or appeared when he was saved—at a moment no one else could account for. That was the event he knew. The reason for it remained unsettled.

## A listener among ruins

In time Naeris left the tidal enclaves and became a **maritime archaeologist, salvage operative, and ruin-diver** known for patient work amid drowned temples, ship graveyards, old river kingdoms, and battered coastlines.

He catalogued what history had left behind before scavengers or the ambitions of powerful states could strip a place of its story. His tools were those of a practiced diver and researcher, but he could also fight with **spear and shield**, aided by the unsettling echo that had followed him since Vey'thal.

Naeris approached the world less as a conquering warrior than as a listener. He attended to stone, debris, current, and the faint traces that remained when living testimony had disappeared. In his experience, ruins were never truly silent.

His unusual abilities and knowledge of hazardous waters made him a valuable addition to the operatives **[[Lavitz Shawe]]** recruited for the undertaking associated with **Blackwater Bay**. For Naeris, the summons offered another journey into places where the past had not finished speaking.

Stone remembered the weight placed upon it. Water bore the traces of what had passed through it. And Naeris had devoted his life to hearing what both might still reveal.
