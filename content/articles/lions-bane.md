## The royal guard

**Lion's Bane** is a royal guard serving the crown of Avalor under the command of [[Lavitz Shawe]], the kingdom's **Lord Marshal**.

Its name is sometimes encountered in the same account as the professional standing army, the court's protective institutions, and the celebrated career of its commander. These belong to related but distinct responsibilities. A royal guard cannot be treated as a synonym for every soldier who serves under the lion banner.

## Under the Lord Marshal

Lavitz's office combines military counsel with direct responsibility for Lion's Bane. As a member of [[The Aegis]], he advises the crown on the defense of the realm, while the guard has its own institutional role in royal protection.

Lavitz is best known for the deeds of [[The Misfits]] and their victory over Cid the Blood King. The work of command, however, is not simply an extension of a heroic adventure. It asks for preparation, discipline, and decisions whose consequences may be borne by ordinary soldiers and civilians.

Lion's Bane's public record does not establish an exhaustive officer roster, a fixed numerical strength, or the complete arrangements of its watches.

## Not the entire standing army

Avalor maintains a professional army, supplemented by regional military formations, garrisons, and local militias. The ancient [[Great Fork Tower]] is now a major training base for the kingdom's armed forces.

Those troops require coordination with royal and regional authority, but their entire service should not be described as membership in Lion's Bane. The army and the guard fulfill complementary obligations rather than identical ones.

## The separate order of Dina Hyanda

**Dina Hyanda** is a distinct royal protective order established by **Queen Melmiri**. It is not another name for Lion's Bane, and its origin does not lie in Lavitz's appointment.

Their duties can bring them into some of the same ceremonial spaces or matters of royal security. The distinction remains important: the kingdom has more than one protective tradition and more than one history of service.

## Protection before spectacle

A royal guard is easily described through ceremonial presence or the reputation of a famous commander. Its deeper significance lies in preventing danger and remaining capable when celebration has passed.

The best measure of Lion's Bane is not the number of songs written about Lavitz, but the reliability of those entrusted with the protection of others.
