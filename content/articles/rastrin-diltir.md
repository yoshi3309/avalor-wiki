## Keeping the realm at peace

**Rastrin Diltir** serves as the **Warden of Order** on [[The Aegis]]. This office concerns the maintenance of **internal order** within Avalor, a realm containing cities, ports, rural communities, and settlements governed under longstanding agreements.

The responsibility is substantial. Public order is necessary for people to trade, travel, and live securely. Yet an institution entrusted with preserving order must also recognize that quiet streets do not always signify justice.

## Authority with limits

Avalor is a centralized monarchy, but its laws and royal decisions operate alongside inherited rights and practical constraints. The crown's power is considerable; its use still requires judgment.

The Warden of Order's concern is different from that of the [[Katiri Tenderfoot|Silver Hand]], which gathers official intelligence. Nor is the Warden the military commander of all troops within the kingdom. Royal, regional, civic, and military functions must work together without being confused for one another.

## The shadow of earlier laws

The persecution of drow communities under Arnaud Frozenroar shows how damaging it can be when power is exercised through laws that treat identity as guilt. The eventual reforms and the vindication of [[Jaznaar]] required the kingdom to confront that legacy.

An official of the present age inherits a government whose authority once enabled such injustices. Recognizing this history does not determine every contemporary policy, but it makes blind faith in a legal command harder to defend.

## The Aegis and disagreement

The Warden of Order may approach a proposed measure differently from the Golden Hand or the Devoted. A policy might appear necessary for immediate safety while placing an unacceptable burden upon civil rights or public trust.

The record does not establish Rastrin's personal political philosophy, a complete body of policing law, or every subordinate office answering to the Warden. Those details are left unresolved.

What is clear is the place of the Warden within the council: to speak for internal security while participating in the wider obligation to govern a realm rather than merely control it.
