## A patient guardian of order

**Rastrin Diltir** is an **aging tortle** who serves on the Aegis as **Warden of Order**. He approaches questions of law and public safety with a measured, methodical temperament.

Rastrin trusts established procedures, not because he believes every old rule is good, but because decisions made too quickly can harm people who have no way to make themselves heard. He values consistency and worries about authorities who use urgency as an excuse to disregard fairness.

That caution brings its own difficulty. Sometimes a compassionate decision seems to require an exception, and Rastrin knows a rule can be both useful and imperfect.

## The paladin he admires

Among his fellow councilors, Rastrin has particular respect for **[[Lavitz Shawe]]**, the drow paladin who became Lord Marshal after the Misfits defeated Cid.

Rastrin admires the clarity of Lavitz's commitment: when another person is in danger, a paladin may step forward and offer protection with his own hands. Part of Rastrin wishes he possessed the skills to dedicate himself to the same cause.

But he considers himself **too old to begin that path**. He fears that the strain of training and battle would make him a burden to the very companions he hoped to help. It is a sincere regret, though it rests on his own judgment of what he can still accomplish rather than on a formal refusal from a paladin order.

There is already a close connection between his family and the paladin's work: **Rastrin's nephew Quill**, a fellow **tortle**, commands the **[[The Avalorian Forces#the-royal-guard|Royal Guard]]** division of Lion's Bane beneath Lavitz. Where Rastrin serves through law and restraint, Quill serves through the immediate protection of the crown.

Rastrin is not a trained paladin, and his admiration does not mean he intends to leave the Aegis.

## Another way to protect

There is an irony in Rastrin's regret: the work he already performs can protect people who will never know his name. Fair procedures and restraint in the exercise of authority may prevent harm before a sword is needed.

He knows that order is not automatically justice. Avalor's past under [[Arnaud's Edicts]] shows how faithfully obeyed laws can still be profoundly wrong. That history gives his preference for rules a necessary counterweight.

The broader duties of his office are explained in [[The Aegis#intelligence-and-public-order|the council's record]]. Rastrin's own story is of a tortle who admires a warrior's calling, but must learn to recognize the value of the protection he already offers.
