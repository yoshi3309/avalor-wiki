## The city beneath the lion

**Renada**, capital of [[The Kingdom of Avalor|Avalor]], stands upon the River Rhys, where the everyday labor of a river city meets the obligations of the lion throne. The [[Great Citadel]] looks down from the heights, while markets, workshops, landings, and ordinary homes fill the streets below. The wider city is estimated at **100,000–200,000 inhabitants**, depending upon the inclusion of outlying settlements.

It is tempting to judge the capital by its monuments alone. Yet the vessels unloading grain, families tending workshops, officials recording weights, and people seeking charity or justice are as important to Renada's life as those who govern from the citadel.

## Older than the crown

Renada was inhabited **before the founding of Avalor**. [[Rynhold Frozenroar]] chose it as the new kingdom's capital rather than merely enlarging the established royal seat of his former kingdom of Kroubia. His decision expressed the making of a political union, not the discovery of an empty city.

The [[Great Citadel]] was first raised during Rynhold's reign and has since been altered by successive rulers. Royal functions brought work and opportunity to the older settlement, but also pressures and changes whose effects did not fall equally upon all inhabitants.

## The River Rhys and the city's crossings

The **River Rhys** joins the capital to the movement of inland goods and routes toward the coast. Two bridges illustrate the different tasks performed by a shared waterway. The **Great Stone Bridge** is familiar from royal ceremonies as well as daily journeys; **Millers' Bridge** is known especially to grain carriers and riverside workers.

The water connects the prosperity of the capital to people whose names seldom appear in an official register. Along the quays, boats, carts, inns, ropewalks, and warehouses turn river traffic into the work of thousands.

## Five districts, many lives

Renada's five major districts offer distinct views of one city: **Old Renada**, **Crownrise**, **Rhys Quays**, **Lantern Ward**, and **Cathedral Ward**. The **Mudsteps** is a neighborhood **within Rhys Quays**, not a sixth major district or an independent town. **Rynhold Park** is a civic space, not a separate settlement.

The districts are connected by roads, bridges, households, markets, and institutions. None can explain the entire capital alone.

## Old Renada

**Old Renada** preserves a city older than its royal status. Irregular streets, established workshops, family houses, and small courts recall a settlement whose life was already underway before the union of northern kingdoms.

These buildings have not all survived unchanged from the founding age. The district's history is one of gradual rebuilding and adaptation, not a perfectly preserved ancient quarter.

Its streets descend from [[Renada#crownrise|Crownrise]], linking the ceremonial center to a neighborhood arranged more by habitation and long practice than the needs of royal processions. The older households gained opportunities when Renada became capital, though their own experiences of that transformation are less fully recorded than the crown's.

## Crownrise

**Crownrise** occupies the heights leading to the [[Great Citadel]]. Royal offices, affluent residences, and ceremonial approaches lend the district a character distinct from the riverfront below.

From its higher streets, the city may appear orderly: the River Rhys marks its passage, the Great Stone Bridge carries travelers, and roads lead toward the business of the court. At ground level those routes are crowded with merchants, petitioners, workers, and people whose lives have little connection with royal ceremony.

The contrast between Crownrise and the Rhys Quays is economic as well as topographical. Proximity to authority has often meant greater opportunity, while families nearer the water have lived more directly with the uncertainty of daily labor.

## Rhys Quays

**Rhys Quays** is Renada's working waterfront. Its landings, warehouses, ropewalks, inns, and laborers' homes gather along the River Rhys, where shipments arrive by boat and continue along the King's Road.

Grain, timber, woven goods, stone, metalwork, and other supplies pass through the district. Workers count cargo, repair vessels, mend rope, carry loads, and settle the disputes that accompany busy trade. The importance of **Millers' Bridge** to the grain trade is particularly evident here.

Among the quays' known establishments is the **Three Moorings**, an inn serving boat crews and travelers looking for a meal or place to rest. The district is neither entirely impoverished nor uniformly prosperous: the same river economy sustains substantial commercial ventures and households whose fortunes depend on the next vessel's arrival.

## The Mudsteps

**The Mudsteps** is a poorer neighborhood **within Rhys Quays**, where uneven lanes rise above the working bank. A household may depend on a ship docking when expected, on the availability of cargo work, or on the health of a wage-earner whose income cannot easily be replaced.

[[Lavitz Shawe]], the drow paladin who later became **Lord Marshal**, grew up in the Mudsteps. His father's livelihood was connected to riverfront work, and the family's circumstances were modest. For generations, prejudice toward drow residents intensified the difficulties faced by some households in the city's poorer districts.

Lavitz's appointment to high office has given the neighborhood an unusual place in public stories of the kingdom's progress. It should not be taken as proof that every child here now enjoys equal opportunity. The Mudsteps matters beyond the childhood of one celebrated man: its families help sustain the capital whether or not any of them ever appears in a royal chronicle.

## Lantern Ward and the Markets

**Lantern Ward** is the capital's principal mercantile district. **Lantern Market** brings together food, fabric, tools, and goods arriving from across Avalor and through trade routes beyond it. Grain from the Royal Heartlands, worked steel from [[Stanleyville]], timber, cloth, resin, dyes, and spices all pass between hands here.

Its **Hall of Measures** serves an essential but less theatrical purpose: recognized weights, customary standards, and records used when buyers and sellers dispute quantity, quality, or delivery. The gold lion, silver shield, and copper torch change hands in transactions whose visible price does not reveal every laborer who helped bring an item to market.

Guilds and markets can preserve skill and quality, yet also shape who has access to training and trade. Lantern Ward's prosperity is inseparable from Rhys Quays, where much of its cargo first arrives. Neither district is self-sufficient.

## Cathedral Ward

**Cathedral Ward** surrounds [[The Great Cathedral]], one of Renada's most prominent religious institutions. The ward draws worshippers, residents, travelers, and petitioners who may come for shelter or a fair hearing as readily as for ceremonies.

The Cathedral honors **Tyr, Helm, and Torm**, whose traditions concern justice, vigilance, and duty. [[Astronoph]], a female deva, serves as High Priest. Its charitable work is as much a part of the district's identity as its architecture.

The ward is not the only center of faith in Avalor. The kingdom also contains communities devoted to Lathander, Corellon, Eilistraee, Moradin, and other traditions. The [[The Cathedral Paladins|Cathedral Paladins]] at [[Heaven's Heart]] share devotion to the Threefold gods but are a distinct institution in a different place.

The correction of the old history of [[Jaznaar]] has raised further questions about how religious and civic institutions can protect those harmed by inherited prejudices. The cathedral's promise of justice is measured partly by the attention it gives those least able to demand it.

## Rynhold Park and Public Memory

**Rynhold Park** is a public green space amid the busy capital. Its paths and monuments recall the founding king and figures associated with the early kingdom, while residents of differing stations use it for rest and meeting.

Its most disputed monument is a statue of [[Aldus Greystone III]]. For centuries the healer was praised as a founding hero while [[Jaznaar]] was falsely condemned as the Betrayer. After [[The Misfits]] defeated [[Cid the Blood King]], the city chose **not to remove the statue**. Instead it removed the old inscription, preserving it in [[The Great Library of Aldus Greystone]] at Heaven's Heart, and installed a corrected account identifying Aldus with Cid and acknowledging Jaznaar's innocence.

Citizens and scholars continue to disagree about this choice. Some value a monument that exposes historical error; others believe the statue should no longer stand. The historian [[Professor Xiomara Thal]] has warned that a changed inscription cannot repair lives damaged by the original lie, while Lady Miren Dast has argued for preserving evidence of what earlier generations were taught.

The park remains both a place for living citizens and an invitation to question what a kingdom chooses to honor.

## A capital still becoming itself

Renada benefits from the Age of Ascendancy, though the rewards of prosperity do not reach every district equally. One visitor may know the city through the Great Citadel and Great Cathedral. Another may know it through the Three Moorings, the Hall of Measures, or the climb from the Mudsteps toward Crownrise.

Those views are not competing locations on the world map. They are parts of one capital—and the history of Renada is diminished if any of them is mistaken for the whole.
