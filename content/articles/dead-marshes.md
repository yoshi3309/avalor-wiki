## A country fiercely alive

The name **Dead Marshes** has encouraged the belief that the region is a lifeless waste. Travelers who have crossed its waters know otherwise. The marshes teem with growth, decay, strange creatures, and places that resist dependable description. Its waterways and paths can change sufficiently that an old account of safe passage may offer little comfort to the next visitor.

The marshes occupy country between the [[Forest of the Fey]] and Avalor's settled heartlands. They are part of the mortal landscape, though their position near the fey wilds and the unusual powers found within them have given rise to many explanations for their character.

## The first wound: the founding wars

The land was treacherous even before Avalor existed. The wars against **Lord Osmo** inflicted further damage upon it, and accounts of the decisive struggle at [[Heroes Hill]] have long been associated with the marshes' troubled condition. The surviving [[Lord Osmo’s Tower]] stands within these wetlands, a reminder of the conqueror's military reach before the founding.

Popular retellings often describe the marshes as the scar of a single battle. Yet the dangerous waters predate Osmo's final defeat, and the fighting added to injuries already borne by the land. The region's history is older and more complicated than any one field of war.

## The second wound: the Black Tower

Generations after the founding struggle, the catastrophe at the Black Tower disturbed the region again. The magical consequences of Aldus Greystone III's experiments deepened dangers already present.

This distinction matters. The Black Tower did not create the marshes, nor did it give them every strange property that travelers now encounter. Their history is one of **more than one upheaval** and of lives that continued between and after those events.

The Great Desert also bears the consequences of the Black Tower, but it is a different region whose transformation cannot simply be equated with that of the marshes.

## Wayward Sun and the Hunters Guild

It was within these dangerous reaches that **Faelynn**, the wood elf ranger remembered among [[The Six]], founded the **Hunters Guild** at [[Wayward Sun]]. After suffering a fiend's deception and the destruction of a village, she devoted her gifts to protecting those whom ordinary defenses could not easily reach.

The guild's work is more than the pursuit of monsters. It calls for the judgment to guide travelers, safeguard settlements, understand changing terrain, and recognize danger before it reaches the vulnerable.

## Lanterns and the Sisters of the Night Garden

Among the warnings carried through the marshes are accounts of misleading lanterns, witches who wear borrowed faces, and a hut able to move on great twisted roots. The traveler **[[Silas Dreadmore]]** encountered one such dwelling and its occupant, **Mother Vey**, a hag associated with the **Sisters of the Night Garden**, who serve a power called the **Den Mother**. His attempt to rob the hag ended in a bargain that changed him and placed him under a lasting obligation.

Such stories help explain the caution of people who know the marshes well. They also recall a particular danger: a traveler may mistake hospitality for safety or assume that a bargain ends when the agreed price is paid.

## Shelter, routes, and remembrance

Accounts of journeys through the Dead Marshes frequently recall the **Leaking Bucket Inn**, a place of relative safety amid otherwise unreliable country. Its modest shelter reminds the reader that a dependable roof may mean more to a traveler than a heroic monument.

The Royal Commission records the marshes not merely to warn those who might cross them, but to remember the people who have learned to live alongside their hazards. This landscape remains dangerous; it is not devoid of homes, institutions, or history.
