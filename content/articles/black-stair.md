## A stair remembered by travelers

The **Black Stair** rises among the approaches to [[Heaven's Heart]] and the [[Spine of the World]]. Travelers know it for the severity of its ascent and for the way the mountain seems to draw close around the path.

Its name has endured in accounts of pilgrimage, commerce, and study. The effort required to reach the sanctuary has become part of how generations remember the journey.

## The road to a sanctuary

Heaven's Heart preserves the teachings of **Lowkee**, one of [[The Six]], and the paladin tradition that places the protection of those in need above obedience to the powerful. Travelers ascend for many reasons: faith, instruction, scholarship, or the work of supplying a settlement far from easy roads.

The Black Stair belongs to all of these journeys. No single celebrated pilgrim or hero can claim the path as a private monument.

## The learning beyond the ascent

The nearby **Great Library of Aldus Greystone** preserves records whose significance changed after the revelations surrounding Aldus and [[Jaznaar]]. Its custodians retained the original inscription removed from Aldus's monument in Renada, allowing the words to be studied without continuing to honor a false account.

The path may be measured in steps, but the labor of correcting a cherished history is less easily counted. Both are familiar to those who seek learning at Heaven's Heart.

## The limits of the record

The public sources identify a difficult approach, not a hidden magical purpose or an authenticated ancient designer for every stone. A historian should resist adding wonders merely because the place seems to invite them.

The Black Stair needs no invented mystery to possess meaning. It records the efforts of generations who carried goods, questions, and convictions up the mountain.
