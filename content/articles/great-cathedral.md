## A cathedral among the people

The **Great Cathedral** rises near the heart of [[Renada]] in the district known as [[Cathedral Ward]]. Its steps and charitable houses belong to the everyday movement of the city. Petitioners and worshippers arrive alongside those who seek shelter, advice, or relief from a difficulty no private household can resolve alone.

The building is a prominent part of the capital, but its importance extends beyond its stonework and ceremonial role.

## Tyr, Helm, and Torm

The cathedral honors [[The Threefold Devotion|the traditions of Tyr, Helm, and Torm]]. Their teachings are familiar within Avalorian religious life and are shared in part with [[The Cathedral Paladins]] established by [[Lowkee]].

[[Astronoph]], a female deva, serves as **High Priest**. Her responsibilities connect the cathedral to the crown and to a long history of religious service, while requiring attention to the people who seek aid without the benefit of rank or wealth.

Avalor's faiths are broader than one institution. Communities also honor Lathander, Corellon, Moradin, Eilistraee, and other traditions. The prominence of the Great Cathedral should not be mistaken for the absence of such practices.

## Charity and conscience

A religious institution serving a capital has obligations extending beyond ceremonies. People seek counsel, justice, shelter, and practical help. The cathedral's history also carries difficult questions about times when sacred teachings were invoked to justify unequal treatment or political wrongdoing.

The new examination of Avalor's past requires its religious leaders, no less than its royal historians, to distinguish inherited authority from evidence and moral responsibility.

## A sister tradition, not the same institution

[[Heaven's Heart]] shares the cathedral's religious inheritance but remains a separate sanctuary and settlement near the [[Spine of the World]]. Its paladins are not merely guards stationed in Cathedral Ward. Their training reflects Lowkee's principle that an oath is owed above all to those who cannot stand without protection.

The Great Cathedral is therefore best understood as a living institution within a living city. Its lasting measure is not only what has been proclaimed beneath its vaults, but what its people do for those who enter.
