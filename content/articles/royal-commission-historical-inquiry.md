## The kingdom that questioned its own story

The **Royal Commission of Historical Inquiry** prepares and maintains the historical account known as **The Living Chronicle**. Its work arises in a kingdom that has learned how deeply a false story can enter its institutions.

For centuries, the founding hero [[Jaznaar]] was remembered as a betrayer. After the defeat of Cid the Blood King and the recovery of evidence concerning the [[Black Tower]], the old accusation could no longer stand. The revelation did more than correct a name: it compelled historians to revisit records, monuments, and traditions that had treated an error as settled truth.

## The Commission's contributors

**[[Lady Miren Dast]]**, Royal Archivist of Renada, chairs the Commission.

**[[Brother Halden Korr]]**, an archivist of [[Heaven's Heart]], brings knowledge of Cathedral records and inherited testimony.

**[[Professor Xiomara Thal]]**, an independent scholar associated with [[Shamman’s Tower]], examines the circumstances in which evidence survived and the consequences of public historical error.

Their different methods and judgments are preserved as part of the account rather than erased to make the history appear unanimous.

## Evidence and reconstruction

The Commission distinguishes contemporary testimony, later copies, regional tradition, and scholarly interpretation. Where no authenticated word-for-word record survives, it marks illustrative reconstructions as such rather than presenting them as newly discovered original documents.

This distinction matters. A historian may reliably identify what a decree accomplished while lacking the exact words in which the decree was first written.

The Commission does not promise that every conclusion is final. It promises to preserve its reasoning and leave room for later correction.

## Beyond the monuments

In Renada, [[Rynhold Park]] has become a visible example of the difficulty of public remembrance. The monument to Aldus Greystone III remains, but its inscription has been revised and the old plaque preserved at [[The Great Library of Aldus Greystone]].

The disagreement over what should stand, what should be preserved, and what should be corrected belongs to the Commission's work as surely as the examination of manuscripts.

## An institution serving the living

History is not owned by the crown or by its chroniclers. It belongs also to the people whose lives were shaped by the policies, beliefs, and misjudgments of earlier generations.

The Royal Commission's most important obligation is to make its findings useful to those who now inhabit Avalor—and answerable to those who may discover better evidence in the future.


## The bard's published testimony

[[Heureux]], who traveled with the Misfits and later wrote their history, is a crucial firsthand source for the period. The Commission distinguishes his participant testimony from independent corroboration; affection for one's friends neither invalidates a witness nor makes the witness infallible.
