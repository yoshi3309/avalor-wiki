## S’vendyal of the Misfits

**S’vendyal Shadowwhisper**, called **Sven**, was an **eladrin** among the original adventurers brought together by Avalor’s Lord Marshal. His striking grayscale appearance attracted attention, but his place in history arose from his choices rather than from the expectations others attached to his ancestry.

His people have traditions connected with the Feywild. Such connections should not be used to assign the same customs or loyalties to every eladrin.

Sven accompanied [[Lavitz Shawe]], [[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]], and the others through dangers that ultimately revealed the Blood King’s true identity.

## The Iron Shadow

In the years after Cid’s defeat, Sven became known in rumor as the **Iron Shadow**. His activities grew difficult for public chroniclers to trace. Tales credit him with interventions outside the reach of the court, but their repetition does not establish a reliable account of their circumstances.

The Royal Commission does not certify the existence, organization, membership, or methods of any alleged secret apparatus on the strength of rumor. The limits of the record are especially important when a figure's reputation depends partly upon being unseen.

## A friendship that remained

Lavitz entered public service as Lord Marshal. Tel’Lora became High Wizard and restored Shamman’s Tower as a place of teaching. Sven's path was less visible, yet he remained closely associated with his two friends.

Later storytellers describe the three as a pair of celebrated royal officials with a third, unseen companion. This may capture something of the public imagination; it cannot substitute for verifiable testimony.

## More than an absence in the archive

For twenty years after the Blood King's fall, the five Misfits did not reunite as a complete company. Some worked together, while [[Heureux]] and [[Amara]] followed more distant roads.

Their separation was not a rupture. Sven's history offers a reminder that friendships need not be displayed in court and that a person’s importance is not measured by the number of records filed under his name.

The Archives preserve what is publicly known and leave the rest unclaimed.
