## More than a footnote in someone else's story

Some of the communities that shaped the history of Avalor never established a kingdom, guarded a famous capital or founded a great guild. They appear in the record because travelers crossed their territory, because their people were threatened, or because one of their members departed on an extraordinary journey.

Such meetings are worth preserving without reducing an entire people to a single fight or the deeds of one notable individual. **The Tide-Touched kobolds** and **the Rotten Husk Clan** are distinct communities in different historical accounts. Their presence within the same volume does not establish a common ancestry, alliance, belief or homeland.

This collection records modest but meaningful communities for which the surviving testimony is too limited to justify inventing a separate, elaborate political history. Communities with a wider, independently preserved history—such as the **[[The Ringtree Clan|Ringtree Clan]]** of the Forest of the Fey—retain their own full account.

## The Tide-Touched Kobolds

In the **2020 AF** undertaking later called the Blackwater Bay affair, five operatives sent by **[[Lavitz Shawe]]** entered an ancient passage whose walls were bounded by enchanted water. The corridor was disrupted by the intrusion of crimson blood magic, throwing the travelers out of their intended route and into **a forgotten, partly flooded tide chamber**.

There the outsiders met a tribe of **Tide-Touched kobolds**. Their scales were blue-green, and their adornments included shells and coral. Their community had developed around the old waterway; the Tide-Touched were at home among the remnants of a place the strangers had barely begun to understand.

### The Tidecaller and Grik

An elder shaman, identified by the title **Tidecaller**, interpreted the sudden arrival of the outsiders in terms of the tribe's traditions. Not all of the community agreed. **Grik**, a more aggressive kobold, challenged the elder's interpretation, and the dispute contributed to violence between the tribe and the travelers.

A fight took place, but the encounter did **not** end in the extermination or defeat of the tribe as a people. The travelers were able to resolve the underlying disturbance and move on. The conflict had arisen from fear, incompatible understandings and an internal disagreement—not proof that the Tide-Touched existed only to oppose visitors.

### The kobold who went with them

The travelers also encountered a small kobold named **[[Skrik, the Tide Blessed|Skrik]]**, who styled himself **“the Tide Blessed.”** He possessed a remarkable confidence in his own importance, a confidence not universally shared by the rest of his tribe.

The Tide-Touched leaders sent Skrik with the outsiders. He joined their company and remained part of the ensuing journey rather than merely guiding them through one corridor. His later exploits and testimony would attract attention from notable figures in Renada, though stories told by Skrik himself often outpaced what independent witnesses could confirm.

Skrik's departure changed the course of his own life. His people remained beside the old waterway, with their history and concerns continuing beyond his adventure.

## The Rotten Husk Clan

The **Rotten Husk**, an established **orc tribe** also called a clan in the old histories, became known to Avalor through an encounter with **[[The Misfits]]**. Its name appears in the fellowship's adventures, but the community had its own existence and needs long before the travelers arrived.

### Under a chieftain who harmed his people

During the Misfits' campaign in **2000 AF**, the Rotten Husk lived under a **chieftain who had become a danger to his own people**. The leader's authority and actions created a crisis within the tribe, but that does not establish that the community supported his behavior.

Later tellings sometimes shorten the event to a confrontation between adventurers and an orc chief, losing the perspective of the people who lived beneath his rule.

### Freedom, not ownership

The Misfits intervened and **helped free the Rotten Husk from the chieftain**. Surviving accounts differ on the precise form of the confrontation yet agree on its essential result: the tribe was liberated, and the companions departed with its goodwill.

This freedom was not a claim that the heroes had become the clan's new leaders, nor evidence that the community was annexed into an army or absorbed by the Crown. The orcs remained a people with their own choices to make.

The incident is preserved at length in **[[Freedom for the Rotten Husk]]** and in **[[the-misfits#the-liberation-of-the-rotten-husk|the Misfits' historical account]]**. Those records concern the intervention and what it revealed about the fellowship; this entry preserves the Rotten Husk's identity as a community.

### The history still to be told

What leadership arose afterward, what territory the Rotten Husk claimed, and what alliances its members formed have not been recovered. The end of one oppressive rule did not settle every question about their future.

The essential distinction is worth remembering: **their chieftain was an adversary, but the Rotten Husk themselves were people whom the Misfits chose to defend**.

## Distinct histories, equally worth recording

The Tide-Touched lived beside an ancient waterway, while the Rotten Husk faced a crisis of leadership during the earlier Misfits era. One people sent a talkative young adventurer into a much larger story; the other gained the freedom to continue its own.

Neither is an appendage of another civilization or a symbol for its whole ancestry. Their histories are modest in the surviving record, but they belong in Avalor's account of the people who endured extraordinary events and continued living after famous visitors had gone.
