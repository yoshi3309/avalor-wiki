## The voyage that delayed a queen's rescue

The adventures of [[The Misfits]] after the [[The Tournament of Champions|Renada Games]] are sometimes told as an uninterrupted pursuit of Cid into the Shadowfell. They were not. **Ros Furband**, a pirate captain operating under a paid arrangement, intercepted the companions after the tournament and turned the rescuers themselves into captives.

The timing is essential. **Queen Melmiri had already been taken by Cid**, and the Misfits had already committed themselves to finding her. Ros's ship did not carry them on an earlier, unrelated expedition. It prevented them from continuing an urgent rescue.

## The captain and his bargain

Ros commanded a **galleon** with a substantial pirate complement. The captured companions were disarmed and restrained; their own magical strength offered little immediate freedom. Ros was a contractor in dangerous work, not a follower whose beliefs have been shown to match those of the faction to whom he delivered his prisoners.

His arrangements led toward **Jinlin Vechon**, the dragonborn vampire who had organized the [[Independent Factions of the Misfits Era#the-wrath-of-innocent|Wrath of Innocent]]. Jinlin's hatred of the Blood King was genuine, but the Wrath could treat even enemies of Cid as obstacles to its vengeance.

Ros's exact fee, the point at which he took the companions aboard, and his subsequent career remain absent from the reliable public record. The Commission does not turn their absence into a second biography of invented raids or motives.

## The work of captivity

The most enduring memories of Ros's voyage are not all memories of combat. **Lavitz and Heureux were set to labor at the oars**. **Tel'Lora and [[Anna, Lowkee's Keeper|Anna]] worked in the galley**. Sven was among those given duties as a lookout.

Accounts also place **[[Beldir Willowrun]]**, a senior naval officer and later royal counselor, within this strange company. His exact reason for sharing the captives' voyage remains unresolved. The presence of the experienced admiral does not convert Ros's ship into an official naval mission.

These details give the episode its human scale. The companions who would eventually restore a queen could still be exhausted, humiliated and made to work under another person's command.

## An interruption, not an alliance

The Misfits' path through Ros's captivity led to an actual confrontation with Jinlin. The companions **defeated Jinlin in the waking world** before the later illusions of [[Zauks and the False Lives|Zauks]].

Ros belonged to the obstruction of the rescue rather than its purpose. The pirate's actions should not be credited to a supposed alliance with Cid unless evidence for such a pact emerges. The history presently establishes his paid role and the serious cost of the delay, not the secret loyalties behind every order he gave.

In a campaign crowded with kings, undead creatures and ancient magic, Ros stands for a simpler danger: the freedom of ordinary criminals to profit while others try to save a life.
