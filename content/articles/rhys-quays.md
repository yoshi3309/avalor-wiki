## Where Renada wakes

The **Rhys Quays** form the working face of [[Renada]]. River landings, warehouses, ropewalks, inns, and streets of laborers' homes gather beside the **River Rhys**, where cargo arrives by boat and departs again along the King's Road.

The capital's wealth depends upon this movement. A procession through Crownrise may draw a chronicler's attention, but an arriving grain barge can matter more directly to the families waiting for work or to the merchants arranging the next delivery.

## The work of the water

The river brings grain, timber, woven goods, stone, metalwork, and other necessities. Workers count loads, repair vessels, mend ropes, and settle the disputes that accompany every busy landing.

The **Great Stone Bridge** is admired for its ceremonial importance. **Millers' Bridge**, by contrast, is especially familiar to those moving grain toward the mills and to people who cross the river in the ordinary course of work. Both connect the district to the wider city.

Along the quays stands the **Three Moorings**, an inn remembered for a bed, a warm meal, and the company of boat crews and travelers whose arrival may not have been planned.

## The Mudsteps within the quays

The poorer neighborhood known as the [[Mudsteps]] lies **within the Rhys Quays**, not beside it as an independent town or district. Its narrow lanes and uncertain livelihoods are part of the same river economy whose prosperity reaches wealthier households elsewhere in Renada.

[[Lavitz Shawe]] spent his childhood there before becoming the kingdom's Lord Marshal. His public honor should not obscure the daily circumstances of families who still depend upon work along the river.

## A city beyond its monuments

The quays are neither merely a place of poverty nor simply a prosperous commercial district. They contain the labor, trade, hospitality, and inequalities through which Renada's status as a capital becomes real.

Their story is not separate from the kingdom's history. It is one of the means by which that history has been sustained.
