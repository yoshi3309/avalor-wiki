## Six names at the beginning

**[[Aldus Greystone III]]. [[Lowkee]]. [[Faelynn]]. [[Petris]]. [[Kelsalor]]. [[Jaznaar]].**

For generations these names were learned together, spoken in royal halls, and commemorated in songs. The fellowship called **the Six** helped shape Avalor's earliest understanding of what a united kingdom might mean: people of different ancestries, faiths, and temperaments choosing to face danger together.

It is tempting to imagine that the six had always traveled as one company. The sources tell a less convenient story. Some had crossed paths during the struggle against Lord Osmo, while their united fellowship emerged **shortly after the founding of Avalor**. The precise circumstances remain contested. One tradition credits [[Rynhold Frozenroar|Rynhold Frozenroar]] with bringing the heroes together; another sees their friendship growing from smaller companies during the founding wars. Ballads prefer the explanation that destiny arranged everything.

The Royal Commission regards their enduring choice to remain companions as more revealing than any certainty that fate required it.

## A friendship that changed the kingdom

The Six were not a royal regiment, an order governed by a single doctrine, or six people who always answered a difficult question alike. Their gifts complemented one another, but their convictions often differed.

A healer might seek time to study a danger that a ranger wished to pursue. A paladin devoted to duty might question a spellcaster who would not disclose the full purpose of his work. A druid concerned with living balance might caution against a road or settlement where others saw only the promise of prosperity.

For years, these differences strengthened their fellowship. They traveled, served the developing kingdom, and made choices whose consequences reached beyond one battlefield. Their example mattered partly because the kingdom itself depended upon cooperation among communities that had not always shared a sovereign.

In surviving records, the Six are remembered chiefly through battles, institutions, and the catastrophe that ended their unity. Their ordinary companionship is less visible. Later stories offer campfire conversations, arguments, and jokes in remarkable detail, but historians cannot certify such reconstructed private moments as fact.

## Aldus Greystone III — the healer

[[Aldus Greystone III]] possessed extraordinary restorative abilities and a rare affinity for weather-working magic. The sick and injured sought his help. In the early days of the fellowship, his work offered reason to believe that suffering might be prevented on a scale no ordinary healer could manage.

His companions knew of his first inquiries into the boundaries of healing and did not initially regard them as criminal. It was only as his ambition turned from preserving individual lives toward **ending mortality itself** that his work took on a terrible character.

After the Black Tower catastrophe, Aldus's name would be preserved for centuries in a distorted account of heroism. The later discovery that he survived as [[Cid the Blood King]] forced the kingdom to distinguish his genuine early compassion from the crimes that followed.

## Lowkee — the protector

[[Lowkee]] was a human paladin whose friendship with Rynhold Frozenroar helped connect the fellowship to the ideals of the young kingdom. He founded [[The Cathedral Paladins]] at [[Heaven's Heart]], where his **First Teaching** placed the defense of the helpless above unquestioning obedience to powerful patrons.

His legacy was not a simple command always to fight. It was an insistence that strength must have a righteous purpose, even when that purpose brings a protector into conflict with established authority.

That conviction would eventually make the question of Aldus's actions especially painful. Lowkee loved a companion whose experiments had become a danger to those he was sworn to defend.

## Faelynn — the Night Mistress

[[Faelynn]], a wood elf ranger known as the **Night Mistress**, understood places beyond the kingdom's settled roads. Her history included a devastating deception by a fiend and the loss of a village she had unwittingly helped expose to danger.

She later founded [[The Hunters Guild]] at [[Wayword Sun]], within the perilous [[Dead Marshes]]. Its work extended protection to travelers and communities threatened by dangers that city walls and royal decrees could not reach.

The records do not establish whether redemption, duty, or both guided every decision she made. They establish that the suffering she had seen did not cause her to abandon others in need.

## Petris — the light that asked for nothing

[[Petris]] was an elven cleric devoted to [[Lathander and the Devotion of the Dawn|Lathander]], the Morninglord. Her service to wounded soldiers, displaced families, and communities recovering from war belonged to the work of making victory into peace.

She believed that life could be renewed even after grief. Unlike Aldus, she did not come to regard the existence of mortality as sufficient reason to disregard the suffering of those still living.

Petris would eventually die peacefully. Her inheritance endured through her descendants, among whom was [[Amara]] of [[The Misfits]], a companion of a much later age.

## Kelsalor — the guardian of balance

[[Kelsalor]] was a firbolg druid who understood the living world as a changing balance rather than an arrangement that should be kept permanently still. Forests grew and declined; rivers altered their courses; life and death formed parts of a larger pattern.

He guarded the conditions upon which living things depended and questioned the cost of needless destruction. His travels did not produce one great institution equivalent to Lowkee's paladin order. His influence persisted through people, places, and traditions of care.

Nearly two millennia later, his descendant [[Heureux]] would find a very different calling as a bard among the Misfits.

## Jaznaar — the sixth name

[[Jaznaar]] was a drow spellcaster of extraordinary power and a trusted companion of the founding generation. He was close to Rynhold and to the members of the fellowship. His arcane knowledge made him invaluable where military power alone could achieve little.

Jaznaar could be independent and guarded with his knowledge. Later enemies turned those qualities into supposed evidence of betrayal. The truth was the opposite: he would undertake an extraordinary burden to preserve the kingdom, even while its citizens were taught to condemn him.

His precise spellcasting class is **not securely established** in the surviving public sources; the Archives do not force the competing labels of wizard or sorcerer into a false certainty.

## Six answers to one terrible question

The fellowship began to fracture when Aldus's experiments progressed beyond healing. The resulting arcane corruption came to be known as [[The Blood Sickness]]. His companions learned gradually that the man they had trusted was inflicting suffering in pursuit of his ambition.

They agreed the harm must end, but not about what ending it required.

**Lowkee** came to believe Aldus must be stopped even if it meant killing him. **Faelynn** increasingly shared that conclusion. **Petris** continued to hope he could be redeemed, and **Jaznaar** remained determined to find a means of saving him. **Kelsalor** tried to mediate between friends whose positions had become almost impossible to reconcile.

It is important not to mistake disagreement over redemption for disagreement about the suffering Aldus caused. None of his five companions endorsed the cruelty of his later experiments. The tragedy lay in the fact that loyalty and duty no longer pointed toward the same choice.

## The last journey to the Black Tower

Approximately a decade after Avalor's founding, Aldus undertook a ritual at the [[Black Tower]] in the [[Spine of the World]]. Its aim was not merely to lengthen life but to change the fundamental conditions of mortality, involving the boundary between the mortal world and the Shadowfell.

The remaining five traveled to stop him. Histories disagree about the exact sequence of the confrontation. Cathedral traditions emphasize an assault, arcane scholars the magical backlash, and later ballads offer final speeches none can authenticate.

The established account is narrower and more terrible: **the ritual was interrupted, Aldus's mortal body was slain, and his intended catastrophe was prevented from reaching its full conclusion**. No single final blow is reliably established.

The surviving companions departed believing the threat ended. The details of their division are explored in [[The Sundering of the Six]].

## Five remained, and a false story grew

Avalor survived, but the consequences of the catastrophe endured. Land in the north was gradually transformed into the [[Great Desert]], while magical instability troubled other regions, including the already dangerous [[Dead Marshes]] and [[Forest of the Fey]].

Aldus had not truly ceased to exist. He endured in the Shadowfell as Cid. Jaznaar later discovered the truth and assumed the task of containing the threat and much of the Blood Sickness. For almost two thousand years, he sustained that burden largely unseen.

Meanwhile, agents of Cid helped spread [[The Betrayer's Legend|a false account]] that presented Aldus as a savior and Jaznaar as the saboteur of his work. **The founding eyewitnesses had not believed Jaznaar guilty.** The falsehood came to dominate only in later generations, after firsthand memory passed away and repeated testimony acquired the appearance of authority.

This distortion harmed more than one hero's memory. It helped reinforce suspicion toward drow communities and supplied a convenient justification for prejudice that later rulers could exploit.

## A legacy corrected, not erased

When [[The Misfits]] uncovered the truth of the Black Tower and defeated Cid, the old accusation could no longer stand. [[King Gradvum Frozenroar]] formally [[The Vindication of Jaznaar|vindicated Jaznaar]], though the surviving hero had died before that proclamation.

The Six are now remembered differently. Their names continue to appear upon monuments and in institutions, but the Archives distinguish genuine service from later invention. At [[Rynhold Park]], the statue of Aldus remains with a corrected inscription; the former plaque is preserved at [[The Great Library of Aldus Greystone]] as evidence of the history once taught.

In [[Heaven's Heart]], Lowkee's teaching continues. At Wayword Sun, Faelynn's guild still speaks to the needs of travelers. Petris's compassion and Kelsalor's stewardship survive in descendants and traditions. Jaznaar's restored name stands beside theirs.

Their legacy is neither a flawless fellowship nor a story that ends with betrayal. It is the knowledge that six friends helped give a kingdom its beginning, and that the kingdom's obligation to truth outlived the friendship it once celebrated.
