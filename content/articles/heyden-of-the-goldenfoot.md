## The prince who chose the trail

**Heyden Goldenfoot**, usually known as **Heyden of the Goldenfoot**, was born beneath a shooting star on a brisk autumn night in the **Kingdom of Labos**. Whether the star heralded a great destiny or simply provided a story for his family to retell, Heyden never cared much. He was the fourth son of **King Geoff** and **Queen Viviliv**, and his older brothers already seemed to have claimed the lives expected of royal children. **Jeothor**, the eldest, would inherit the throne. **Verbos** followed the martial tradition that led toward the office of Lord General. **Seals** chose a monastic path.

That left Heyden with a luxury many princes never enjoy: freedom. He received a royal education in history, diplomacy, politics and etiquette, but he did not find his purpose in the palace. There were too many walls and too many people measuring their words because they knew his father. Beyond the gates, a forest judged him by patience, skill and endurance. A beast did not bow to a prince. A track could not be followed by offering it gold.

He first hunted for recreation, then became devoted to the pursuit itself. The broken branch, the faint mark in mud, the hours of uncertainty before quarry and hunter finally saw one another—these interested him more than the kill. He valued competence because it could not be inherited. Royal wealth made expeditions to distant lands possible; it could not do the hunting for him.

## The Allosaurus of Chult

One of Heyden's earliest great expeditions carried him across the sea to **Chult**. He joined hunters tracking an enormous **Allosaurus** through hot, ancient jungle. Their pursuit lasted days, marked by shattered foliage and immense footprints. When they finally cornered the predator, it turned on them. The formation scattered as arrows and spears struck its hide.

Heyden found himself far too close. The Allosaurus slashed the right side of his face, cutting through flesh and parting his mane. Before it could strike again, he lunged into the creature and tore into its throat with his claws. The beast fell. Heyden collapsed beside it and survived.

The long scar running down the **right side of his face and into his mane** became his most obvious distinguishing feature. He does not conceal it. In a sense, he regards it as a better trophy than anything that might hang on a wall: he had been tested at a distance where his wealth and name were of no use.

## The Great Elk of Weyland

The hunt that most changed Heyden was not the one that almost killed him. In **Weyland Forest**, travelers spoke of a magnificent **Great Elk** that seemed to evade everyone who sought it. Some thought it only an exceptionally old animal; others suggested something ancient and uncanny. Heyden wanted to find it.

His search lasted **nearly two years**. The **Woodland Elves of Valiant** understood the forest far better than he did, and under their guidance Heyden was forced to abandon impatient habits. They taught him to hear what belonged among the trees rather than merely looking for signs of disturbance, and to move without demanding the forest's attention. He lived beneath the canopy through storms and changing seasons, watching tracks disappear and waiting for them to return.

At last he saw the Great Elk standing among the trees. His arrow found its mark. Yet the moment he remembered was the silence after the shot—and the two years of patient work that had preceded it.

The Woodland Elves had watched a prince choose hardship over ease and refuse to abandon a hunt he could have left at any time. They accepted him as one of their own, marked his **left thigh with their sigil**, and gave him a **Revenant Double-Bladed Scimitar**. The tattoo is usually hidden; the weapon travels with him. Together they are reminders of belonging earned rather than bestowed by birth.

## Gomo's coin

Heyden continued to roam. He hunted beside elves, dwarves, humans, gnomes and anyone willing to share skills around a fire. He hunted predators and monsters, sometimes following rumors of impossible quarry simply because no one believed the beast could be found.

When a small gnomish village was threatened by a **Giant Constrictor Snake**, Heyden joined local hunters to organize the pursuit. They killed the creature, then gathered at a tavern, as successful hunting parties often do. There he met **[[minor-characters#gomo|Gomo]]**, a halfling hunter with stories worth hearing.

Gomo spoke of the **[[Dead Marshes]] of Avalor**, where strange magic had altered the animals and monsters of the wilderness. Ordinary predators sometimes developed extraordinary, dangerous traits. The possibility of tracking creatures shaped by such corruption immediately captivated Heyden.

Before they parted, Gomo gave him a **Hunters Guild coin** and advised him to present it if he ever reached Avalor. He also told him to seek **[[minor-characters#silver-of-moonlight|Silver of Moonlight]]**, a hunter who could help him find his footing. Heyden kept the coin, and the next journey all but decided itself.

## A prince in Avalor

The timing proved convenient. King Geoff and Queen Viviliv were preparing a diplomatic journey to **[[The Kingdom of Avalor|Avalor]]**, accompanied by one of their sons, to explore a relationship between the two kingdoms and the possibility of an alliance. Heyden traveled with the delegation. He spent several days in the capital, meeting the minimum obligations of royalty before turning toward the Dead Marshes.

**Gomo's coin opened the way into [[The Hunters Guild]]. His title did not.** That distinction pleased him.

At the guild Heyden met Silver of Moonlight, who introduced him to its customs, its members and the peculiar dangers of the marsh country. He planned to stay briefly. The first contract became a second, and the second another. The hunts were challenging, the pay worthwhile, and the hunters' skills too interesting to leave behind. Weeks became months.

He began to feel something unusual for a man who had spent most of his adult life departing places: the possibility of putting down roots.

## The hunter called Shadow

Eventually Heyden followed increasingly dangerous trails, including journeys into the **Wood of the Fey**. He seldom describes precisely what happened there. He mentions paths that defied expectations, strange teachers amused by a mortal hunter, and lessons about movement that changed his understanding of concealment. The precise identity of those fey and their connection, if any, to other known woodland communities has not been established.

Heyden learned to disappear into terrain that seemed incapable of hiding a Leonin of his size. He moved swiftly through the canopy, using branches, darkness and foliage in a way that unsettled other hunters. They would glance toward him and discover he was no longer there, only to hear something move overhead.

The guild began calling him **Shadow**. The name endured because it described a skill he had earned. He was no longer only Prince Heyden, fourth son of Geoff and Viviliv. Among the hunters he could be simply Shadow—one of their own.

## A hunter's family

Heyden had always understood that hunting was dangerous. He had nearly died beneath an Allosaurus's claws and seen injuries that no amount of preparation could prevent. Yet he had not expected the grief that followed when, only a few months after his arrival in Avalor, **several guild hunters failed to return**.

He had not been especially close to all of them. He remembered shared drinks, conversations, jokes and small arguments over tracks. Their empty places mattered anyway. A hunting party entrusted one another with more than stories: the creature approaching unseen, the trail behind them, and the promise that someone would carry word home if the worst happened.

The guild's members often repeated an old saying: **"No one misses a dead man's party."** Sometimes it was a grim jest over drinks; sometimes an invitation to honor those who had fallen. Heyden joined them when they gathered to mourn.

For most of his life, freedom had meant belonging nowhere long enough for departure to hurt. The Hunters Guild taught him another kind of freedom: the choice to remain among people worth grieving.

## Appearance, possessions and temperament

Heyden is **an unusually large Leonin**, weighing approximately **265 pounds**, with amber-gold eyes and a tawny mane darkened by brown undertones. The scar divides the right side of his mane. Despite his size he moves almost noiselessly, a combination of practiced hunting skill and the lessons he brought back from the fey woods.

He dresses as a hunter, not a prince: weathered leather, durable boots, layered clothes in muted earth tones, and a dark hooded cloak. He carries a **Goldenfoot signet** but rarely displays it. Gomo's guild coin is considerably more personal to him. The double-bladed scimitar from Valiant receives more care than most of his possessions. His other equipment is practical—longbow, specialized arrows, field-dressing knives, rope, traps, climbing gear and survival tools.

He enjoys difficult tracks, unfamiliar beasts, skilled hunters, campfires, strong drinks, friendly rivalries and tales exaggerated just enough to entertain. He dislikes wasteful trophy hunting, boastful incompetence, cowardice disguised as caution, empty ceremony and people who treat others differently because of a title. He loves his family and is protective of Labos even while complaining about its royal rituals.

Heyden is **chaotic good**: impatient with formal expectations, generous with his abilities, devoted to the chase but increasingly aware that the lives beside him matter more than the quarry ahead. He is **thirty-two years old** in this account. His current place among the guild, and the course of later hunts, remain stories to be told rather than outcomes to invent in advance.
