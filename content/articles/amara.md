## The druid remembered from the road

**Amara** was among the earliest companions of the fellowship called [[The Misfits]]. Later accounts identify her as a **druid**, a traveler in service of the natural world, and a descendant of [[Petris]], the elven cleric of [[The Six]].

She was part of the company that uncovered Avalor's great historical deception and defeated [[Cid the Blood King]]. Yet unlike [[Lavitz Shawe]] and [[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]], whose public appointments made them frequent subjects of royal records, Amara's later life is known chiefly through scattered testimony and the stories of people she encountered.

The difference is important. Historians possess fewer documents about her not because she contributed less to the fellowship, but because the service she chose did not require an audience at court.

## An inheritance across two thousand years

Amara's descent from Petris connects two ages that Avalor had long learned to remember separately. Petris served the people of a newly founded kingdom through compassion, healing, and devotion to [[Lathander and the Devotion of the Dawn|Lathander]], especially among those injured or displaced by war.

Amara did not simply repeat her ancestor's calling. Her work followed the practices of a druid, taking her into the living world beyond the institutions through which Petris had served.

The two lives can be read together without treating ancestry as destiny. Both women belonged to fellowships whose decisions affected the kingdom, and both placed value upon lives that might otherwise be overshadowed by royal histories.

[[Kelsalor]], another of the Six, also served the living world, teaching the principles later remembered as [[The Sacred Balance]]. Amara's family line led to Petris, while the two druids' callings shared a concern for the living world.

## Among unlikely companions

Amara belonged to the early fellowship with Lavitz, Tel'Lora, [[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]], and [[fo-gurn|Fo'Gurn]]. Their earliest recorded adventure brought them to the living halls of [[The Misfits#moonville-manor|Moonville Manor]]. **Only afterward** did the preceding Lord Marshal call upon them to investigate an unnamed necromancer. That first *royal commission* ended with the enemy's defeat, although fragments of magical research recovered afterward would acquire significance much later.

A second investigation, concerning [[The Cult of Gol’Goroth|Gol'Goroth's cult]], brought the bard [[Heureux]] into their company.

The Misfits were not selected to resemble a founding legend. Their friendships grew from hazards survived and choices made together. Amara's connection to Petris was a remarkable historical inheritance, but it did not dictate how she responded to danger or whose friendship she might earn.

What drew Amara to the Lord Marshal's commission is a quieter part of her story. What she did afterward is clear enough: she joined unlikely friends and stayed beside them through dangers no one had expected.

## Adventures worth remembering separately

The company traveled through encounters that did not all originate in Cid's schemes. After Moonville Manor and the royal commissions came the strange condition of the wizard [[Noki]]. In the aftermath of the Hydra and the losses at Stanleyville, the companions helped the [[The Rotten Husk Clan|Rotten Husk]] people escape a chieftain who had become dangerous to his own tribe. This episode is especially revealing of the fellowship's wider character: the companions judged the wrongdoing of a leader without treating an entire people as enemies.

These histories do not name Amara as the sole architect of each victory. They place her among the friends whose decisions made those outcomes possible.

The life of a druid can be measured by relationships to living things and threatened places, but there is no reliable list of every grove, creature, or settlement Amara personally protected during these travels. An archivist should resist turning plausible deeds into undocumented feats.

## The loss of Fo'Gurn and the companionship of Jack

The Misfits' early travels included a loss that reached the entire company. Fo'Gurn stayed behind before a hydra to allow the others to escape. They survived, believing he had died.

Later inquiry revealed complications in Fo'Gurn's fate involving the necromancer [[Jaaris]]. Those later revelations did not make their grief during the intervening years any less real.

At [[Stanleyville]], the group also befriended [[Jack of Stanleyville]], a child's conscious ghost seeking to discover who caused his death. Jack traveled with the Misfits when they left the town. He was neither a pet curiosity nor merely a piece of evidence: he was a companion whose unfinished life mattered to those who knew him.

For Amara, as for the others, these events meant that the fellowship's work concerned people and losses, not merely enchanted creatures or dangerous rituals.

## The owl and the lives that never were

Among the most recognizable details in Amara's surviving history is an **owl** associated with her during the ordeal created by [[Zauks and the False Lives|Zauks]].

The mind flayer used the companions' own memories to recreate earlier threats, then offered them convincing visions of lives in which their grief seemed undone and their desires fulfilled.

While the others were held in Zauks's counterfeit experiences, **Amara was outside their illusions**. The owl remembered at the close of the ordeal was Amara herself, whose intervention enabled the captives' escape and ended the mind flayer's cruelty. The record should not turn her rescue into an imagined false life of her own.

That rescue matters as more than an answer to a riddle. The companions were offered lives in which their sorrows had been undone; Amara reached them from the imperfect world they still belonged to. The owl of this encounter is distinct from the separate story of her sister, whose fate remains uncertain.

## The truth of the Black Tower

The fellowship's search eventually brought them before [[Jaznaar]], the last living member of the founding Six. At [[Jaznaar’s Hidden Refuge|his refuge]] they learned that the long-revered healer Aldus Greystone III had survived as Cid, while Jaznaar had been falsely condemned for the very catastrophe he spent centuries trying to contain.

The discovery changed the meaning of the history Amara had inherited through Petris. Her ancestor had traveled with men and women whose true disagreements had been concealed beneath an official lie.

The companions' later victory in the Shadowfell helped restore Jaznaar's name. Yet restoring a public history was not the same as restoring the ordinary lives damaged by its falsehood. The kingdom's past had become a responsibility for the living.

During [[The Tournament of Champions]], Cid abducted [[Queen Melmiri]]. **Amara was herself taken after the Games**, although the exact circumstances of her capture and return have not been established in the public record. The larger pursuit was interrupted by Ros Furband's pirate ship, a real battle with Jinlin Vechon, and [[Zauks and the False Lives|Zauks's false worlds]] before the Misfits reached the Shadowfell. By Zauks's final snare Amara was free to act against him from outside her companions' illusions. They succeeded in bringing the queen home and ending the immediate threat of the Blood King.

## A life away from the citadel

After the victory, Amara continued traveling in service of the natural world. Unlike companions who accepted royal posts, she did not become a regular presence at the council or the courts of Renada.

As Amara traveled farther from the court, the stories of her deeds reached Renada less often, until her appearances began to resemble the stuff of folklore.

Such stories should be read carefully. An absence from royal accounts is not proof of a miraculous disappearance, a death, or a permanent separation from her companions. Nor is it permission to ascribe new supernatural powers to her.

The quieter history remains substantial: Amara chose to continue caring for the world beyond the places where the kingdom kept its best records.

## The friends who did not forget

For twenty years after the defeat of Cid, the five celebrated Misfits did not meet again as one complete company. Lavitz served as Lord Marshal; Tel'Lora became High Wizard; Sven followed a more elusive path; and Heureux traveled and taught.

Amara's journeys took her far from their daily lives, but no authenticated account describes their separation as a rupture of friendship. The companions allowed one another to pursue responsibilities no longer arranged around the same campfire.

There is something quietly important in that. Avalor's founding Six were broken by a disagreement over one of their own. The Misfits preserved their mutual affection even when their lives led them in different directions.

## Where the kingdom's stories end

Amara remains one of the central figures of Avalor's recent history, even though her later deeds are difficult to trace through royal records.

Her story is not a tidy itinerary. It is the story of a woman who stood beside the friends who saved Avalor, carried the legacy of Petris into another age, and then chose the open road over the rewards of court.

The limits of that record are not a failure of her life. They are a reminder that the natural world, and the people who tend it, exist beyond the pages written in the capital.
