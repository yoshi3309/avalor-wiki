## A life before the sacrifice

**Fo’Gurn**, a **brass dragonborn** paladin, is remembered for standing against a hydra so that [[The Misfits]] could escape. Long before that sacrifice, however, he had been a trusted friend and a companion whose courage grew from years of shared service.

Fo'Gurn had known [[Lavitz Shawe]] long before either adventured with the company. Their friendship began during their years of paladin training at [[Heaven's Heart]], beneath the [[Spine of the World]]. By the time they faced a hydra together, theirs was a bond forged through shared duty and tested long before that final stand.

## Heaven's Heart and a lifelong friendship

The paladin traditions of Heaven's Heart trace their founding to [[Lowkee]], who taught that the strength of a protector was owed first to those least able to defend themselves. The order honors the virtues of [[The Threefold Devotion]], associated with Tyr, Helm, and Torm.

Fo'Gurn trained within that tradition during Lavitz's years at Heaven's Heart. He was among the first to treat the young drow with respect, and the two pushed one another to become better paladins. Their friendship grew through a shared calling and would carry them far beyond the sanctuary's walls.

Their bond mattered because it survived into a very different life. A friendship forged before an adventuring company existed became one of the human — and dragonborn — ties by which that company held together.

The moment of Fo'Gurn's death would later show how much Lavitz had come to depend upon the friend beside him.

## A member of the first company

Fo'Gurn belonged to the early adventurers who traveled together **before** their first royal commission. Their earliest recorded adventure was [[The Misfits#moonville-manor|Moonville Manor]]. Only afterward did Avalor's preceding Lord Marshal summon them to investigate an unnamed necromancer. The company included Lavitz, [[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]], [[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]], and [[Amara]].

They defeated their enemy, recovered fragments of magical study, and accepted further work. Their second commission concerned [[The Cult of Gol’Goroth|the cult of Gol'Goroth]] and brought [[Heureux]] into their circle.

The necromancer defeated in that first commission was **a different man from Jaaris**, whose later work would reach into Fo'Gurn's fate in a far more terrible way.

Fo'Gurn's place among the Misfits was established through these travels. Although the five heroes most celebrated after Cid's defeat were Lavitz, Tel'Lora, Sven, Heureux, and Amara, Fo'Gurn had been one of the original companions.

## The hydra and the decision to remain

During the **third month of the Misfits' 2000 AF campaign**, after their investigation at [[Stanleyville]], Cid's servants took the companions and other prisoners to a ruined temple. There a failed rite became a new danger when a **hydra** rose from the sacrificial mass and threatened those attempting to escape.

**Fo'Gurn remained behind** to hold the creature back while his friends fled. The decision gave the others time to survive the ruin. **He died confronting the hydra**, with no promise that anyone would ever be able to bring him home.

The sacrifice did not undo the other lives lost at the temple. For those who escaped, it meant continuing the journey without a friend whose place in the company had long been secure. The ordeal is also recorded in [[The Misfits#iv-the-hydra-and-the-missing-companion|the fellowship's account of the hydra]].

## The altar and the people he protected

The captives at the temple included **[[Anna, Lowkee's Keeper|Anna]]**, the noncombatant friend the Misfits had met while searching for Lowkee's Keeper. She was threatened at the altar while the prisoners' magic was suppressed.

Fo'Gurn's stand was therefore not a feat performed in isolation. Behind him were people with names and lives, including the woman who had joined the fellowship without any intention of becoming a warrior. Anna escaped the temple. **Kel'Maris, the preceding Lord Marshal, had died earlier at Stanleyville**; Captain Funkin had returned her body safely to Renada and **later died at this temple** alongside Inspector Sihnion and trapped guards.

The paladin's choice left the companions time to flee, but it also left a living obligation. The others continued a mission that Anna would accompany to the Shadowfell, carrying the **[[Emerald of Armanti]]** into the last confrontation with Cid.

## Grief on the road

For Lavitz, Fo'Gurn's death was more than the loss of an adventuring companion. It was the loss of a friend whose place in his life preceded the Misfits.

[[Heureux]], who had witnessed enough losses to understand the difference between song and grief, later reflected that heroic sacrifice is more comfortable to celebrate than it is to survive. The people spared by Fo'Gurn's decision had to live with it long after the danger had passed. Jaaris's later necromancy did not make Fo'Gurn's sacrifice less real.

His absence also shaped the fellowship's history. The five publicly celebrated Misfits went on to face threats in which Fo'Gurn could no longer stand beside them. Their growing renown did not restore his place among them.

To speak of the paladin only as a symbol of sacrifice would omit the grief his friends carried through the remainder of their campaign, and again after his final rest.

## Jaaris and the stolen return

Fo'Gurn **truly died at the Hydra**. The necromancer **[[Jaaris]]** subsequently raised his body through dark necromancy and **compelled him against his former companions**. The changed paladin who appeared at Cid's fortress was neither an impostor nor a man who had merely survived unnoticed: he was Fo'Gurn, forced into a borrowed existence governed by another's will.

When the Misfits reached the Shadowfell, Lavitz followed his oldest friend into a separate room and fought him. Lavitz came to understand that defeating Fo'Gurn in combat would not be the same as saving him. Rather than deliver a final blow, **Lavitz deliberately lowered his defenses** and appealed to the friend he had known since Heaven's Heart.

Fo'Gurn had an opening to strike. **He chose to defend Lavitz instead**, resisting Jaaris's conditioning through his own decision. **His freedom was reclaimed before Jaaris died.** Jaaris's later death brought the borrowed life sustaining Fo'Gurn toward its end; it did not create the paladin's redemption.

## The companion at the final confrontation

After Fo'Gurn resisted Jaaris's conditioning, **he and Lavitz returned to the throne room together**. The fuller account of their reunion with the company is preserved in **[[The Shadowfell Confrontation]]**.

Cid was preparing to strike down the badly wounded **[[Samuel the Devoted|Samuel]]**. Lavitz and Fo'Gurn intercepted the blow and fought alongside the company. The **[[Emerald of Armanti]]**, carried earlier by Anna, had enabled Samuel to channel Lowkee's spirit temporarily. Its purpose was to prevent Cid from returning to the Material Plane.

As the Blood King tried to escape, **Fo'Gurn pressed the emerald into Cid's chest**. Black hands reached from the darkness and dragged Cid into the Beyond. The hands' identity remains unknown, and their arrival is not proof that they were controlled by the gem.

Fo'Gurn's choice had brought him back to his friends for the end of the struggle, but **it could not preserve his borrowed existence**. Jaaris had been killed, and the necromantic bond sustaining Fo'Gurn was failing. He could not return to the Material Plane with the companions and **remained in the Shadowfell to rest one final time**. He and Lavitz were granted the goodbye that the Hydra's collapse had denied them.

Nearly twenty years later, **Fo'Gurn appeared again beside Samuel** at the next summons—a sight that would bring the old fellowship's history into a new age.

## An original Misfit, not an afterthought

The five names most commonly celebrated in Avalor's public histories belong to Lavitz, Tel'Lora, Sven, Heureux, and Amara. Their continued public activity after the victory made them familiar figures of the [[The Age of Ascendancy|Age of Ascendancy]].

Fo'Gurn's place is different, but no less real. He was a member of the early company and the dearest friend of one of its most visible heroes. He shared their first commissions and their dangers before their fellowship became a legend.

His later necromantic restoration and final rest did not erase that earlier membership. The question of his reappearance beside Samuel two decades later belongs to another chapter; the friend who stood beside Lavitz at Heaven's Heart and on the road remains at the center of this one.

Avalor's best account of the Misfits should have room for those whose deeds cannot be neatly reduced to the famous five.

## What his example leaves behind

Fo'Gurn's legacy rests upon a simple, difficult truth: the safety of others may depend on a protector who knows the cost of remaining behind.

At Heaven's Heart, such a choice echoes Lowkee's teaching of duty toward the vulnerable. In the Misfits' story, it also illustrates the price of courage to those who survive it.

The mysteries surrounding his fate have never mattered as much to his companions as the man himself. Before Fo'Gurn became the hero of a famous last stand, **he was someone his friends wanted to come home**.
