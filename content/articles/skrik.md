## A little kobold with a very large destiny

Among the unusual people who accompanied the five operatives sent into [[Blackwater Bay]], none had a smaller stature or a larger opinion of his own importance than **Skrik, the Tide Blessed**. A kobold of the [[clans-and-communities-of-avalor#the-tide-touched-kobolds|Tide-Touched]], Skrik wore the shell and coral ornaments of his people and spoke of legendary accomplishments with utter conviction. His confident declarations could bewilder, irritate, or amuse anyone who heard them.

The problem with dismissing Skrik was that, every so often, he actually did something extraordinary.

The truth of all his stories has never been established. Indeed, several sound less credible after repeated telling. Yet the events of **2020 AF** demonstrated that beneath the little kobold's tremendous capacity for exaggeration lay a courage, inventiveness, and unaccountable strangeness that demanded attention.

## A dreamer beneath the waters

Skrik grew up among the Tide-Touched kobolds, a community shaped by an ancient waterway and the forgotten chambers beside it. Their blue-green scales and adornments of shell and coral marked a people whose customs had long grown around the tide complex. An elder known as the **Tidecaller** preserved an understanding of the waterway through inherited tradition; the more confrontational **Grik** challenged the elder's interpretation when strangers later arrived.

Skrik, however, was more interested in imagining a life beyond those chambers.

He listened for tales of distant kingdoms, fearsome creatures, and wandering heroes. Where others considered the work of keeping a community alive, Skrik thought about the deeds that would someday make his name known throughout the world. He was a familiar storyteller whose ambitions exceeded any reputation he had yet earned. The tribe heard his declarations often enough to greet them with amusement or exasperation.

Beneath that confidence was a fear he seldom showed: **he dreaded living and dying without being remembered**. Skrik's longing for an audience was more than vanity. He wanted proof that his existence mattered beyond the tunnels in which he had been raised.

## The trouble with Skrik's stories

Skrik did not always remember events the same way other witnesses did. A stumble could become a tactical maneuver in his recollection; an unlikely escape might acquire the proportions of a tremendous victory. His listeners often suspected deliberate invention.

But the accounts present a complication. On occasion, details that other people dismissed as impossible were later borne out by observation or independent testimony. That did not establish that every magnificent boast was true, nor did it prove that some unusual force was altering the past. It simply left those who knew him with an uncomfortable question: **why did Skrik sometimes seem so certain of things no one else remembered?**

Skrik himself displayed little doubt. If other people recalled an event differently, he assumed they had simply failed to appreciate what had happened.

His greatest gift in ordinary company was a confidence so overwhelming that others sometimes hesitated before contradicting him. When conversation became a contest of nerve, Skrik could sound like the only person in the room who knew what he was doing.

## The strangers from the Tide Gate

In **2020 AF**, the five covert operatives assembled by [[Lavitz Shawe]] entered an ancient [[other-locations-of-avalor#the-tide-gate|Tide Gate]] beneath Renada. Blood magic interrupted their journey and forced them into a partly flooded chamber inhabited by Skrik's people.

The unexpected visitors provoked an internal dispute. The Tidecaller sought to interpret their arrival through the tribe's traditions, while Grik's opposition helped turn the encounter violent. The travelers fought the kobolds but ultimately helped resolve the underlying disturbance rather than treating the community as a people to be destroyed.

Skrik saw a very different kind of opportunity. The outsiders represented the greater world he had dreamed about.

To the Tide-Touched leaders, sending him with the travelers was also a convenient way to gain some relief from an enthusiastic dreamer whose boasts had become a constant feature of village life. Skrik understood their decision as the fulfillment of his destiny. Whatever disagreement surrounded the departure, he was not banished from a people he hated: he remained attached to the community he hoped someday to impress.

The expedition now had a sixth traveler. **Skrik joined it after the original five**, rather than becoming another of Lavitz's commissioned operatives.

## The smallest voice at the largest table

Skrik accompanied the company beyond the tide chambers, through a Black Diamond excavation and into the immense harbor city of Blackwater. The mission called repeatedly for disguise, improvisation, persuasive speech, and the ability to act as though a dangerous undertaking were perfectly ordinary.

Those circumstances suited him surprisingly well.

Skrik's strength was not a formal command of soldiers or a reputation earned in tournaments. It was his refusal to be intimidated by the authority of people much larger than himself. He could make a declaration so preposterously confident that a guard might pause long enough to accept it, or at least to wonder whether someone else had already approved it.

The crew gained another indispensable ally when it rescued the smuggler [[Corvin Voss]] from the Black Diamond outpost. Corvin later proposed stealing an entire ship because the artifact they sought was too large to remove from its hold. Skrik agreed with such casual enthusiasm that he made the absurd idea sound obvious. **The plan was Corvin's**, not Skrik's, but the kobold saw no reason why a suitably determined company should fail to steal a ship.

## The kobold who flew

The stolen vessel escaped from **Pier Seven**, fought off pursuit, and passed through the treacherous **Teeth** before its mysterious cargo transformed it into an airborne ship. Its crew faced hostile flying mounts, attacks by Gladios and the wyvern Shadowthorn, and an extraordinary struggle to break the artifact's hold on the vessel.

Skrik survived those dangers alongside the other travelers. In later testimony, witnesses credited him with feats that sounded almost as improbable as his stories, including an exceptional effort involving the ship's rigging and occasions when his unshakable confidence influenced armed guards.

His accounts of encounters with a sea dragon, a frost giant, and an orc chieftain remained unreliable. Skrik continued to recount them without embarrassment. Neither the absence of proof nor the experience of his listeners dissuaded him.

When the ship finally reached **[[Renada]]**, the crew's escape was a remarkable shared achievement. Skrik had witnessed a vessel fly, helped the people aboard it endure, and returned with companions who could attest that at least some of his claimed deeds had been real.

## An audience with the High Wizard

After the expedition, **[[Lavitz Shawe]]**, **[[tellora-ebbenflow|Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]]**, and **[[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]]** spoke with Skrik privately about his purported exploits. His recollections mixed astonishing claims with episodes that surviving crew members could substantiate. A simple question about what was true became more difficult than any of the three had expected.

Tel'Lora used a silver **resonance focus** to examine the unusual kobold. The instrument at first gave no exceptional response.

Then an apparently unattended candle extinguished and relit. Papers shifted without wind. The silver focus trembled and rose, while pale energy briefly showed beneath Skrik's scales.

The High Wizard established that an unusual magical resonance surrounded Skrik. **He could not determine its type or origin.** The test did not prove that the kobold understood its source, controlled it deliberately, or possessed any particular known magical classification.

Skrik behaved as though the examination confirmed what he had always claimed about himself.

For Tel'Lora, the mystery was only beginning.

## The little kobold of Renada

Skrik's journey did not end with his arrival at the capital. He remained in **Renada**, finding a home amid a city of elaborate buildings, crowded taverns, strangers with stories of their own, and people who had witnessed some portion of his unlikely adventure.

He held no established royal office or military commission. His position near the great figures of the kingdom was more informal—and, to some of those figures, perhaps more perplexing.

**Tel'Lora continued to study Skrik** because the initial resonance examination had revealed an anomaly the High Wizard could not readily explain. The ongoing inquiry involved further observation rather than a settled diagnosis. No approved account identifies the source of Skrik's magic or specifies an additional power gained through these studies.

Skrik, for his part, found the idea of personal attention from Avalor's High Wizard altogether appropriate. The fact that Tel'Lora considered him a mystery seemed to Skrik simply another sign that the rest of the world was finally catching up.

He still spoke of returning one day to his Tide-Touched community with stories worthy of the great heroes. His affection for the people who sent him away survived his eagerness to leave their chambers behind.

## The greatest story yet

Skrik has never stopped imagining the day when all of Avalor might know his name. Beneath the boastfulness remains the same fear that haunted him beneath the tides: that he could pass through the world and leave nothing anyone would remember.

Yet the Blackwater expedition furnished an answer he has not fully grasped. Skrik did not need to conquer a legendary creature or command an army to matter. He traveled with people who needed one another, faced danger when he might have fled, and helped them make their way home.

Whether the strange magical resonance around him will ever be understood remains a question for Tel'Lora and the years ahead.

**Skrik already has a story worth telling.** The little kobold who wished for a legend of his own has become part of someone else's survival—and may someday discover that this is where greatness begins.
