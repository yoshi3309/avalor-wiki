## A school born after the adventure

The battles of [[The Misfits]] altered the course of Avalor, but the lives their companions built afterward may prove just as enduring. In **[[Taveras]]**, the firbolg bard **[[Heureux]]** founded an academy where others could learn the art to which he had devoted his life.

He had crossed dangerous roads, stood beside friends who faced death, and helped uncover the truth of a kingdom's founding. He had also played for crowds who knew nothing of those struggles, collected the stories of strangers, and learned that a well-told tale could survive long after its teller moved on.

The academy grew from that second calling. The world did not need another generation to endure the Misfits' dangers in order to learn how to sing, perform, write, or remember. It needed somewhere those gifts could be practiced before they were offered to an audience.

Known in the Archives as **Heureux's Bard Academy**, the school was established **after the defeat of [[Cid the Blood King]]**. Its descriptive name honors its founder; no different formal chartered title has yet been established.

## The city around its doors

Taveras was a fitting home for such a school. Set at a bend in the **River Rhys** beneath the foothills, the city had become a gathering place for musicians, actors, and storytellers. Its **Great Opera House** offered one vision of artistic success, but its streets, inns, and smaller stages held many others.

A new student arriving in Taveras would find no shortage of examples of what a life in the arts might look like. Some performers worked toward the grandest stages. Others carried their craft along the roads and rivers, or found their audiences in places unlikely ever to appear on a theater bill.

That variety matters to the academy's identity. Bardic talent does not belong exclusively to the famous, nor must every gifted student aspire to a royal audience. There is room in the art for a voice that commands a hall and one that can hold the attention of a handful of listeners around a fire.

## What Heureux brought home

Heureux was not merely an accomplished performer who happened to know celebrated heroes. He had been one of them.

He joined the Misfits after their earliest adventures and remained with them through strange encounters, bitter losses, and the confrontation that brought Cid's reign to an end. Their journeys included the liberation of the [[The Rotten Husk Clan|Rotten Husk]] and the death, apparent at the time, of their companion **[[fo-gurn|Fo'Gurn]]**. Heureux knew firsthand how differently a deed could appear to the people who had lived it and to those who later heard it sung.

He also witnessed a more unsettling lesson. For centuries the kingdom had honored **[[Aldus Greystone III]]** while condemning **[[Jaznaar]]** as a betrayer. The Misfits discovered that the celebrated healer and the Blood King were one man, while the supposed betrayer had struggled to contain his evil. An attractive, familiar history had proven profoundly false.

Heureux became one of the witnesses who helped carry the corrected account beyond the proclamations of the crown. His published history preserved what he remembered of his companions: not only their victories, but their grief, disagreements, absurdities, and friendships.

That experience gives the academy its significance. A bard's work can be joyful, comic, or wholly invented; it need not always instruct. But when a performer claims to tell the truth of the past, the choice of what to repeat—and what to leave unsaid—can have consequences long after applause has faded.

## Learning an art, finding a purpose

The academy is devoted to **bardic education**, and its founder's life offers more than one model for those drawn to that calling.

There is the pleasure of performance: the confidence to stand before listeners, the patience to improve an imperfect piece, and the ability to recognize when an audience has truly heard what was offered. There is also the work of storytelling: learning how words, music, and gesture can carry an idea farther than a ledger or decree.

For some, that calling may lead to the stage. For others it may become a life of travel, gathering tales and giving them back to the communities from which they came. Still others may study simply because art enriches a life already devoted to another duty.

These are the possibilities suggested by the school's purpose, rather than a fixed list of prescribed courses. The academy's exact curriculum, teaching appointments, and rules of admission have not been settled. Its identity rests on the opportunity it provides: **to develop a voice before deciding what to do with it**.

## A Golden Hand among the students

One of the academy's known students is **[[Myxi Twinefoot]]**, the halfling who serves as **Golden Hand** on **[[The Aegis]]**, the royal council.

Myxi's duties concern the kingdom's finances, not the organization of a troupe or the management of a theater. Yet she has chosen to study the bardic arts while continuing her service to the crown.

Her presence illustrates something important about the school. Not every student arrives in search of an artistic profession. Some come with an established place in the world and a desire to learn something that place has never asked of them.

There is no need to turn Myxi into a celebrated musician before her studies have earned such a description. The remarkable thing is simpler: a member of the kingdom's highest council is willing to sit among students and learn.

## School and society

Taveras is also home to **[[The Bard Guild|the Society of Silk & Story]]**, an influential fellowship of musicians, playwrights, performers, and keepers of oral traditions.

The distinction between the two institutions is straightforward. **The Society is a professional fellowship. Heureux's academy is a school.** One brings together people who practice the arts in the world; the other gives people an opportunity to study and develop their abilities.

Their interests naturally overlap, and a bard's life might bring that person into contact with both. But that shared vocation does not mean the Society governs the academy, that graduation grants guild membership, or that Heureux founded or commands the Society.

Nor is the academy simply a branch of the Royal Commission of Historical Inquiry. Heureux's experiences give its founding a connection to the importance of truthful memory, but it remains a place for the **bardic arts**, not a second royal archive.

## The founder who would not stay still

Founding the academy did not end Heureux's travels. He continued to perform, gather stories, and carry news of the Misfits into places where their deeds might otherwise remain only rumor.

That is perhaps a fitting arrangement for a school established by a wanderer. Its founder did not have to remain behind a desk forever to leave something lasting. The academy could offer students a place to begin, even as Heureux continued to practice the craft that had led him there.

He was descended from **[[Kelsalor]]**, the druid of [[The Six]] who sought to protect the living world's balance. The descendant's calling was different, but both understood that something precious could be lost through neglect.

An academy cannot promise that every song will endure or every story will be remembered accurately. It can, however, offer the next generation a chance to learn why making art matters—and to decide what they wish to leave behind.
