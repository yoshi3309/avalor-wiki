## A community under threat

The **Rotten Husk** were an orc tribe whose chieftain had become dangerous to the people he ruled. The Misfits' encounter with them is remembered for a choice that too many war stories overlook: the companions distinguished the conduct of the leader from the character of the whole tribe.

The Rotten Husk were not simply an enemy force to defeat. They were people whose own safety had been put in jeopardy.

## The intervention

[[The Misfits]] **helped free the tribe from its chieftain**. The surviving versions of the confrontation differ in their particulars, so the Royal Commission does not present a definitive sequence of blows, speeches, or negotiations.

The outcome is clearer. The companions departed with the tribe's **goodwill**, not with a claim of ownership over the people they had aided.

No reliable public source gives the chieftain's name, a new tribal constitution, or the exact geography of the Rotten Husk's homeland. Neither the tribe's history nor its future should be invented merely to complete a map.

## The importance of the distinction

Avalor's own past demonstrates the danger of assigning guilt to an ancestry or community. The false condemnation of [[Jaznaar]] contributed to generations of injustice toward drow families. This is not evidence that the Misfits made that precise historical comparison while helping the Rotten Husk. It does, however, help explain why historians consider the episode significant.

A leader's cruelty did not make all of the people beneath him cruel. The Rotten Husk were worth defending on their own terms.

For a tribe whose experiences might otherwise appear only in the biography of a famous adventurer, that difference is the heart of the account.
