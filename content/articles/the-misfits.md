## The fellowship that no prophecy assembled

The adventurers who became **the Misfits** did not meet because of a prophecy or a royal command to correct the kingdom's founding history. Their earliest known adventure carried them into Moonville Manor, a house that refused to behave like a house. Only afterward did Avalor's preceding Lord Marshal summon them to investigate a necromancer.

In later years their names would be celebrated beside those of ancient heroes. Their victory over [[Cid the Blood King]] exposed the truth of the Black Tower catastrophe, helped restore the name of [[Jaznaar]], and contributed to the period remembered as the [[The Age of Ascendancy|Age of Ascendancy]].

But the fellowship formed before anyone understood that purpose. Its members became companions by surviving dangers, arguing over decisions, caring for one another, and choosing again and again to continue the road together.

The ancient [[The Six|Six]] had been united during Avalor's founding and ultimately broken by a terrible division among friends. The Misfits became another kind of example: a fellowship whose members survived great danger and later allowed one another to lead different lives without surrendering their loyalty.

## Meet the companions

The five most commonly celebrated Misfits are those who continued to shape Avalor's public life after the victory. The earliest company, however, included **Fo'Gurn**, and its travels also gave a place to **Anna**, a noncombatant companion called Lowkee's Keeper, and **Jack**, a child whose conscious ghost befriended them. The five whose later careers became widely celebrated were not the whole fellowship.

### Lavitz Shawe — the paladin

[[Lavitz Shawe]] came from the [[Mudsteps]] of [[Renada]] and trained as a paladin at [[Heaven's Heart]]. His courage carried him through the company's defining dangers; his later responsibilities brought him into the royal council as **Lord Marshal** and commander of [[Lion's Bane]].

His story follows a movement from the **Oath of Vengeance** toward the **Oath of Devotion**—a change from pursuing a particular enemy to accepting the continuing burden of protecting a kingdom.

### Tel'Lora Ebbenflow — the wizard

[[Tel'Lora Ebbenflow]], a male half-elf wizard, helped investigate extraordinary magic and recover evidence of Avalor's forgotten history. His meeting with Jaznaar at [[Jaznaar’s Hidden Refuge|the hidden refuge]] became particularly significant.

After the victory, he became High Wizard and the first holder of an independent eighth seat on [[The Aegis]]. He also revived [[Shamman’s Tower]] as an academy for disciplined magical inquiry.

### S’vendyal Shadowwhisperer — the Iron Shadow

[[svendyal-shadowwhisper|Sven]], a grayscale eladrin, was among the earliest companions. In the years after Cid's defeat, his reputation acquired the epithet **Iron Shadow** as his activities became difficult for public scholars to trace.

He remained close to Lavitz and Tel'Lora. Rumors of covert undertakings should not be confused with verified accounts of the people or methods involved.

### Heureux — the storyteller

[[Heureux]], a firbolg bard descended from [[Kelsalor]] of the Six, joined during the company's second commission. His wit and published recollections helped preserve not only heroic victories, but also the embarrassing, frightening, and sorrowful moments that gave their friendship its character.

He later founded [[Heureux’s Bard Academy]] in Taveras while continuing his travels and work as a storyteller.

### Amara — the druid

[[Amara]], a druid descended from [[Petris]] of the Six, belonged to the first company. Her later path carried her increasingly far from the capital, in service of the natural world.

She is remembered in particular for an owl associated with the ordeals of [[Zauks and the False Lives|Zauks]], when the companions had to recognize and reject convincing false lives.

### Fo’Gurn — the friend who stayed

[[fo-gurn|Fo'Gurn]], a dragonborn paladin and lifelong friend of Lavitz, was an **original member** of the company. He remained behind before a hydra so his friends could escape and was believed dead for years.

The published history and authorized testimony contain later accounts of his unexpected participation in the final struggle with Cid. The circumstances of that return remain contested in the public record.

### Jack — the boy who traveled onward

[[Jack of Stanleyville]] was a child murdered through a spell concealed in a manuscript sold by Cid in disguise. His conscious spirit befriended the Misfits during their investigation of [[Stanleyville]].

Jack did not remain behind in the town. He went with the company, a traveling companion whose life and death mattered for reasons beyond the evidence he helped uncover.

### Anna — the Keeper who traveled with them

The companions met **[[Anna, Lowkee's Keeper|Anna]]** during a search for knowledge concerning the ancient paladin [[Lowkee]]. A waitress when they first encountered her, Anna traveled with the Misfits as a **noncombatant**, carried the [[Emerald of Armanti]], and found a place among friends whose lives were increasingly dangerous. Her name belongs alongside those who fought, not below the story as an object to be escorted.

### Jack and the companions who were not warriors

[[Jack of Stanleyville]] was a murdered child whose spirit chose to remain with the company after his death. Neither Anna nor Jack was an original player-character warrior of the Misfits, yet both shaped what it meant for the others to be a fellowship. Their stories deserve attention beyond the dangers that brought them onto the road.

## Moonville Manor

The earliest surviving account of the fellowship's adventures brings them to **Moonville Manor**, a **living house**. Its uncanny halls became one more danger the companions shared, and one more adventure that would return to haunt them when Zauks began turning their memories against them.

The mind flayer [[Zauks and the False Lives|Zauks]] later recreated Moonville Manor as one of several memory-based trials. That imitation was **not a second visit to the original house**; it was an old adventure turned against those who had survived it.

The Manor stands **before the first royal commission** in the reconciled history. Its rooms belong to the beginnings of the companions' trust, not to a later assignment from the crown. The exact order of every episode within the living house has not survived, and the Archives decline to supply one.

## I. The Lord Marshal's first commission

After Moonville Manor, the preceding Lord Marshal called upon the companions to investigate an **unnamed necromancer**. They defeated the practitioner and recovered pieces of arcane research later understood to have connections with the dangerous work of Aldus Greystone III.

This first commission was not their first adventure, nor was the enemy the later necromancer [[Jaaris]]. The recovered writings do not establish that Cid had directed the practitioner or arranged the companions' meeting. The full inquiry is preserved in [[The Misfits’ First Commission|the record of the first commission]].

The Lord Marshal called upon them again to investigate the [[Independent Factions of the Misfits Era#the-cult-of-gol-goroth|cult of Gol'Goroth]]. During that second commission, Heureux joined the company. Their subsequent encounter with [[Noki]] was remembered for the mage's transformation into a sheep. He survived the affair and would later be known as **Noke**, although the Misfits believed his story had ended. His later disfigurement must not be projected onto the mage they fought. Not every strange event was an instrument of the Blood King.

## II. Anna and the search for Lowkee's Keeper

Not every road the Misfits traveled was part of a scheme laid by Cid. A living manor, a cult, and the affair of a mage transformed into a sheep belonged to their earlier adventures. Another search, undertaken in hope of understanding the ancient paladin **[[Lowkee]]**, brought someone into their company whose name was too long treated as a footnote.

They met **[[Anna, Lowkee's Keeper|Anna]]** at a tavern. Sven's awkward stumble into the waitress made the first encounter memorable for embarrassment rather than prophecy. In the days that followed, she became more than the person they had met while following clues: **she traveled with them as a noncombatant friend**, needing their protection and offering her own form of steadfast companionship.

The name **Lowkee's Keeper** attached itself to Anna and to the search. She bore the **[[Emerald of Armanti]]**, an object connected in testimony with the hope of calling upon Lowkee's help against the Blood King. That was a hope the companions carried; it is not proof of a permanent resurrection of the founding paladin, nor a complete account of the gem's capabilities.

Protecting Anna was never merely an errand to deliver a magical object. When the company was captured near Stanleyville, **Anna was among those endangered at the ruined temple**, and she survived alongside the preceding Lord Marshal. Later, after the Games, she endured a second captivity on **[[Ros Furband|Ros Furband's]]** pirate galleon, working in the galley beside Tel'Lora while the queen they were trying to rescue remained in Cid's hands.

Anna continued with the fellowship to the end of its campaign. The emerald reappeared in the final confrontation, allowing Samuel to channel Lowkee briefly and later serving as Fo'Gurn's seal against Cid's return to the Material Plane. After the royal audience **Anna revealed herself as Erevan Ilesere**, who had maintained the guise as a divine test of Sven. The full mechanics and origins of the gem remain unsettled, but **Anna's identity and the relic's known uses are part of the completed history**. The friendship the Misfits showed Anna was real even before they understood whom they were protecting.

## III. The hydra and the missing companion

At a temple near [[Stanleyville]], the Misfits confronted a more direct manifestation of Cid's cruelty. Captives had been restrained against their use of magic, and the site was connected to a rite seeking to turn divine power toward the Blood King's purpose. The evidence does not give scholars license to reconstruct every detail of that rite.

Cid's servants there included [[Jaaris]], Karina, Black Agnes Mudwallow, and the mind flayer Zauks. **Anna, whose companionship had begun during the search for Lowkee's Keeper, was endangered at the altar**. Captain Funkin, Inspector Sihnion, and trapped guards died in the catastrophe. The preceding Lord Marshal and Anna survived this episode.

A hydra threatened the companions' escape. [[fo-gurn|Fo'Gurn]] remained behind so the others could live. Their memories of his choice, and the years in which they mourned him, would remain among the fellowship's deepest losses. For Lavitz, it was the apparent end of a friendship that predated the Misfits themselves.

Later accounts connected Fo'Gurn's fate with the necromancer Jaaris, but that knowledge did not undo the choice at the temple or the grief carried afterward. The public history distinguishes what the companions witnessed from explanations of events they could not yet understand.

## IV. Stanleyville and the Stalker

In [[Stanleyville]], the Misfits investigated mysterious deaths. Among the victims was Jack, whose spirit remained in the mortal world seeking an explanation for his death.

The town was stalked by a murderous creature later attributed to Cid's control. [[The Stalker of Stanleyville|The Stalker]] killed the Lord Marshal who had first commissioned the party, leaving the office vacant for the remainder of the campaign.

The Misfits departed with Jack in their company and a deepening awareness that the events they had witnessed could be part of something older and more dangerous than any one town.

The Lord Marshal's death here must not be confused with the losses at the earlier temple: he had **survived the hydra affair** and continued to serve the crown until the Stalker killed him.

## The Liberation of the Rotten Husk

Following the tragedy at Stanleyville, the Misfits encountered **the Rotten Husk**, a [[The Rotten Husk Clan|clan of orcs]] whose own chieftain had become a danger to his people. The adventurers recognized that the leader's wrongdoing did not make the entire tribe culpable.

The Misfits helped **free the Rotten Husk from his rule** and departed with the people's **goodwill**. For a clan who had suffered under a dangerous leader, what mattered was not the precise order of blows or speeches, but that their future was theirs again.

The event mattered independently of the later war against Cid. For the Rotten Husk, it was not a footnote in another group's legend but a turning point in their own lives. Their continuing identity is recorded separately in [[The Rotten Husk Clan]], while the encounter belongs here among the fellowship's journeys.

## V. Jaznaar's testimony

The adventurers' inquiries eventually led to [[Jaznaar#the-refuge-beyond-ordinary-knowledge|Jaznaar's hidden refuge]], reached through a passage associated with the tomb of [[Bradgem Glav]] at [[Heroes Hill]].

**Jaznaar Mizzrorzza**, the last living member of the founding Six, revealed that **Aldus Greystone III and Cid the Blood King were the same being**. For almost two thousand years Jaznaar had struggled to contain the consequences of Aldus's forbidden experiments while Avalor falsely condemned him as the Betrayer.

He instructed Tel'Lora and entrusted knowledge to the company. As they prepared to face Cid, he also gave Lavitz **Cid's Bane**, the sword once carried by his founding companion [[Lowkee]]. His death after their visit left the Misfits with both the truth and the responsibility of acting upon it.

## VI. The Tournament and the broken pursuit

At [[The Tournament of Champions]] in [[Renada]], the Misfits entered under assumed names while [[King Gradvum Frozenroar]] and [[Queen Melmiri]] attended the public celebration. [[Cid the Blood King]] seized Melmiri and fled. The queen's captivity began **before** the later encounters with Ros Furband, Jinlin Vechon, and Zauks, and continued throughout them.

**Amara was taken after the Games**, during the dangers that followed the public celebration. The surviving account does not establish the exact circumstances of her capture or return; what is certain is that she later intervened when Zauks imprisoned the companions.

The rescue did not follow an uninterrupted passage from the tournament grounds to Cid's throne. A pirate captain named **[[Ros Furband]]**, operating under a paid arrangement, captured the companions and delivered them into the hands of another of the era's dangerous figures. The Commission has not established Ros's point of embarkation or every condition attached to his bargain.

## Jinlin Vechon and the Wrath of Innocent

Ros's captives came before **Jinlin Vechon**, a **dragonborn vampire** and founder of [[Independent Factions of the Misfits Era#the-wrath-of-innocent|the Wrath of Innocent]]. Jinlin hated Cid, yet the fellowship found that shared hatred was no guarantee of shared justice. **Aldus had experimented on Jinlin during the Blood Sickness era**, cursing the dragonborn with unwanted vampirism and a seemingly unattainable death. Jinlin had a genuine grievance, but it did not authorize the harm he inflicted on others.

The Misfits **fought Jinlin in the waking world and defeated him**. His defeat is an event of their actual campaign, not a scene invented by the mind flayer who later attacked them. Melmiri remained in Cid's custody while these events unfolded.

Accounts of the voyage preserve the humble work assigned to the captives: Lavitz and Heureux labored at the oars, while Tel'Lora and [[Anna, Lowkee's Keeper|Anna]] worked in the galley. **Sven and Admiral [[Beldir Willowrun]]** were also placed among the lookouts; Beldir's precise reason for being aboard remains uncertain. Such details are useful counterweights to ballads that remember only crowns, monsters, and weapons.

The broader history of Jinlin's faction is preserved separately. Later imitations of older adventures should not be mistaken for a resurrection of Jinlin or a second assault upon his stronghold.

## Zauks and the False Lives

**After the real confrontation with Jinlin**, the mind flayer [[Zauks and the False Lives|Zauks]] trapped the Misfits and turned their memories into a succession of counterfeit adventures. He reproduced dangers already endured: Moonville Manor, the first necromancer, the Stalker, Gol'Goroth, Noki, the hydra, Jack's family home, and an encounter involving Jaaris and flesh golems.

The order in which Zauks presented these horrors was **the order of a torment**, not a second chronology of the fellowship's lives. At Jack's house, for instance, the companions were offered another chance to prevent a child's death; that painful imitation did not change what had happened in Stanleyville.

The mind flayer's cruelest deception was to offer convincing **false lives** in which grief and separation had been repaired. **Amara was outside the captives' illusions**. She came into the lair in the shape of an owl, returned to her own form, and **killed Zauks**, freeing her companions. Another owl is remembered in accounts of her family; the two episodes are not the same event.

When the Misfits emerged, the queen was still captive. They had escaped an invented happiness, not completed the rescue that had sent them on the road.

## VII. The Shadowfell confrontation

What eventually became known of the final confrontation changed its historical meaning. **Anna was Erevan Ilesere**, who had maintained the guise during the travels as a test of Sven's compassion and commitment to justice. The **Emerald of Armanti** was intended to keep Cid from returning to the Material Plane and enabled Samuel briefly to channel Lowkee's spirit. **Jaaris had resurrected Fo'Gurn through necromancy** after his true death at the Hydra. Fo'Gurn broke that imposed control to protect Lavitz before Jaaris was killed, and afterward remained in the Shadowfell to rest once more. The black hands that removed Cid into the Beyond still have no established identity.


The road from the tournament, through Ros's ship, Jinlin's defeat, and Zauks's illusions, ultimately led into the **Shadowfell**, where Cid held Queen Melmiri. The complete public account of this conclusion is preserved as **[[The Shadowfell Confrontation]]**, a historical episode in its own right rather than merely a chapter in the Blood King's biography. The Misfits found the Blood King amid deadly servants and creatures whose presence recalled the wrongs committed by Jaaris. The necromancer was defeated in the final struggle.

Authorized accounts describe **Fo'Gurn's unexpected presence** and a confrontation with Lavitz. They preserve the moral outline of a friend who refused to answer danger with abandonment, and of Fo'Gurn **choosing again to protect Lavitz**. The precise circumstances of his appearance and the words exchanged between the paladins remain outside the securely established public narrative.

As Cid sought to escape, testimony places the **[[Emerald of Armanti]]**, the jewel associated with **Anna and Lowkee's Keeper**, in Fo'Gurn's hands. The final testimony also places **[[Samuel the Devoted|Samuel]]** in the struggle, though the precise nature of the brief intervention associated with Lowkee is not fully established for public readers. A vast black hand, or hands in some accounts, reached from an opening into darkness and took the Blood King away. No witness could name the force responsible; later scholars must not assign it a divine or other identity merely to supply an ending.

**Melmiri helped her rescuers escape** the failing fortress. Her part in the escape was an act of agency, not simply the passive recovery of a captive queen. The details of the passage remain incompletely recorded. All the returning companions survived the final battle, and the kingdom received its queen again.

## VIII. The kingdom after the victory

After Cid's defeat, the Misfits' testimony helped expose the old accusation against Jaznaar as a lie. [[The Vindication of Jaznaar|Gradvum's royal proclamation]] restored his name, while historians and citizens began reexamining how the false account had endured for centuries.

The kingdom gained extraordinary renown, but fame did not resolve its daily obligations. During the ensuing years, Lavitz became Lord Marshal, Tel'Lora became High Wizard, Heureux founded his academy and published his history, Sven grew more elusive, and Amara continued traveling in service of nature.

The group remained together briefly to help Avalor recover. Over the following twenty years the five celebrated companions did **not** reassemble as one complete party.

That separation did not mean estrangement. Lavitz, Tel'Lora, and Sven maintained especially close ties, while Heureux and Amara followed other callings. They had survived their shared adventure without requiring their friendship to remain frozen in its original form.

## More than five names in a song

The most familiar account of the Misfits records the five companions whose later careers remained visible during Avalor's Age of Ascendancy. That account is not false, but it is incomplete if read as the entire history of the fellowship.

Fo'Gurn was an original Misfit; Anna and Jack were companions they chose to bring into their lives. **All three belong in the story**. Their presence reminds readers that belonging cannot be measured only by whether one's name remains on a royal roster.

The history of the Misfits is ultimately the history of a friendship that endured loss, exposed a kingdom's oldest lie, and allowed its members the freedom to become more than the battles they had fought.
