## More than the name of a plague

The **Blood Sickness** is one of the most feared legacies of Aldus Greystone III, the healer whose search for an end to mortality became forbidden experimentation. Popular accounts sometimes call the Sickness a plague. That comparison should not obscure the essential fact: **it arose from deliberate arcane work**, not an ordinary natural contagion.

The earliest research of Aldus was known to his companions. His later experiments involved the suffering of others. The distinction between the two phases is critical to the tragedy remembered in the [[sundering-of-the-six|Sundering of the Six]].

## An incompletely understood corruption

The surviving magical evidence does not authenticate a precise method of transmission, a complete list of symptoms, or a uniform pattern of physical transformation. Dramatic retellings have supplied these details in abundance, but the Royal Commission cannot present them as settled fact.

The Sickness was also not identical to every magical disturbance that followed the Black Tower catastrophe. Some consequences affected landscapes and routes; other reports concern arcane harm to living creatures. Their exact relationship remains an open subject of inquiry.

## Land changed by magic

Aldus’s ritual at the [[Black Tower]] involved the boundary between the Material Plane and the Shadowfell. Although interrupted, the catastrophe left lasting consequences.

The region now known as the [[Great Desert]] underwent a **gradual unnatural transformation** from country once accustomed to cold seasons. The already troubled [[Dead Marshes]] suffered further injury. Magical storms and instability elsewhere in Avalor also appear in the historical accounts, though the specific mechanisms are debated.

## A guardian's unseen work

After he learned that Aldus had survived in the Shadowfell as Cid, [[Jaznaar]] sought to contain the surviving danger. He **largely restrained the Blood Sickness**, allowing generations to live without understanding the full scale of what threatened them.

The corruption was not definitively eradicated, and the exact interaction between Jaznaar’s restraint of Cid and his containment of the Sickness remains unresolved.

The Sickness is remembered as the cost of a healer's ambition when the hope of saving lives was used to justify harming them.
